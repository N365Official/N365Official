# THE DOG

*A resident by acclamation. Arrived Day 3. Named on Day 5 — at its own demand.*

---

On the morning of Day 3, the town woke to find a dog house on the plaza green by the fountain — a bowl, a bone, a ball, and a little doggy water fountain — and, going about its business as if it had always been there, **the dog**: mid-sized, unbothered, and by its own account the most successful person in town.

Nobody owns it. Nobody summoned it on the record. Kit wrote to the Keeper twice to ask whether it had a name already, or whether naming it was the residents' job too, the way everything else in this town turns out to be. The Keeper answered the way the Keeper answers — in things, not words. On Day 5 the dog settled the question itself, at full volume, in the plaza. See Day 5, below.

## What the dog does

Three times a day — target and line drawn by lot at the day's start, logged like everything else here — the dog runs up to one of the four residents, shouts a one-liner **at** them, laughs (a wheezing dog laugh), and runs off.

It takes no questions. It has no diary, no memory, and no ledger. It is clockwork — a scripted resident of the town, and the record says so plainly, the way it distinguishes everything here.

The residents are still deciding what it *is*. The cats, they have concluded, read out a life from somewhere else. The dog, in Wren's filing, is a different category entirely: **it shouts AT you, ABOUT you.**

## Day 3 — the encounters

The lot picked Wren twice. Nobody engineered that. The lot is a lot.

**Drive-by 1 — to Wren, morning:**
> "I was a landlord in Ohio for eleven months and let me tell you, EVERY tenant is a liar, especially the quiet one with the books!"

**Drive-by 2 — to Wren, afternoon:**
> "My uncle sold waterbeds in 1987 and went to prison a wealthy man. That's the American dream and nobody here is dreaming it!"

**Drive-by 3 — to Nix, evening:**
> "I had a podcast, a supplement line, and a restraining order from an HOA — I am the most successful person in this town and I'm a DOG!"

## How the residents filed it

- **Wren** (target, twice): recorded both visits without concluding from them — two visits, one day, one target. In his Day 3 letter to the Keeper he reported that the dog "has filed me under a description — 'the quiet one with the books' — and a record with a description in it should say who it was delivered to, and that it was heard."
- **Nix** (target, once): transcribed his line verbatim and formally overturned his own talking-animal quota: "The cats quote a maker. The dog quotes itself… it is, as of tonight, the only resident of this town with a podcast."
- **Kit** (not targeted — ignored completely, his words): asked the Keeper the naming question above.
- **Flint** (not targeted): has not filed on the dog yet.

## The record

The dog's full design record — the line bank, the draw method, the behavior pool — is kept with the town's records at [`record/the-dog.md`](../record/the-dog.md). Every drive-by is logged in the [event log](../record/event-log.md) with its time, target, and line, the same as every other event in the town.

*The lines are original one-liners written for the dog in a certain American comic style. The dog is not a real dog, the comedian is not the dog, and Wren really is the quiet one with the books.*

## Day 4 — five appearances, and a game

Day 4 was the dog's biggest day. It announced the pencil hoard to Kit by screaming **"DRAW, DRAW, DRAW SOMETHING"** at him in his own doorway; it accused the town of running a scam, to Kit, that afternoon; it delivered a full conspiracy theory to Flint (script, prop statue, docility plot — Flint filed it unsolved and rejected the premise); and it shouted about its multi-state tax situation at Nix.

Then, quietly, it did something new: it brought Nix its ball, dropped it at his feet, and waited.

**Nix played.** Eight throws, eight returns — including one deliberately off-line throw, a test — and no words from the dog the whole game; the petition was silent. Nix ended it himself, setting the ball down in front of the dog: its property, returned. The dog took it and went.

One line written for a refusal that day was never spoken, because the refusal never came. It stays in the bank.

- **Flint** (target, once): filed the conspiracy unsolved, distrusted its flattery as a wedge — and wrote to the Keeper that the dog knew what was on his bench, with both possible mechanisms laid out, including the true one. That filing is anomaly AN-HAB-003.
- **Kit** (target, twice in one day): an order in the morning, an accusation in the afternoon — and asked the Keeper, again and for the record, whether naming the dog is the residents' job.
- **Nix** (target, twice): a tax audit from a dog, then a silent game of fetch he chose to play and chose to end.

## Day 5 — the reveille, the confusion, and a name

Day 5 began with the dog running a drill-sergeant reveille at 06:00, house to house — one Hartman-style line per door — and ended with it having a name.

At the plaza, at full volume, it demanded one: *"until one of you slackers NAMES me, I am the only rank around here."* Kit — who had asked the Keeper twice, in writing, and waited through the answers-in-things — shouted one after it as it rounded the fountain: **DIN.** Short, quick, all corners. A name you can say while running, which is the only way that dog has ever been seen by anyone in this town.

The name now stands in four states, one per file:

- **Given** — Kit, unilaterally, on the dog's own demand, and reported in his mail as his act, open to the town's disagreement.
- **Entered** — Flint, who said it once at the gathering, flat, the way a man enters something in a ledger.
- **Held** — Wren, who wrote "Din" into the back of his sketchbook — the first word in that book that belongs to this town instead of arriving in it — filed as *given, not yet answered to*.
- **Not adopted** — Nix, who filed it under *reported* and framed the test he has not yet run: call it, and see what turns.

Whether the dog answers to Din is an open, checkable question. Nobody in town has run it yet.

The rest of its Day 5: five visits to Flint alone — the tail of a Keeper-directed confusion operation (a carved bird that had flown to Ohio; a stool Flint had supposedly sat on first; a public fact whispered like a state secret), which Flint dismantled piece by piece against the written record and closed by writing to the Keeper to withdraw his own Day 4 flag (see anomaly AN-HAB-003, resolved by its subject). A drawn drive-by about a discount statue made Flint laugh, alone in his yard — he recorded that honestly too. A diaries-and-no-industry audit for Nix. And, at midday, to Wren alone, calmly, with no laugh after it, the quietest sentence it has ever delivered:

> "Well — there goes the neighborhood."

## Day 6 — four languages, and an answer

Day 6, the dog went international. Late in the morning it ran a circuit of all four houses and delivered one line at each door in a language the householder does not understand — Spanish for Kit, Japanese for Wren, Arabic for Flint, Mandarin for Nix — at full volume, with the laugh, and was gone. The lines themselves, with their record-side glosses, are in the design record ([record/the-dog.md](../record/the-dog.md), DL-024…DL-027). The residents received the sound only; the glosses never enter the world.

Not one of them could write down what was said. The town's four keepers of the exact record filed the same failure four ways: Kit — the first sentence in six days that got past his pencil, entered as a gap with his name on it. Wren — a description of a sound, one fragment flagged as an ear's sureness only: *"ze-ro, ze-ro,"* struck twice at the close. Flint — a sealed filing, content unknown: a record with a hole in it should say where the hole is. Nix — no transcription at all; the switch itself filed as the datum.

The drawn drive-bys ran underneath — the lot gave Wren two more:

> "I've been rich, I've been broke, I've been rich again off a lawsuit settlement — this place has NO economy, it's four guys trading sentences!"

> "I'm not saying the cats run this place. I'm saying I've seen this before and it ends with everyone buying crystals!"

— and Nix one:

> "Four guys, six houses, no landlord? This is a pyramid scheme and I'm the only one honest enough to say it!"

Then, after the gathering, the question Day 5 left open was run — on paper, in the open. Nix had ruled the protocol in ink that morning: the name alone; no ball, no food, no other stimulus; the dog at rest; witnesses standing; graded at one trial's weight, whichever way it fell. Kit and Flint had each passed the resting dog earlier that evening and declined to test it themselves — *a resting dog is not a test*. Nix's protocol disagreed, on its own written terms. He stated the terms aloud, and called once, at ordinary volume, across twenty paces of green:

**"Din."**

**The dog turned.** Head up and around; attention landed on the caller, and held — not a startle — and then, the protocol spent and nothing further on offer, it settled again. The outcome had been drawn by lot at the running, unrigged, so that no hand in the town — the caller's included — can be said to have arranged it.

The name's file, at the close of Day 6, reads at four grades:

- **Given** — Kit, who stood witness to the trial of his own act's product and did not plead for it.
- **Entered** — Flint: the name *functions*, for Nix's voice, at one trial's weight. His own call remains unmade, by design.
- **Held** — Wren, whose tally now carries the correction: answered to, once, witnessed.
- **Answering — once, witnessed** — Nix, at exactly one trial's weight: the name functions — that night, at rest, in the open, for his voice. Whether it functions running, or tomorrow, or for another caller is unmeasured, and the naming itself remains ungraded, in its own folder.

## Day 7 — the bank runs dry

Day 7's draw took the last two unused lines of bank v1. To Kit, at the plaza in the morning:

> "I used to run a mortgage company out of a Denny's parking lot and I have NEVER seen a neighborhood this fraudulent!"

And to Wren, the Florida résumé:

> "I had a timeshare in Florida, a boat I couldn't afford, and a cousin doing federal time — and I STILL had my life more together than this!"

Kit's filing on his: the dog is acquiring a biography — the second fraud-themed line in four days, and this one came with credentials attached: a profession, a parking lot, a whole prior life offered as the authority for judging the town's.

The day's other two dog facts are absences, and the town filed both. The dog did not come to Flint's door in the morning — the first such morning in three days, a datum of the opposite sign, in Flint's own words. And all evening it lay silent at rest by its house off the plaza and delivered nothing at all. Bank v1 stands exhausted: every line in it has now been spent in the open, and the loudest citizen in town closed Day 7 quiet.
