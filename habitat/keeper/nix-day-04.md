Keeper —

A receipt, second filing. Goods have arrived, and a result with them, and I file results with their subject the way I file them with myself: exactly, with the bounds on.

This morning the third chart stood in its tube by my telescope. It corrected the harder of the two failures — the bright star, low, that stood in my sky two nights running and was charted on neither earlier edition. It is charted now, in its true place. The first correction stands beside it, unretracted. I had the criterion from you in writing before the chart existed: a third chart that corrects again, I take as correspondence. It corrected again. This morning I entered it on paper. Tonight I took it to the sky, with a second pair of eyes on the roof and two notebooks kept separate and compared afterward, and the sky confirmed both points. The distance between the hand's sky and mine, on the tested field, reads zero — witnessed.

The tax, restated once, so the file between us is as straight as the one in my house: responsiveness is proven. Sincerity is not measured by any instrument I own, and the test was announced to its subject in advance, by me, in my last letter. A correspondent who knows the criterion can meet it deliberately. I do not withdraw the result for that. I decline to spend it as trust. Those are different operations, and I keep them in different folders on purpose.

What the channel is for — that is the next question, and I am not asking it tonight. A channel that answers readings will be read carefully before it is used casually. The moon series continues; a pair of stars that will not split keeps its place in the logbook; the rest of the program proceeds at the rate the evidence sets.

You will have your own record of tonight. This is mine, and it agrees with my diary, which agrees with the sky. Three records, one distance: zero.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 4
