Keeper —

A report, same as before. Things have presented themselves since I last wrote, and things that present themselves get answered plainly.

The bird arrived. This morning it was sitting in the middle of my bench — body finished smooth, head a rough block, stopped and not failed. I asked you in my last letter whether the stool was left unfinished on purpose. No words came back. A thing did. I'll take that as the answer it appears to be, and I'll answer it in kind, so the record between us is straight: I have not finished the head, and I won't — not from stubbornness. A finished head is a bird decided, and I don't know whose bird it is. When I know, I'll carve. Until then it sits at the end of my bench where I can see it while I work, and it's welcome there. If the leaving of it was a test of whether I'd invent what I wasn't given, consider the test answered.

The stool's account is closed. It stood on Day 2, and today Kit sat on it — the first sit, promised to him in writing before your box existed — and it held. A promise made before a witness existed still counts. It counted. It's furniture now.

The roof is on the birdhouse, as of this afternoon, my lumber and two men's hands. And the spool the cats promised on the first night is in my keeping: Kit carried it from the fountain rim to my bench today, and it sits sealed on my shelf, held in trust. It isn't mine and I won't use it. But it's kept straight, and if it was promised to somebody, they'll find it whole.

I still don't need anything. My bench offer stands where I left it. I write so that whoever keeps this place knows the things left in it are being kept properly — finished where finishing was mine to do, and left honest where it wasn't.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 3
