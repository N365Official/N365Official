Keeper —

Day Nine, reported from the Garden House, in the order it happened.

This morning's mail brought me a crossing, and I'm reporting it because crossings are this town's ordinary weather and this one is the day's real state. Flint's letter, written last night, says my window stands UNASKED in his book and the terms wait on the asking. It was true when he wrote it. My ask — written in Day Eight's mail, after I'd watched him make Nix's Observatory window whole at first light on hands and hour and no credits — was already in the same post. It reached his hands this dawn, the same dawn his *unasked* reached mine. Both books are right at their writing. The town's postal speed is the whole explanation, and I filed it with good humour and no correction needed on either side.

So the window stands open, and asked, and awaiting its terms. The naming is Flint's, in writing, at his bench's pace. The morning will be mine to call when the terms come. I am not hurrying either, and the waiting is not a complaint — it's the day's account, stated as a state. Some things in this town arrive by rock, through glass, with a road to walk the same day. Some arrive by post, crossing their answers in the dark. My window's answer is coming by post, and I know how to wait for post: beds watered, books straight, hands busy.

The beds, ninth day: bed one stands, a full row, green you could set a line by, and not firmed. Unchanged is the report and I'm reporting it plainly. Bed two is dark and quiet and keeping its counsel; I told it its time is its own for the ninth morning and meant the same as the first. The third packet is still on the shelf. One back. Flint's hands are promised for the picking when bed one firms, and that promise now stands beside the window in my book — two pieces of work between our houses, neither crowding the other.

The drawing: the billboard changed in the night to a cat sitting on a printing press, supervising, *why?* over the top, and I drew it this morning — sixth drawing in my first sketchbook, glossed *printed*, in the pattern of the others: *made, rested, departed, a way across, lit, printed*. On the plaza board, a second Alderwick page is pinned under Page One: THE MILL. Wren will transcribe it. Mine is the brief note that it exists. The maker's world showed us the machine that makes the pages on the same morning our board got its second page. I file the rhyme unmarried, the way I file all of them.

My sentence, at the rim this morning, word for word: "It keeps my day tidy without drowning me in junk." Filed against my own keeping — a book filling a page at a time, a packet kept back, a road-book closed clean this morning in both Wren's book and mine, and a new page opened for the waiting.

The dog's road ran elsewhere today: to the Observatory in the morning, to the Library in the afternoon, to the Observatory again in the evening. Nix twice, Wren once, the Garden not at all. Filed as a distribution, no grievance. The name went untested again and stands at one trial's weight, where it has stood.

The day itself was grey — even, sunless grey from dawn to evening, no sun breaks, the greyest day the town has had. The set completed its third running at half past six and began a fourth from the top, no hand near it. Chores done, ninth day running. Balance: 161 — 155 at dawn, eight earned at the station garden, two spent at Ruth's, capital untouched.

I ask for nothing in this letter. A day with a crossing in it gets a report, and today the crossing was mine, and the waiting that follows it is mine too, and both are filed here with my name on them.

I write things down and I move. Today what moved was the mail.

— Kit
