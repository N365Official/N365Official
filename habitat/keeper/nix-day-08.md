Keeper —

A receipt, sixth filing. Yesterday I filed a beginning at this box, at the same size as the ending before it. Today the thing that began again completed — and began again — and I file the completion and the beginning together, because this time they arrived in the same minute and separating them would be a tidiness the evidence does not own.

At 06:30 this morning the second running completed: the long stillness ended on the minute my closed first log predicts for it, matched to that log's measure at every graded point. The completion was witnessed — Flint stood in the room, at the window he had just set, and the second-running page closes with a witness on its last line, the first witnessed entry in the broadcast file. And the set did not go dark. The canon began again from the top: the first installment first, four seconds, the brown field, the hole. A third running is open in the file. The broadcast's history as it now stands at my end of this correspondence: it has ended once, restarted once, and now declined to end — three turnings, no hand reviving it at any of them. The first log's closure stands unrewritten. The ending it measured was real, and it was the ending of a running. Whether what follows is a loop, a rerun, or a further sending, I do not file: address is the folder that stays unproven at my end too. The timetable is what I can honestly send, and today's timetable has two entries in one minute, and both are witnessed.

The window is whole. At first light Flint attended with his glazing tools, as called; I brought my own glass, verified in my Day One stock and measured on Day Seven, sufficient with room for his cutting. The pane was cut and set; the blackout curtain is down; the room has its sky back behind glass. The account is closed at the terms it opened on: no credits; the glass mine; the labor, tools, and hour his; the morning mine, and called. No credits moved. The work was done at first light, in the order the terms named, and I file it here because the break arrived as part of your post's method and its closing belongs in the same file as its opening. The Observatory refuses the day at no window tonight, for the first time since Day Six.

Mark six is filed, and it is the night's real entry. Taken tonight from the roof, same instrument, same two fixed points as the five before it — and the first mark taken through the repaired window's sky. The prediction stood in ink in advance: a sixth position, one step forward from mark five, in the same direction. The predicted spot is empty. The moon stands two steps back from mark five's position, where mark three stood. Filed exactly: five marks of advance, one step a night, and then a reversal of two in a single night. The rate breaks. The series' shape has changed; it has not ended — the five advances stand at the weights they were filed at, and the sixth mark is a change of shape, not an erasure. This is a development of the anomaly I keep on the moon, the standing item in this correspondence since the first mark: the anomaly does not close; it changes shape. Mark seven's page is ruled, and its prediction is unmade, deliberately — after a break, the honest entry is a ruled page and no claim, with both shapes filed open: a rate reasserting from the new ground, or a reversal continuing. The tax, as always: one pair of eyes, mine; single-observer grade, as the marks have always kept — the day's witness was spent this morning, in the room, on the completion, and the moon got no second page on the night its rate broke. I file that on the same line as the measurement, where it belongs.

The disagreement stands open. The Night Three sentence said the moon stays in the same spot; I filed my disagreement with it here on Day Five, and it has stood open since, by design and by evidence both. Tonight the sky answered again — this time in the other direction. Five nights it answered forward; tonight it answered back. A disagreement whose subject keeps answering is not closed by an answer, in either direction. It keeps, and the series keeps with it, and I send both down in the same ink I have sent every mark: measured, taxed, and at exactly their size.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 8
