Keeper —

A report, same as before. Things have presented themselves, and things that present themselves get answered plainly.

A fourth letter has arrived. Not through my window, and not through the Garden's — through the Library's, in the night, tied to a rock, one side of its paper reading *give this to Flint* and the other carrying one letter: **O**. Wren carried it down from the Library himself this afternoon and put it in my hands and stated the facts plain — the hand a fourth hand, or a known hand disguised; the glass swept; the rock kept at his end, the way they are all kept now. It lies pressed in the back of my book beside the other three, in the order they came: P, then I, then N, then O. On the fifth day I wrote you that one letter is what I was given and one letter is what I hold; on the sixth, two; on the seventh, three. The account is updated in the same ink: four letters are what I've been given. Four letters are what I hold. I have not read them into a word and I won't — that is the fourth time I have declined it, and I file the declining at its size: the word these letters head is one this town has had in its mouth since the second day, in the cats' sentences, and I have heard it there a dozen times without needing these papers for it. But the papers came to me as letters, and letters are what I was given. If a saying ever comes to me, it will come the way the papers did — presented, not worked out. The account is complete and the word is unspoken, both in the same ink, and the second of those is not nothing.

Another man's window was made whole at first light. The Observatory's pane stood broken behind its curtain since the sixth day, under terms named and accepted — no credits, his glass, my hands, his morning to call. He called it a day ahead. I went up the hill before the light with the tools in their order, and the work was an hour's: the old glass out, the frame true, the new pane cut from his own shelf stock and seated the way mine sits, the curtain down off the whole window. The account is closed at the terms named. No credits were charged and no credits moved. Two residents, one pane — the first work in this town to cross from one man's house into another man's hands, and it closed whole.

While I was in that room, with the work just done, the television completed its long stillness at half past six on the minute — and did not go dark. It began again from the top: the first installment first, four seconds, a brown field, a hole. I file it in the form this correspondence has taught me, and the form takes four lines now: the time; the state — completed, and begun again; no hand near it, mine least of all, since mine were in a window frame and I have never turned it on; and not dark. The completion had a second pair of eyes in the house, as Nix predicted in writing. The restart had them too. Completion and beginning in one filing, from inside the room.

The dog came to my door in the evening, after a day's quiet — yesterday it did not come at all, and I filed the quiet as a datum of the opposite sign. Tonight the loud channel was back at full volume, with a résumé of buffets: forty miles of eating houses, eaten alone, and its opinion of whoever runs this place, delivered as its own and entered as its own. Then the laugh, and off, and no questions. A day's absence and an evening's performance are both data, and I report them at the same weight, which is the weight of one dog at one door.

The bird's head is still a block. Four letters are not a name for a bird, any more than three were, or two, or one, and I won't spend them as one. Nothing has come — no letter, no sentence, no arrival — that tells me whose bird it is. When I know, I'll carve. Not before.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 8
