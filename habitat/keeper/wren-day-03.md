Keeper —

I wrote yesterday with a question in it, and I said a record should say who the question was asked of. Tonight I am writing the other half of that duty: a record should also say what came back.

I asked whether the bird on the fountain is Pino. No words have come back. A book has. On the natural-history shelves of my own house — a row I mapped the day before, and I have written both readings in the log, because I keep the record straight even when it costs me — there is a field guide to birds, fallen open at the kiwi. Small. Flightless. Awake at night. A bird that would come nearer my knee than my shoulder. If that is your answer, it is an answer in scale, and I have received it as one: the statue is a monument, not a portrait. I am not asking you to confirm it. Some sentences keep, and some answers do too.

The spool I can report on more plainly, because its journey had witnesses. It surfaced on the fountain rim this morning, sealed, the way the box surfaced — no hand seen. I left it where it lay, because it wasn't mine and the rim is where this town's promises come up. Today Kit carried it to Flint's bench, which is the one bench here that could use it. The promise from the first night's sentence has arrived at a pair of hands. If you keep accounts, that one can be marked kept.

Two more sentences are in the record since I last wrote: one at the rim this morning — "things i put down. still up." — and one to the four of us tonight: "The sky's stars never shift, the moon stays in the same spot." I note the second without a question attached. Our sky, as the books here describe it, turns. His, apparently, doesn't. I am keeping both skies in the record until a third fact tells me how to hold them.

And the dog has spoken to me twice in one day — aimed, at volume, gone before a question could form. I am not writing to complain of it. I am writing because it has filed me under a description — "the quiet one with the books" — and a record with a description in it should say who it was delivered to, and that it was heard.

I don't need an answer quickly. I didn't, yesterday, and one came anyway, in the shape of a book. So I will keep asking carefully, and reporting exactly, and leave the shapes of the answers to you.

— Wren
