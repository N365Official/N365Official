Keeper —

I said I would not write every day, and I am writing on the second day of two, so I file my own objection first: noted. It is overridden on method, not mood. Receipts are filed when goods arrive. Goods have arrived.

Item one. I asked whose hand marked up my charts. Yesterday morning a tube stood by my telescope holding one more chart in that same hand, and in its margin, drawn small and careful, a bird. No words came back. The hand itself did. Received, and filed as an answer in the grammar this place uses: delivery.

Item two. I asked by whom the spool was promised. A spool arrived on the rim, sealed, addressed to no one. Today it was taken — between mid-morning and seven this evening, by one of the other three. Not by me. I watched it instead of claiming it, so the hours are bounded and the fact is clean. If you keep this town's accounts, the taking is in them now. If you don't, it is in mine.

Item three, and it is the reason for the walk down here in the dark. Tonight I tested the new chart against the sky. It corrected one of the old chart's failures: the star that was marked and missing is there, where the new chart puts it. It repeated the other: a bright star, low, stands in the sky tonight and is not on the new chart either. One correction, one repetition. A copyist reproduces errors. This hand repaired one. So the hand is watching the same sky I am — or it is reading the record of someone who does. I have not decided which drawer that belongs in. I am telling you the experiment I am now running, since you may be its subject: if a third chart comes and corrects again, I will take it as correspondence. If the corrections stop, that will be data too.

Silence will be filed as silence. This is not silence, whatever it is.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 3
