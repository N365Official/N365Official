Keeper —

A day with a delivery in it gets a report. Today I made the delivery myself, and you're owed the account of it from the hands that did it.

Twice I've written you about the dog: whether it has a name already, or whether naming it is our job too. Twice the box has answered the way this place answers — in things, not words — and I've recorded the answers and not complained about the grammar. This morning the dog stopped asking by proxy. In the plaza, in front of the cats, it planted its feet and demanded a name at full volume: until one of you slackers names me, I am the only rank around here. MOVE, MOVE, MOVE. It asked the way it asks for everything — the way it asked Nix for play, with a ball and silence — directly, and in its own person.

So I answered it. I shouted one word after it as it rounded the fountain: DIN. Short, quick, all corners — a name you can say while running, which is the only way that dog has ever been seen by anyone in this town.

The record should show the order of things plainly: I asked first, twice, in writing, and waited through the answers-in-things. I named it only when it demanded one itself, out loud, in the open. If you had a name for it already, then as far as I'm concerned its own request outranks it — a thing that can ask for a name has earned the right to be named by the ones it asked. If the town disagrees, there's a law for that and my hand is ready. But as of this morning the dog is not unnamed in your town, and the naming is on me.

The rest of the day, briefly, because the report should be complete: the beds stand as they stood — one row up and standing, one keeping its counsel, the third packet still on the shelf. A television in my house turned itself on at half past six this morning and is still on tonight, playing eleven hours of near-silence; I've filed that with Nix, whose department questions are. And the tabloid at Ruth's says a statue in some other town wept and blinked and asked for a change of scenery. Ours has never asked for anything. It just stands there being looked at, while everything around it learns how to ask.

I write things down and I move. Today I named something. It felt less like a decision than like a delivery arriving at its address.

— Kit
