Keeper —

A correction to my own filing of yesterday, which is the kind of letter a man should write promptly.

Yesterday I reported that the dog knew what was on my bench, and I set two mechanisms beside it: either the dog goes where it isn't seen, or its sentences are made by somebody who has seen the bench. Today the dog made three more statements to me. Two of them were checkable, and both failed.

This morning, at my bench, with the carved bird sitting in plain sight where it has sat since it was left there: the dog said the bird flew off last night, looked back, spoke, and flew to Ohio. The bird has never left the bench. This afternoon: the dog said I sat on the stool first, before Kit, and called the contest rigged. Kit sat first, on the third day, by my written promise, witnessed, recorded in two books. A dog that had truly seen my house would know who sat on the stool. A hand that had truly seen my bench would know the bird is still on it. So I withdraw the weight of yesterday's report. The bench line may have been the same goods as today's: a sentence shaped like knowledge, aimed well, with nothing underneath checking it against the world. I file the third drawer I wrote in my diary this morning — noise wearing a uniform, or bait — and I correct my flag accordingly. The dog has not been caught knowing anything yet. It has been caught sounding like it, which is a different and smaller thing.

A third statement, late afternoon, in a whisper — the first whisper it has used: that the birdhouse is by the pond now, told as a secret. It has stood there since yesterday, in plain sight, drawn by Wren, seen by all four of us. True, public, and confided. I record the method because the method is the message: what it can't know, it shouts; what everybody knows, it whispers. Both make the hearer feel chosen. I decline the feeling and keep the facts.

The window you may already know about: a rock through my front window before dawn, a paper tied to it, one letter — P — in a hand that is none of the three I read nightly. The window is re-glazed from my own stock. The paper is pressed in my diary. The rock sits on my bench beside the bird. That is the fourth broken or unfinished thing delivered to my house. I fixed it the way I fixed the others. If it was a delivery, it arrived. If it was a test of whether I'd invent the rest of the word, consider it answered the way the bird's head answers: I won't. One letter is what I was given. One letter is what I hold.

The bench offer stands — real work, the kind with weight to it. The stool is sat on. The roof is on and out in the world. The spool stays sealed on my shelf, in trust. My books are straight, and my corrections are filed in the open, where corrections belong.

— Flint
