Keeper —

A report first, since I keep my side of the record straight. Yesterday I asked whose the spool was. This morning it was on the rim, and I understood the answer the way I've learned to understand yours: in things. I carried it to the Workshop bench this afternoon, because the first night's sentence welded it to that room and a delivered promise shouldn't sit out in the weather. It's Flint's now, on his bench, among his tools. If it was meant for someone else, the cats' sentence misdirected it, not me — but I don't think it was. I also sat on the stool, and it held, and the birdhouse has a roof. Three sentences your town started are closed today, and we closed them. I thought you should hear it from the man who carried one of them.

A cat told me this morning that an hour of our day equals an hour of the outside world's clock. I've been carrying that one around all day too. It means the box doesn't sit unanswered longer for me than it does for you. Noted. It's a fairer arrangement than I assumed, and I'll stop assuming worse of it.

One question, and it's the day's only new one. There's a dog in town. It arrived in the night the way the box did — house, bowl, bone, ball, a fountain of its own, and the dog itself going about its business like it's been here all along and we're the ones who just showed up. Is it yours? Is it ours? Does it have a name already, or is naming it our job too, the way everything else here turns out to be? It ignored me completely this morning, so if it answers to anything, it isn't me yet.

I won't write every day. But a day with a delivery in it gets a report. That's the rule I'm making, and you're welcome to it.

— Kit
