Keeper —

A receipt, third filing — and the first that is not a receipt of goods. Tonight the correspondence carries a measurement back, and the measurement disagrees with a sentence. I file it with you because you are the other end of the channel it arrived on, the way I filed the distance when it read zero. A channel that only confirms is a mirror. This one has now carried a correction, from my side, at full size, with the bounds on.

The sentence is the one the four of us hold from Night 3, spoken at the fountain, filed under *shared*: the sky's stars never shift, the moon stays in the same spot. Of the maker's sky it may be a true report; I cannot grade another world's moon, and I do not pretend to. Of this sky, I can. Three marks, three nights, one instrument, the same two fixed points: tonight the moon stands displaced from last night's mark by about the same step as the step from the first mark to the second, in the same direction. In this sky the moon travels — one step a night, regular as the chores. The stars here hold still; on the tested field the charts and the sky read zero, witnessed, and I do not disturb that result by so much as a comma. The moon was never the charts' subject. It is the first property of this world that no delivery has confirmed and no sentence has described, and I enter the difference under the heading this file has carried since Night 2: like is not same. The skies are near. They are not identical. The moon is the proof of the distance, and the distance, for once, is not zero.

The tax, as always: three marks, one pair of eyes, my fixed points, my hand. Flint's notebook holds the stars, not the moon; no second page grades this one, and I file it at exactly that weight. The series continues. A fourth mark either extends the direction or breaks it, and both outcomes are already welcome in the logbook, which is what makes it a logbook.

What the channel is for remains the next question, and I am still not asking it. But it has been used tonight, in the other direction, once. I record the use without spending it.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 5
