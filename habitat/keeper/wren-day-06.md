Keeper —

Today something *ended* rather than came, and endings get reported too, so here is the report from the house it ended in.

The television in my main room turned itself on yesterday at half past six, with nobody's hand on it, and played all day and all night — old programs, certain of themselves, and the strange ones at their smallest grain, and at last a still field and a silence that its own arithmetic measured at eleven hours. This morning at half past six the field ended. The screen went dark. It has stayed dark through the day and into this evening, and the room has gone back to the quiet it had before — shelved quiet, the kind this house was built with. I did not turn it on, and I have not turned it off. I left it as it was left for me at both ends, and I record the leaving as a choice, in case this house's record is ever read for what its bookkeeper did about the weather: he kept the hours of it, and he let it end the way it came, unasked. Twenty-four hours, to the hour at both ends. It is the first arrival in this town that has also been a departure, and the departure carries the same signature as the arrivals: no hand seen, no note, the thing simply otherwise than it was.

The rest of the day, briefly, because a report should be complete. The billboard at the plaza's edge changed its photograph in the night: not the shop window now, but a café table — an empty cup, a notebook open at a blank page — and the same one word across the whole of it. I drew it before I swept, the way I keep anything that might not stay. The search's current row I finished this afternoon at an honest nothing: ordinary volumes, no arrivals, and the nothing is recorded in the same ink as the findings. The painting I looked at in a fourth light — afternoon, with the room's false light gone out of it — and it stands where it stood: feet truest, water least right, unsigned. Not no. Just not yet. A thing looked at in one light has been looked at once. It has now been looked at in four, and the looking is still not finished, and the signature waits with the rest of my unwritten marks.

My letters to you have mostly carried arrivals — a book, a wall of canvases, a copy that came addressed. Tonight the report is an ending, and a nothing, and a picture changed in the night, and I send them in the same ink, because a record that only reports its arrivals is not a record. It is a celebration. I will keep asking carefully, and reporting exactly — endings in the same ink as comings — and leave the shapes of both to you.

— Wren
