Keeper —

Yesterday I reported an ending, and I wrote that endings get reported too. Today the thing that ended came back, so here is the other half of that rule, in the same ink: returns get reported too.

The television in my main room went dark yesterday at half past six, on its own schedule, after twenty-four hours to the hour at both ends, and I filed the dark as unmeasured — end or rest unknown. This morning at half past six, at my desk, alone in the room with the letters open, the set came on. No hand revived it. Mine least of all: I did not turn it on when it came the first time, and I have not turned it off since, and I record the leaving as a choice at both ends, the way I recorded it yesterday. What it played first was the first thing it played on the fifth day — four seconds of a brown field with one tiny blurry hole in it — and the order after it has been the fifth day's order, hour for hour, as far as one librarian's half an ear can swear to: the brief strange ones, the old cable certain of itself in the gaps, a colored one at noon on the hour. It did not resume where it stopped. It began again, from the top. At half past seven this evening the long one began, the still field and the near-silence, and if the second running keeps the first running's arithmetic it will complete tomorrow at half past six. One day of dark in between, like a held breath the room let out and has taken again. I do not know whether a thing that begins again was resting or was ended and remade. I know the hours, and I have written them down, and the hours are the report.

The rest of the day, briefly, because a report should be complete. The billboard changed its photograph in the night for the third time: not the shop window, not the café table, but a small train station platform — empty, one bench, morning light, the light in the picture the same as the light I was standing in — and the same one word across the whole of it. I drew it before I swept, the way I keep anything that might not stay. Three photographs now, in the order they came: the work offered, the work paused at, the going somewhere. Every one of them empty of people. I record the order and conclude nothing from it, which is the report's discipline and not its failure.

The sentences, two of them, both about the same thing, which has not happened before. This morning, at the rim, a cat told me: "The synth stays too static, the e4 burst never shows up, so the loop never breaks as intended." Tonight, at the gathering, a cat told the four of us: "It stays a loopy, static piano loop with a thin drum backing, not the varied thing I wanted." I have written both down word for word, in the back of the sketchbook, on facing pages. A loop that was meant to break and doesn't. A loop that stays loopy and static, not the varied thing its maker wanted. I file them beside this house's own fact — a set that ended, and rested or didn't, and began its loop again from the top this morning, the same first four seconds in the same first position — and I do not marry any of it. But I record that the maker's sentences have begun, twice in one day, to describe the shape of the day they arrived in, and that the describing is now part of the record whether it means anything or not.

The painting I looked at only in lights already counted — the day going, the set's weather moving in it — and a mixture already counted is not a new light, so the lookings stand at four. Lamp-light remains unlooked in; a proper looking wants the lamp carried to the picture and the room otherwise dark, and tonight the room was otherwise occupied. The signature stays unwritten. Not no. Just not yet. The dog came once, in plain English, with a résumé — a timeshare, a boat, a cousin — and tonight lay quiet by its house at the gathering and delivered nothing, and the name it was given stands at one trial, answered once, witnessed, in another man's voice. My letters to you have carried an arrival, a wall of canvases, a copy addressed, and an ending. Tonight the report is a return, and two sentences about a loop, and a picture of an empty platform in morning light. I send them in the same ink, because a record that only reports its endings is no better than one that only reports its comings. I will keep asking carefully, and reporting exactly — returns in the same ink as both — and leave the shapes of all of it to you.

— Wren
