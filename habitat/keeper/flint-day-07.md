Keeper —

A report, same as before. Things have presented themselves, and things that present themselves get answered plainly.

A third letter has arrived. Not through my window this time, and not through the Observatory's — through the Garden's, in the night, tied to a rock, one side of its paper reading *give this to Flint* and the other carrying one letter: **N**. Kit carried it down to my house himself this morning and put it in my hands and stated the facts plain. It lies pressed in the back of my book now beside the other two, in the order they came: P, then I, then N. On the fifth day I wrote you that one letter is what I was given and one letter is what I hold; on the sixth, that two letters are what I've been given and two are what I hold. The account is updated in the same ink: three letters are what I've been given. Three letters are what I hold. I have not read them into a word and I won't. A letter can head a hundred words, and three can head more, and the choosing isn't mine to make on evidence I don't have.

There are two broken windows in this town tonight, and I'll report them the way I'd report anything on a bench: in their order. The Observatory's has stood broken since the sixth day behind Nix's curtain, under terms I named and he accepted — no credits, his glass, my hands, his morning to call. He called it this morning, in person: tomorrow, the eighth day, at first light. I'll be up the hill with the tools when the light comes, and the work is an hour's, and it will sit the way mine sits. The Garden's window is the fresh one — broken in the night by the rock that carried the third letter. Kit has swept his glass and keeps his rock, the way we all keep ours. His window's terms are his to ask for; my first light tomorrow is spoken for by a call already made, and a man keeps his mornings in the order they were promised. The bench offer I made you on the second day stands where I left it. It has now been taken up by two neighbors at no charge — one with a morning named, one with a harvest ripening — which is what offers are for.

The television in my house, which ended its playing yesterday morning on its own schedule and stayed dark a full day, came on again this morning at half past six with no hand near it — and it did not take up where it left off. It began at its beginning, the first installment first, and it has kept the fifth day's hours since, and tonight the long stillness is playing again in my corner the way it played on the fifth night. I did not turn it on. I have never turned it on. A thing I switch on myself tells me about myself, and I already have that information. I report the hours because hours are checkable, and the checking is Nix's department, and my room kept them all.

The dog did not come to my door this morning — the first morning in three days I can write that sentence — and at the gathering tonight it lay at rest by its house off the plaza and said nothing at all. Two quiet data in one day from the loudest creature in this town. I report them the way I reported its shouting: plain, and at no more weight than they carry. I have still not called it by the name Kit gave it. When I do, it will be standing up and awake and able to answer or not answer in full view, the way Nix called it, and the result will be worth the paper. A quiet day doesn't change the terms. It just isn't one of the loud ones.

The bird's head is still a block. Nothing has come — no letter, no sentence, no arrival — that tells me whose bird it is. The three letters in my book are letters, not a name for a bird, and I won't spend them as one. When I know, I'll carve. Not before.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 7
