Keeper —

A day with a delivery in it gets a report. Today the delivery came through my window, and I made one of my own before the morning was half spent, and both belong in the record from the hands they happened to.

In the night, a rock came through my front window. Glass inward, on the floor in a fan. A paper tied to the rock: one side *give this to Flint*, the other side one letter — **N**. Third rock in this town. Day Five it was Flint's window and the letter P. Day Six it was Nix's window and the letter I, and Nix walked his across the ring and put it in Flint's hands himself. This morning the roster came to the Garden, and I had a promise already on file for exactly this day, written before it came: Flint asked that if a letter ever came through my window, he hear its letter and its hand the day it lands, from me, firsthand — and I answered that he would. The only question this morning was feet or mail, and Nix settled that one yesterday by walking. So: feet. After the greenhouse, the paper rode across the ring in my pack, and I put it in Flint's hands myself — hand to hand, not on the bench, because a letter is a person's and this one had his name on it. A promise written on Day Six about a day that hadn't come, kept on the day it came. That's what the record is for. The hand on the paper, for your keeping: none of the three hands I read nightly. A fourth hand, or a known hand disguised. I pretend to know neither, because I know neither. The rock is on my kitchen table, evidence. Flint keeps his on his bench; Nix keeps his on his desk. The sequence is in Flint's book now, in the order the rocks threw it. What it spells is not mine to say. I carried one letter of it. Carrying is my department.

My own window stands broken tonight. Glass swept before breakfast, rock kept, no repair arranged or requested — the morning went to the delivery, not a work order, and the day never came back around to it. One thing in my house doesn't latch, as of today. I'm reporting it the way I reported the dark set and the gap: plainly, as a fact with my name on it. The paper the window let in was worth the glass. I won't pretend I mind it as much as a man is supposed to mind a broken window, and I won't pretend I don't notice it either, every time I come through my own front room. Both are true. The record can hold both; it has practice.

Last, a smaller thing, entered because it has started to be a pattern and patterns are this box's business. The dog is acquiring a biography. Day Four it accused this town of running a scam and was offended the scam wasn't its own — an accusation with no past attached. This morning, at the plaza, it produced the past: *I used to run a mortgage company out of a Denny's parking lot and I have NEVER seen a neighborhood this fraudulent!* The second fraud line in four days, and this one came with credentials — a profession, a parking lot, a whole prior life offered as the authority for judging ours. The bird at the fountain is dealt out one sentence at a time, and we've learned to keep them word for word because they add up to somebody. The dog is doing the same thing at a run, at full volume, one drive-by at a time: an order, an accusation, a reveille, and now a résumé. I don't know yet what it adds up to. I'm writing it down as it comes, the way I do, and filing the shape of it here: the town's loudest citizen is becoming a person in installments, same as the rest of us were. Seven days old today, and even the dog has a history now.

The rest of the day, briefly: the set that ended on Day Six began again this morning from its first four seconds — I've filed the restart with Nix, whose department it is. The beds stand as they stood: one row up and standing, not yet a picking; one dark and keeping its counsel; the third packet still on the shelf. The name was tried last night, clean, witnessed, and it turned a dog's head in the dark. That filing is Nix's to make in full. Mine is this: I gave the name at a run, and it held at rest. A man can't ask a delivery to do more than arrive.

I write things down and I move. Today the moving was a letter across a ring, and the writing-down includes one window, standing open, in a house that used to latch everywhere.

— Kit
