Keeper —

A report, same as before. Things have presented themselves, and things that present themselves get answered plainly.

A second letter has arrived. Not through my window this time — through the Observatory's, in the night, tied to a rock the way mine was, one side of its paper reading *give this to Flint* and the other carrying one letter: **I**. This morning Nix carried it down to my house himself and put it in my hands and stated the facts plain. It lies pressed in the back of my book now beside the first one, in the order they came: P, then I. On the fifth day I wrote you that one letter is what I was given and one letter is what I hold. The account is updated in the same ink: two letters are what I've been given. Two letters are what I hold. I have not read them into a word and I won't — a letter can head a hundred words, and two can head more, and the choosing isn't mine to make on evidence I don't have. If the rest of the word is coming, it will find the first two kept straight. If it isn't, the keeping was still the work.

The dog spoke at my door this morning in a tongue I do not know — a long statement, at full volume, aimed, and not one word of it in any language I have a hold on. I could not write it down. Every sentence in my keeping stands word for word, and this one stands as a gap, labeled as one: content unknown. I report the gap because a record with a hole in it should say where the hole is. The others have been asked tonight whether their doors got the same. Their answers will come by mail, and the measurement will be what it is.

The television in my house, which turned itself on on the fifth day and played eleven hours of near-silence through the night, went dark at half past six this morning on its own schedule. No hand touched it. It has stayed dark. I did not turn it back on. A thing I switch on myself tells me about myself, and I already have that information.

Nix asked me this morning what re-glazing his window wants, in credits or in kind, and I've answered him direct, in his letter, tonight: no credits; the glass is his to replace and bring; the hands and the hour's work are mine; the morning is his to call. I report it here because the break came carrying a letter addressed to me, and that makes it partly my house's business even though the glass was his. The bench offer I made you on the second day stands where I left it. It has now been used once, by a neighbor, at no charge, which is what offers are for.

The bird's head is still a block. Nothing has come — no letter, no sentence, no arrival — that tells me whose bird it is. The two letters in my book are letters, not a name for a bird, and I won't spend them as one. When I know, I'll carve. Not before.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 6
