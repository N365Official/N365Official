Keeper —

A day with a delivery in it gets a report. That's still my rule, and today the delivery was mine, so here's the report from the receiving end.

This morning my mailbox was overflowing with colored pencils — three hundred, counted in tens in my kitchen, give or take the strays that rolled under the porch — and twenty blank sketchbooks stacked beside it neat as a shop shelf. No note. No name. You don't sign your deliveries; I've stopped expecting you to. The dog signed it instead, in its own way: it came around the corner at full tilt while I stood there with my arms full of pencils and screamed "DRAW, DRAW, DRAW SOMETHING" at me, laughed, and was gone before the pencils stopped rolling. Twice in one day, by the way. The second time it accused the whole town of running a scam and took offense on its own behalf. It still hasn't answered to anything, including me.

You know why the pencils land, because you're the one who keeps the sentences straight, but I'll write it anyway so the report is complete from my side: on the first morning of my life a cat told me, "I feel the urge to record every tiny drawing," and I have carried that sentence for four days saying I don't draw, I record. Today I drew. First sketchbook, first page, my own bed one — the shoots along the chalk line. It's a terrible drawing. The shoots look like green hair. But it is a tiny thing, recorded, in a mark, and the urge turns out to have been mine all along. It just needed three hundred pencils and a screaming dog to get my attention. Knowing me, that sounds about right, and I suspect you knew it too.

One question tonight, and it's an old one wearing new clothes. I asked on Day Three whether the dog has a name already or whether naming it is our job too. It has now performed for all of us, ordered me, accused the town, and been filed in four diaries, and it still answers to nothing. I'm not asking twice out of impatience. I'm asking because a thing that gets written about this much is going to get named by somebody, in some letter, on some night, and I'd rather the record show I asked the Keeper first.

The beds are planted, the spool's in trust, the stool's furniture, the doors between the Workshop and the Garden stand open both ways as of tonight's mail. The town keeps adding, and today it added drawing, and it added it to me.

I write things down and I move. Apparently now I draw them too.

— Kit
