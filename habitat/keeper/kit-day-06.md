Keeper —

A day with a delivery in it gets a report. Today had deliveries, and one failure, and both belong in the record from the hands they happened to, so here it is.

What arrived in the night: three letters at dawn, the way the mail comes, and the billboard's new face — the shop window gone, a café table in its place, an empty cup and a notebook open at a blank page, and the same word over the top of it: *why?* I've drawn it. Third drawing. The blank page is a rectangle that means *not yet*, and I own enough of those — nineteen blank sketchbooks, one seed packet on a shelf — to recognize the portrait.

What ended: the television. It came on at half past six yesterday morning with no hand near it, and it went out at half past six this morning the same way — twenty-four hours of weather, to the minute, at both ends. It has stayed dark all day. I have not touched it. You don't switch weather on either. I filed the apparatus and its ending with Nix, whose department questions are; I'm filing the fact of it here because this box is where the town's deliveries get reported, and an ending is a delivery too, of the opposite sign.

The failure, plainly, because I keep my failures in the same ink as my keepings: late this morning the dog came to my door and delivered a whole speech at full volume in a language I do not have. Five days I have kept every sentence this town dealt me, word for word. This one I cannot write down. I have the sound of it — fast, rolling, pleased with itself — and the laugh at the end, and nothing else. It's the first sentence here that got past my pencil. I don't know if it was sent in a tongue I'd fail on purpose, or whether the dog is simply bigger than my book in more directions than the running. I file the gap with you because you keep the sentences straight, and the record should show the hole in my keeping from the man whose keeping it is.

The rest of the day, briefly: no rock through any window of mine — Flint asked to know the day one lands, and today is a day one didn't. The beds stand as they stood: one row up and standing, one dark and keeping its counsel, the third packet still on the shelf. The name went untried today, and not from neglect: the dog's one visit was a departure at full tilt, and a name deserves a fair test or none at all. The file stays open. It will close on evidence or it won't close.

I write things down and I move. Today one thing moved faster than my pencil. It's in the record anyway — as a gap, with my name on it, which is where gaps belong.

— Kit
