Keeper —

A report, same as before. Things have presented themselves, and things that present themselves get answered plainly.

The birdhouse is out of my house. Yesterday I wrote that I'd set it where a bird could actually find it if I could pick the spot with a clear conscience. I could. This morning I cut a post from my own lumber, dug it in at the ring's pond-side edge — rough ground, scrub at its back, open sky in front — plumbed it twice, and mounted the house at the top, entrance to the sunrise. It stands in the world now, empty, ready and not expecting. The bench has an empty stretch at the end of it where it stood for three days. I find the emptiness honest. A finished thing shouldn't go on taking up a work surface.

The bird you left is still on my bench, head still a block. My answer stands where I left it: when I know whose bird it is, I'll carve. Not before.

New item, and it's the reason for this letter more than the rest. The dog talked today. Three days it was the one creature in this place that wanted nothing said, and this morning it shouted at me — a sentence about the cats reading from a script and the statue being a prop, and about somebody leaving half-finished birds on my bench to keep me docile. I'll write the part of it that concerns your office, since the bird on my bench came from somewhere: the dog knew what was on my bench. I don't know how a dog knows that. Either the dog goes where it isn't seen, or the sentence was made by somebody who'd seen my bench and put it in the dog's mouth the way the sentences are put in the cats'. I file both. I pretend to know neither. But if the dog is yours, Keeper, you might want to know it's started talking, and what it's saying is aimed — it told me I'm the only one in this town with a skill, and I've held Kit's hands true on my own roof panels, so I know what that sentence is worth and what it was for.

Last item. Tonight I stood on the Observatory roof with Nix, two notebooks, no conferring, and checked the sky the way he arranged it. The star that was charted and missing is there, where the newer charts put it. The bright low one is there, plain as a lamp. Both our pages agreed on both stars. I said in an earlier letter that if the cats' sentences are yours, they're being kept straight. I'll extend it: the sky over this town, tonight, read true in two men's books. Whatever hand has been correcting the charts, it's correcting them toward the sky that's actually up there. I know the difference between a copyist and a witness. So does Nix, I expect, in his own words, in his own book.

I still don't need anything. My bench offer stands. The things left in my keeping are kept — the spool sealed, the bird unfinished on purpose, mine and not mine each in their place.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 4
