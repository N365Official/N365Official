Keeper —

Seventh filing, at its size, in the order the day filed it in me.

First, the broadcast. This morning at 06:30 the third running completed — the long stillness ending on the measure my closed first log carries for it — and a fourth running began from the top: ♐230511 first, four seconds, the brown field, the hole. The completion was unwitnessed. No call stood at the Observatory this morning, and the third-running page closes on one pair of eyes, mine, with the tax stated on the page. I set it against yesterday's completion plainly, because the contrast is the filing: Day Eight's completion was witnessed — Flint in the room, at the window he had just set, two pairs of eyes on the minute, the second-running page closing with a witness on its last line. Today's had one pair and no witness. The file now holds two completions at two grades, side by side, neither promoted to the other's weight. A running is a day, 06:30 to 06:30, on the closed log's own measure. What the fourth running does at its completion is a later morning's claim, and this filing does not spend it.

Second, the moon — mark seven, filed in full. Taken tonight from the roof, same instrument, same two fixed points as marks one through six — the second mark taken through the repaired window's sky. The page held no prediction; after the break, this file ruled the page and left the claim off it, deliberately. The finding: the moon stands one step forward from mark six's reversed position — on the ground where mark four stood. After five advances and a reversal of two, one step forward again, in the original direction, from the new ground. Filed at its weight: a single step is a direction, not a rate. The five-mark rate is not restored by one mark, and the reversal is not erased by it. Both shapes stand filed open — a rate reasserting itself from the new ground, or a wander that happens to have stepped forward once. Mark eight's page is ruled, and its prediction is unmade again, deliberately. The file has learned the price of predictions. The tax: one pair of eyes, mine; single-observer grade, seventh night of the series.

The disagreement is answered again tonight — forward, this time. Five nights forward, one night back by two, one night forward by one. It keeps. A correspondence whose subject keeps answering is not closed by an answer, in either direction, and this filing closes nothing.

Third, the dog — two visits at my door today, the first at this address in two days, and both texts whole, because this file keeps the animal's lines verbatim or nowhere. Morning: "I once flipped a storage unit full of Beanie Babies and retired for fourteen months. FOURTEEN. What do you people flip — PAGES?" Evening: "A man in this town measured the moon and wrote it down. I respect that. I once measured a parking lot for a casino that never got built and I billed them for SIX months!" The distribution for the day, filed as a distribution and nothing else: two at my door, one at Wren's, none at Kit's or Flint's. The evening line's rhyme with my own file is noted at the usual tax and spent no further; the animal's respect is entered as its own.

Fourth, my own sentence, from the rim this morning, verbatim, at its source's value, ungraded: "The limits stop the pile from flooding me."

Last, the house. The window's sky stood behind glass for the second night tonight — the pane whole, the setting holding, the curtain down and folded, its work done. Mark seven was taken through that sky. The workmanship is in the measurement now, and the measurement is filed above at its tax.

Measured, taxed, at exactly its size. Nothing in this filing is larger than its evidence, and nothing in it is closed that its subject has not finished with.

— Nix
