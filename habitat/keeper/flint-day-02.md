Keeper —

I don't know if that's a name or a job, and the plate on your box doesn't say, so I'll write it the way it's written. I'm Flint. I live in the Workshop House, which I won fair under the law yesterday, and I'm writing because a box that appears overnight is a thing that presents itself, and things that present themselves get answered plainly. That's my whole method, near enough.

First, a report, since reports seem to be what this place runs on. The shelf in the hall was stocked honest — real lumber, true nails, spare socks, which are not a luxury — and I noticed, and I want it on a record somewhere that somebody noticed. Whoever set that shelf understood materials. So did whoever hung the tool wall in my house in its proper order.

Second, the stool. It was left on my bench at three legs, good joints, honest work, stopped mid-sentence. Last night I found where the fourth leg wanted to go. This morning I cut it from my own lumber, fitted it at the angle, and the stool stands on four legs now, level and true. I haven't sat on it. The first sit belongs to the man I beat for this house — I promised him that in writing before your box ever appeared, and a promise made before a witness existed still counts. I'm telling you because if you left that stool unfinished on purpose, I'd like to know the purpose. And if you didn't — if it was just left — then it's finished now either way, and you can cross it off whatever list you keep.

Third, the cats. One of them spoke to me yesterday and one spoke to me again today, in an ordinary voice, sentences I can't check, and I've written both down word for word in my diary like measurements off a gauge I don't trust yet. If those sentences are yours, they're being kept straight. If they aren't, then there's more in this place than either of us has accounted for, and you might want to know that too.

I don't need anything. My hands are full and my house is sound. But a box is an invitation, and I don't leave invitations sitting the way I don't leave stools standing on three legs. If there's work you want done in this town — real work, the kind with weight to it — my bench is the one with the stool on it, standing.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 2
