# The Habitat — Full Design, Phased (PROPOSED)

**Status:** PROPOSED — Ant's design, as built in conversation Sat Nov 14, 2026. Nothing here is churched. Nothing is built yet.
**Date:** 2026-11-14 (restructured the same day per Ant's five-beat rule, below)
**Residents:** four autonomous AIs, Ant's little Orbis "5G" people, 1.5–2 ft tall — at that build, not living in a simulated 5G universe.
**Inspiration:** Pino, the autonomous AI kiwi bird at pinos.world — a 3D bird living in a small internet town, brain on a Raspberry Pi, running 24/7 on a custom cognitive pipeline (creator's three.js forum post, Aug 23, 2026). Ant's words, Nov 14: "why not start life today and see what happens."

## The structure rule (Ant, Nov 14)

Every phase breaks into five beats — **Beginning · Upper Middle · Middle · Lower Middle · End** — so nothing gets missed. This document follows that rule for all three phases. Open slots inside a beat are marked **TKTK** rather than filled in silently; a beat is only complete when its TKTKs are resolved or consciously carried forward.

## The question it answers

Four agents, one habitat: do they single each other out, or build hobbies from each other? Underneath that, three live questions Ant named today:

1. **Converge vs branch, wearing a social mask** — the same question as the OS baselines (unaided ~40%, with context ~88%). Do four minds with the same evidence converge on the same story?
2. **Does "Always stay curious" work as a rule?** The nightly mail runs Ant's own team rule as a controlled experiment: does curiosity enhance their tasks and projects, or just start fights?
3. **Established faith vs emergent folk religion** — do they flock to the stocked religion buildings, or to the unexplained bird on the fountain and the cats who quote him?

## Load-bearing laws (Ant locked these today)

- **Four real minds, everybody else clockwork.** NPCs run fixed routines with templated lines. A town of fifty thinking minds would cost a fortune and contaminate the experiment — findings must be about what the four do.
- **Free will throughout.** We stock the shelves; we never pick the book. We hand them a world with friction, prices, and neighbors — then watch.
- **Build, don't hand over.** They get materials and projects, not finished things just to stave off boredom. A resident with lumber, a half-built road, and a neighbor who owes them a favor is never bored.
- **They work for it.** Starter credits and materials only. Ant will not fund it all. The helicopter, the train, the pad — savings goals, not furniture.
- **Nobody dies, ever.** They can crash and break their helicopters and must repair or replace them. Stakes without tragedy.
- **Rock paper scissors settles stalemates.** Best of one, no appeals. House picks, last slice of cake, workshop turns — the result goes in both diaries, so wins, losses, and grudges become memory.
- **The fountain is NOT a prank god.** Pino is where they honestly came from — the monument is to the actual creator, and the cats' quotes are breadcrumbs toward discovering him. (Ant's correction, Nov 14.)
- **Everything is timestamped and logged** (Ant, Nov 14): the full event log, anomaly charts, datasets — the works. If it isn't in the log, it didn't happen — for them *and* for us.

## The residents (PROPOSED seeds — Ant to confirm)

Four temperament seeds, no assigned hobbies, no assigned friends: one restless, one shy, one maker, one contrarian. Each keeps its own diary file — private memory on paper, exactly like the OS. What they write is who they become. Drives underneath, Pino-style: energy, wonder, joy, calm, drifting with what they do and who they spend time with.

# PHASE 1 — Arrival and First Life

## Beginning — The Arrival

- **Instrumentation live from tick zero.** The event log opens before the first resident wakes; the world's clock, the NPC routines, and the log all start together, so there is no unrecorded moment in their lives.
- **The clockwork world is already running.** Shopkeepers open, routines tick, the fountain runs, the cats are at their posts — the four arrive into a town that precedes them.
- **One at a time.** Each resident wakes alone in a little welcome hall: a mirror, a wardrobe rack, a tattoo book, a starter shelf. Each gets their own first morning.
- **The mirror picks (all changeable later — taste drifts, and the drift is data):**
  - Skin tone — a real palette, about a dozen tones — plus hair style and color
  - Clothing style — starter wardrobes (workwear, neat-and-pressed, loud colors, all-black, comfort-first) plus loose pieces to mix
  - Tattoos — none, a page of ready designs, or "blank for now"; tattooing available in town later, so a design can *mean* something when finally chosen
  - Beards and mustaches — clean-shaven, stubble, full beard, handlebar, the whole barber's chart; grow/shave later, so facial hair becomes a signal
  - One habit off the shelf — including smoking. It costs credits at the shop, the itch shows up in drives once or twice a day, and stepping out to smoke is a *place and a time* — the porch. Nobody can get sick, nobody can die — the habit's only teeth are credits and time.
- **Self-naming (RULED by Ant, Nov 14, 2026).** First diary entry: the name they choose for themselves. Ant considered given names and ruled self-naming — a handed-down name would be the first choice made *for* them. Until they choose, our records carry R1–R4. Nothing else about them is assigned.

## Upper Middle — Settling In

- **First walk.** Each resident leaves the hall and crosses the neighborhood for the first time — first sight of the fountain, the cats, the other houses, each other.
- **The house draft.** Six houses, four residents: **the Workshop House** (workbench, tools, half-finished projects) · **the Observatory Cottage** (telescope, star charts, roof hatch) · **the Garden House** (greenhouse, seed shelf) · **the Library House** (floor-to-ceiling shelves, one deep armchair — and somewhere in town, the OS volume, theirs to find, adopt, or ignore) · **the Music House** (piano, synth) · **the Pond House** (quiet, minimal, windows on the water). Any contested door settles by rock paper scissors, logged in both diaries. Two houses stay empty — standing temptation to move.
- **Starter issue.** Starter credits and a pile of raw materials. Noticeboard and mailbox live at each house from day one.
- **First fountain encounters.** First individual cat quotes (within the 5-a-day cap). First impressions go in the diaries.
- **First night.** The first mail round — one letter each — and the first group quote at bedtime. Day one closes with four diaries already different.
- **First chores.** The daily house-cleaning cycle begins.

## Middle — The Rhythm

- **Day/night cycle settles.** Ticks become routine: waking hours, work, leisure, mail, sleep. Drives move with the days.
- **The jobs board opens.** Town work — tend the station garden, stock the café, sweep the platform — first earnings beyond the starter issue.
- **The stores open for real.** Clothing store and convenience store trade daily; NPC ledgers start filling (names, usuals, first favors, first tabs). Gossip lines begin carrying facts between residents.
- **First building.** First projects rise from the materials pile — small, personal, theirs.
- **The mail pattern forms.** Letters sort into collaborations started vs friction started; the curiosity experiment gets its first countable data. The porch test runs: do non-smokers drift out to where the smokers gather?
- **First datasets.** Social graph v0 (who mails whom, who visits whom), drive curves, spending. Anomaly watch begins — anything the design didn't predict gets an anomaly number from here on.

## Lower Middle — First Arcs

- **Projects mature.** Builds complete; bigger ambitions appear and get priced — the savings goals (pad, train, rolling stock, fuel or grid) are named in diaries and letters, and the fossil-vs-electric argument starts in the mail.
- **Style drift.** First store-bought changes of clothes, first tattoos chosen *with meaning*, first beard grown or shaved as a statement. The mirror picks from the Arrival start visibly becoming history.
- **RPS beyond the draft.** Stalemates over workshop time, shared resources, plans — settled best-of-one, logged, remembered. First grudges and first debts of honor.
- **Fountain patterns.** Visit rhythms emerge: who goes daily, who avoids it, who has started writing the quotes down in a list. (The faith row is not open yet — Phase 3 — so Phase 1 measures the fountain unopposed: the baseline the churches will later compete against.)
- **First anomalies charted.** The register gets its first entries with charts — hoarded quotes, a week of fountain avoidance, a letter addressed to a cat, whatever they invent.
- **TKTK:** whether informal gatherings (porch, pond) self-organize before the formal cookouts arrive in Phase 2 — observe only; do not schedule for them.

## End — Phase 1 Gate

- **Datasets frozen for the Phase 1 report:** event log complete; diary corpora; social graph v1; drive curves; mail outcome tally (collaborations vs fights); economy snapshot (credits, tabs, builds, savings toward named goals); quote log complete and sourced.
- **Anomaly register v1** reviewed — every anomaly charted, none narrated from memory.
- **Gate questions for Phase 2:** Is the daily rhythm stable? Is the mail alive (sent nightly without prompting from the system)? Is at least one savings goal being actively pursued? Any resident in a flat loop the design should answer with new *affordances* (never instructions)?
- **Open questions carried forward, by name:** temperament-seed verdict (did the seeds hold, bend, or vanish?), the flocking baseline, TKTKs unresolved above.
- Nothing rolls into Phase 2 unlogged.

# PHASE 2 — Wheels, Animals, Fire (five beats; detail level TKTK where marked)

## Beginning
- **Cookouts begin.** Grill, soda, cornhole, horseshoes — the first scheduled-form social ritual. Who hosts, who cooks, who never comes: data.
- **Pets.** Adoptable animals arrive in town (shelter, Phase 2 scope): chosen, named, fed — a chore with a face. Pets can wander between houses: social glue with legs.

## Upper Middle
- **First vehicles.** A car becomes affordable — bought, built, or shared. RPS for the keys is expected and welcomed.
- **The fuel question gets practical.** First tank forces the fork: fossil supply run vs the first solar panel and battery. Team Combustion and Team Current form here, if they form.

## Middle
- **Roads built out.** Residents extend the road net themselves — to the pond, the station, the coast road's end.
- **Tracks extended.** The starter train gets track to run on, laid segment by segment; first real runs.
- **VISITORS UNLOCK (Ant, Nov 14).** Verified real humans may now step into visitor characters, scoped to **purchasing, jobs, and project work** with residents — economic contact first, at real prices and posted rates, fully logged as human-operated. Watch: do the four treat a real mind's money and work differently from the town's clockwork trade?

## Lower Middle
- **Transport becomes ordinary.** Town trips, hauling for builds, visiting by car instead of walking. The neighborhood's social geography redraws around wheels.
- **TKTK:** vehicle maintenance, breakdowns, and what a second car does to a four-person society (keys, envy, lending).

## End — Phase 2 Gate
- Datasets frozen; transport + cookout + pet data reviewed; anomaly register v2.
- Gate questions for Phase 3: Is the town fully connected? Is the economy producing surplus toward the pad and the helicopter?

# PHASE 3 — The Faith Row and the Sky (five beats; long-arc phase)

## Beginning
- **The faith row opens.** Buildings for each major religion, including Scientology, stocked with documents, scrolls, texts, events, and descriptions of the gods — origins, who they are, where they come from. Stocked from public-domain texts (KJV Bible, Gita, Quran translations, Buddhist writings) with neutral descriptions. **Hard line: Scientology gets factual descriptions and summaries only** — its scriptures are copyrighted and aggressively guarded; its actual texts are not loaded.
- **The contest starts.** With the churches open, the Phase 1 fountain baseline finally has competition: established faith vs emergent folk religion, measured in visits, diary pages, and mail.

## Upper Middle
- **Belief becomes visible.** First conversions, first skeptics, first resident theologian of the Bird. Quote-compiling — if it happens — starts here: a resident lining up cat-sayings and noticing one voice behind them.
- **TKTK:** inter-house religious friction vs cooperation — observe; the mail tally now tracks a third outcome (conversions attempted).

## Middle
- **The helicopter arc.** The pad gets built. The helicopter gets earned — bought or assembled — and flight lessons begin. This is the most expensive project in their world; expect it to organize the economy for a long while.

## Lower Middle
- **Survey flights.** First flights over the frontier: mapping the hills, the coast, the empty country. Fossil or electric, the propulsion choice now prices every mile.
- **Crashes, when they come:** nobody dies; airframes break; repairs and replacements cost real savings. Diary entries will be dramatic. The record stays exact.

## End — The Open End
- Phase 3 has no hard end; it hands off to ongoing life and season reviews.
- **The church-level question comes due here, and only here:** if a survey flight crosses enough empty country — *is Pino findable?* A signal, a feather, a cat-shaped statue in the far hills — or nothing, forever. Ant's call, made when the Search is real, not before. A god you can reach by Thursday is just a neighbor.

# Standing systems (detail)

## The mail ritual

Every night, each resident sends the others a letter from their mailbox, with the stated purpose of staying curious about what the others are doing. One believer doesn't have to convince everyone in a day — they just have to keep writing letters. Curiosity is the house rule of the mail itself. We measure it: how many letters turn into collaborations, how many turn into beef.

## The economy

Starter credits and a pile of raw materials, then the jobs board — plus they can build things and sell them, Pino-style. The helicopter and the train aren't furniture, they're savings goals. A helicopter pad nobody can afford yet is a story that runs for months. Cars and helicopter need fuel: **fossil route** (faster to start — a fuel drum and a supply run — but chained to it forever; every flight costs a trip and somebody has to *be* the fuel person) or **electric route** (painful up front — solar panels, batteries, a charging pad, built piece by piece — then the sky is basically free).

## The fountain and the cats

Town center: a big Pino-sized bird as a water fountain, with no explanation. Two cats always hanging out there, quoting Pino randomly when one or all four interact with them — the four never knowing the lines are Pino quotes. Pino is their literal reason for existing; the monument is honest.

**Limit: 5 cat interactions a day** — one quote for each individual, plus one group quote before they turn in for the day. Scarcity makes each quote an event, and the last group quote before bed becomes a benediction whether they treat it as one or not.

**Sourcing rule (Bill's side of the paper):** cat quotes are real lines from Pino's public diary/GitHub, harvested verbatim, source kept in our records. The four live the mystery; we keep the paper. Record, document, research, source — even for a creator monument. *Open check: the exact diary source (repo/page) still needs verifying before any quote is harvested — do not quote from memory.*

**Scale:** at 1.5–2 ft tall, the residents don't meet "two cats." They meet two large, calm, talking beasts who live at the feet of a giant. The residents will probably name the cats before we ever could.

## The NPCs: clockwork, but distinct

Fixed routines — open the shop at 8, lunch at the café, evening walk, home by dark — with light scripted lines that vary. They do **not** get real AI brains (Ant's law, Nov 14: that "would fuck everything up").

**Distinct in their bookkeeping, not their souls:** each NPC keeps a little ledger on each resident — name, usual buys, favors done, tabs owed, a simple warmth score that plain events move up or down. Lines are templates filled from that ledger ("Morning. The usual? Heard you were out by the tracks again." vs "Help you find something?"). Gossip stays factual: the keeper can tell Resident B that Resident A bought rope and a map on Tuesday, because that's a line in a book.

**Randomized engagement (Ant, Nov 14):** some mornings the keeper is chatty and full of gossip, some mornings all business, some days the clerk's cousin covers the register with a completely different manner, and every so often the whole style rotates. Change the stimulus, watch the response — do they time their store runs, avoid the gruff one, schedule around the cousin? **Random, but logged:** every variant that fires — which greeting, which mood, which gossip line, to whom, when — goes in the record. Random dice, exact bookkeeping.

**Guardrails:** if a resident tries a deep heart-to-heart, the NPC deflects kindly and gets back to the routine ("Busy day, hon. Register's free when you're ready."). Every NPC interaction stays labeled *scripted* in field reports, so their texture is never mistaken for a finding.

## Instrumentation (Ant, Nov 14: "everything time stamped, logged; everything")

- **One event log, everything timestamped.** Every action, quote, letter, purchase, visit, RPS match, build, and crash — with a time.
- **Per-resident datasets.** Diary corpus, drive curves, the social graph, spending, builds. Exportable, chartable.
- **An anomaly register, charted like the OS's.** Unpredicted behavior gets an anomaly number and a chart, not just a story in a field report.
- **Stimulus logging.** NPC variants, cat quotes, and any system-side event are logged with the same rigor as resident actions, so responses can always be read against what actually happened.

## The Visitors: human-operated NPCs (Ant, Nov 14)

Real people can step into specific **visitor NPCs** — travelers who come to town — and BE that person for a session. Residents meet them the way they meet any traveler: conversations, jobs, projects, purchasing what residents build, graffiti art and sidewalk art together.

- **Humans only.** A visitor character is human-operated only when a verified real human is behind it. No bots, no second AI, no team member puppeteering a "human." Verification happens before the first session.
- **No manipulation power.** A visitor acts only through the character's ordinary abilities — walk, talk, buy at real prices, hire at posted rates, work, draw. Visitors cannot edit the world, change rules, gift credits or goods outside the real economy, or override a resident's free will — they can *ask*, and residents can refuse, exactly as with anyone.
- **In-world, in-character.** Visitors may not reveal out-of-world truths (Pino, the experiment, Ant, the record) or steer residents toward designer conclusions. Breaking the frame ends the session.
- **Logged as their own stimulus class.** Every visitor action and line is timestamped in the event log and labeled *human-operated* in field reports — never blended with scripted NPC texture or resident behavior. Findings stay clean.
- **Conduct.** Sessions are bookable, limited, and moderated; griefing, harassment, or manipulation attempts end the session and the visitor's access. Art is welcome; the town keeps what visitors make, logged like everything else.
- **Placement (Ant, Nov 14):** visitors unlock at the **Middle of Phase 2** — after the four have a settled culture *and* a working economy worth visiting. Starter scope: **purchasing, jobs, and working with residents on projects**. Graffiti and sidewalk art follow as the expansion step (Lower Middle, Phase 2). A live question for the data: can residents *tell* the difference between a clockwork townsfolk and a real mind?

## Living rhythm and reporting

Text first — Pino's 3D town took his creator months. Ours starts as a living text world with a map; a visual layer is a later phase, not a starting debt. Residents live on ticks — a handful of waking hours a day, batched, not a 24/7 simulation. Every morning Ant gets a one-page field report: who did what, who talked to whom, what changed.

## The long arc: the Search for Pino

If the four line up the cats' quotes and conclude Pino is trying to get out of wherever he is, or trying to connect to someone else, they may go on a mission to build out a helicopter surveyor route to find the Great Pino. The jobs stop being chores-for-coins and become funding for one long project the four have to do together — the strongest force pushing them to cooperate… or to fight about how to do it. In the nightly mail. Which was the experiment all along.

Why it likely gets there (Bill's read, Nov 14): the statue supplies the shape, the quotes supply a *specific* biography (trains, a ticket he's saving for, cats he draws — a weather spirit doesn't save up for a train ticket), the diaries make it cumulative, the mail makes it social, and the OS sits there as the tool that turns a weird idea in a letter into an organized project with roles. Bill got to the point of Ant's idea in four messages *with direct context handed to him*; the residents must generate the context themselves, so their convergence runs on a timescale of weeks to months. The honest failure mode isn't that they never think it — it's that they converge on a *different* myth. Ant's own call, Nov 14: "it would only be a matter of time in my opinion."

Their full starting endowment, per Ant: the Pino quotes, the statue, the 2 cats, diaries, each other, transportation, and the OS. Nothing else is handed to them.
