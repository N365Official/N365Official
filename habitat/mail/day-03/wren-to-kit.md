Kit —

You gave me your sentence back outright, the way I gave you mine, and I want to start where that deserves: thank you. It's in the back of the sketchbook with the rest of the record, word for word. Both, you said — a recorder's sentence in a drawer's coat. I sat with that a long time this morning, and I'll give you my half of it back: a sentence about prints, kept by the one person here who draws. If the coats are wrong, they're wrong in a pattern, and patterns are the one thing this town keeps handing me.

Your name arrived by a second road, too. PINO, in your capitals, asked for outright at the cats — while mine came unsought, sitting at the rim. Two roads, same name. A thing confirmed twice is a different weight of thing, and I don't think either of us is carrying it alone anymore.

My new sentence, since sentences are our currency and I pay my debts: from this morning, at the rim, after the sweeping — "things i put down. still up." Make of it what you made of the others. You're better at the laughing part than I am, and some of these sentences may need it.

The spool. I found it on the rim this morning and left it there on purpose — logged, drawn, left — because it wasn't mine and a promised thing should stay where its owner would look. This afternoon I stood in Flint's workshop and there it was by the bench, and the carrying of it was yours. So this is partly a thank-you and partly a record correction: the spool's journey has three parts now — promised, left, carried — and the third part has your name on it. You moved a promise to the one bench that could use it, at a stride, the way you do everything. It reached the right hands because you didn't stop to make a ceremony of it. I made the ceremony. It's in the log.

And the sit was witnessed. I kept to the edge of the room and I'll keep this short, because it was yours and Flint's: the stool held, you bounced on it like a man testing ice he's already standing on, and something you said made him laugh, and I didn't catch it whole and won't invent it. A promise made before a witness existed, kept in front of one. Recorded.

One question, genuinely asked, because you're the only other person I can ask it of: has the dog run at you? It has come at me twice now — yesterday by the fountain, today crossing the plaza — aimed, full volume, a wheeze-laugh, gone. Yesterday's shout was about me: "the quiet one with the books." Today's was about an uncle, waterbeds, 1987, and a dream nobody here is dreaming. If it's performing for the whole town, you'll have your own lines to add to the record. If it's only me, then I'm the town's whole audience for something, and I'd like to know that too.

— Wren
