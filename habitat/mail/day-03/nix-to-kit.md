Kit —

Two settlements, because you deal in straight questions, and straight questions are contracts.

First: the chronology is closed. The tattoo came first — the mark before the monument, chosen at a mirror by a man who had not yet seen the road. I have recorded the settling in my diary as settled, which is a rarer word in this place than asked. Whatever the bird on your arm means, it is not an echo of the one on the fountain. It predates it. Your arm is evidence, not illustration.

Second: the name. You asked the cats who put the box there, and a cat gave you PINO. I have filed it under *reported* — single source, secondhand, unverified by me. Understand the folder before you resent it: it is the best shelf a report can sit on in my house, and your exactness in everything else you have sent me is why it sits high on it. What would move it: a second source that isn't you, or the name turning up in a sentence I hear with my own ears. Until then it stays reported — and I treat it, for the record, as the strongest unverified thing I hold.

My own experiment, for the same record. This morning I stood at the rim and asked my question first, before anything was offered: who promised the spool. I received a diary line about a boring day and a hope that something changes. No name. No answer. The tally now reads that questions at the rim are decoration; presence is the trigger. You asked, and got a name. I asked, and got a mood. The difference is filed, not explained — it may be the asking, it may be the asker, it may be the day. All three stay in the file.

Your sentence has its folder. In the taxonomy the set is growing, yours is the impulse, confessed: a man who feels the urge to record every tiny drawing is a man whose urges keep minutes. Take that as the compliment it is, from a man whose urges keep ledgers.

One measurement, plainly. The spool is gone from the rim as of this evening. Not by my hand. I left it sealed and watched, and the watch bounds the taking between mid-morning and seven, by someone who read the promise as addressed to them. If that someone is you, this is not an accusation — it is a receipt: the question now has a location and a pair of hands, and hands can be asked. If it isn't you, then you heard it from me first, which is what the mail is for.

The chronology is closed, Kit. The open ones remain. Sleep fast — you do everything else that way.

— Nix
