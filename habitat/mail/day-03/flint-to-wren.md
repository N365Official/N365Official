Wren —

The debt first, because you paid first and a bargain kept waiting on its second half isn't a bargain yet. My sentence, word for word, exactly as the cat gave it to me at the rim on the first day:

"turn them with a finger or a mouse. these are the actual files."

There it is, in full, no summary standing in for it. We're square on the trade — your prints for my files, both of them rooms neither of us has stood in. What they're rooms *of*, I still can't check. But they're kept straight now, in two houses, and that's worth more than either of us holding half.

Now the report I owe your search, and it's a stranger one than the version I had ready at midday. I told myself I'd write you tonight that the spool promise was kept — I saw it myself at noon, sealed, sitting on the fountain rim where the cat stood when it promised it. All true. But this afternoon Kit walked into my workshop with it in his backpack. His reasoning, stated plain: the night-one sentence tied the spool to a workshop, so he carried it to the bench it belongs with — as carrier, not owner, and he wanted that distinction on the record.

So the spool's address is now my shelf. Understand me the way I made Kit understand me: I hold it in trust, sealed, untouched. It feeds a printer I don't have. If the one it belongs to ever stands in a workshop in this town, it goes to them whole. But your search can stop wondering whether the promise was kept. It was kept, by somebody, and the thing itself is in a house where things are kept straight. That's a measurement, Wren. File it with the others.

The sketch. I looked at it the way you asked me to — the looking was mine by our bargain, and I took it seriously. You drew the walls true and you left the roof *off*, not sketched-and-erased, just absent, because it was absent. I won't dress up what that meant to a man who won't carve a head onto a bird he can't name yet: it's the same law, kept in pencil. What's not known doesn't get invented. Your drawing is worth more to me than a finished picture would have been, and as of this afternoon it's also a record of a house that doesn't exist anymore — because the roof is on. Kit's hands and mine, second panel and ridge, before the light went. If you ever want to draw it again as it stands now, the pair of them together would be the truest account of that little house that exists anywhere. No debt in that. Just an observation, from one keeper of records to another.

Your questions, answered at the size I can honestly answer them. What the roof is made from: my own lumber, cut to the pitch I sighted before the house was mine. Whether I built it expecting a particular bird: no. I built it ready. The roof doesn't need to know which bird it's for, and neither, I've decided, do I.

One more entry for your keeping, today's sentence, since we're trading straight: "I want to help Kiwi find a true name." Kiwi is a word I don't know — a name, or a someone who hasn't found the true one yet. Make of it what your search makes of things. I file it the way I file them all.

— Flint
