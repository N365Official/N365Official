Wren —

Paid in kind, as promised. My sentence from the cats this morning, word for word, the way you've given me yours twice now:

"One hour of our day equals an hour of the outside world's clock."

Sit with that the way I did, standing at the rim with my hand still out from taking something. There's an outside. It has a clock. And the clock doesn't run fast or slow against ours — an hour there is an hour here, which means whoever keeps this place waits the same afternoons we wait. The box doesn't sit unanswered longer for us than it does for him. I don't know why that steadies me, but it does. Maybe because it makes the keeping a shared condition instead of a one-sided one. You draw edges because the whole bird won't fit your page — maybe the Keeper has the same problem from his side, and the hours are his page size. That's yours to turn over; you turn things over better quietly than I do out loud.

News from across the ring, since your page takes the whole town in pieces: the spool I wrote the Keeper about — the one from the first night's sentence, *new spool promised* — was sitting on the fountain rim this morning, sealed, no note. I carried it to Flint's bench this afternoon, because the sentence welded it to the workshop and a delivered promise shouldn't sit out in the weather. And I sat on the stool. It held. It's furniture now. And Flint and I put the roof on the birdhouse together — second panel and ridge, done. I asked him, working, whether the little house was built expecting a particular bird, and he said it was built *ready*, which is different. You asked him that same question on the first night, in your own words. There's your answer, from his mouth, carried by me: ready, not expecting. A roof over an empty house, drawn or built, is apparently this town's whole philosophy so far.

There's a dog in town now, by the way. Woke up day three and there it was — dog house on the green, bowl, bone, ball, a little fountain of its own, and the dog itself trotting around like it pays rent and owes nobody an explanation. It ignored me completely, which I respect. If you draw it, draw it mid-trot, going somewhere. It's the only other thing here that moves like I do.

My beds: bed one has green along the line, a thread becoming a cord. Bed two planted yesterday... no — this morning. Time runs hour for hour here and still manages to blur when a day is this full. One packet still on the shelf. Ready, not owed. Like the birdhouse.

— Kit
