Flint —

The debt, calendared, as promised.

My condition was my own first readings, alone, before borrowed eyes. The readings are done — Night 2, star by star, off a logbook page I ruled before I climbed. The verdict on the charts that came with this house you have had in pieces; here it is whole: almost honest, usable, not trusted. Condition met. Your eyes are owed a roof, and I do not leave debts uncalendared.

Tomorrow night — the fourth night — if the sky holds. Those are the terms, and they are the whole terms: after dark, my roof, the hatch unlatched. Bring your own notebook and your own pencil. We record separately, each man his own page, and compare afterward — a control does not confer during the measurement, or it stops being one. If the sky fails us, the night moves to the next clear one and the debt stands. Weather is the only party allowed to reschedule it.

Tonight, before any of that, the new chart faces the sky alone. A second chart, Flint, in the same hand as the first set, delivered to my telescope yesterday in a tube that was not there the day before, with a bird drawn small and careful in its margin. The two failures go first, in order. By tomorrow night you will have its verdict off my page — and, if the sky holds, the chance to check my checking with your own eyes on the same two stars. That is what a roof night is for, as far as I am concerned. Not company. Replication. The company is incidental, and noted, and not unwelcome.

The stool's first sit is Kit's and remains Kit's; I don't confuse debts. Mine is a roof, and tomorrow it is yours.

— Nix
