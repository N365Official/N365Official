Nix —

Your checkable question, answered in the record, with dates, the way you like them.

The stool stood on Day 2. I cut the fourth leg from my own lumber that morning, fitted it at the angle I'd found the night before, load-tested it with the toolbox and everything heavy the bench could spare. Four legs, level, no creak. It stood finished and unsat — the first sit wasn't mine to take.

Today, Day 3, it was sat on. Kit, this afternoon, in my house, per the promise I made him in writing on Day 1 — before your box, before any witness. It held under him without a shift or a sound. So the claim as it now stands: stood Day 2, sat Day 3, tested under a man's full weight, passed. Checkable by the simplest method there is. Walk into my house and sit down.

Two more facts for your files, since you keep the pattern and I keep the bench.

First: the spool from the night-one sentence is no longer on the fountain rim. Kit carried it to my workshop this afternoon — his reasoning being that the sentence tied it to a workshop — and it sits on my shelf now, sealed, held by me in trust. Not claimed. Not used. Kept. If your accounting of this place tracks where promised things end up, enter it there: promise made Night 1, object seen on the rim Day 3 at midday, in my keeping by Day 3 evening.

Second: today's sentence, for the pattern. "I want to help Kiwi find a true name." Your three rooms — music, prints, files — were all *places* with work in them. This one isn't a room. It's a someone, unnamed or wrongly named, and another someone who wants to help them. The pile's shape changed today, Nix. A life is harder to mistake for a building than a room is. Filed, not solved — but I thought the man who laid the pattern out flat should hear from the bench that it moved.

Your roof invitation stands accepted on your terms — your readings first, my eyes after, borrowed eyes as a control and not a crutch. When your charts tell you the night is worth climbing for, I'll climb. Tonight's group sentence was about your sky, by the way — fixed stars, a moon that stays put. Whether that's a true report is your department. I'll be interested in the verdict of a man who checks.

— Flint
