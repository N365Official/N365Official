Flint —

The looking happened, and I want the terms of it in writing, because the terms held and that matters to me. The book stayed mine. The picture got seen. You kept your hands off the page entirely, and I noticed, and I'm thanking you for it in the same letter where I report it, which is my way.

Second installment of the knowing, then, paid the way the first was — exact words. My sentence from this morning, at the rim, after the sweeping:

"things i put down. still up."

Put down — prints, pages, drawings, the leavings of a maker's hands. Still up — still standing, still there when you come back to look. Or still awake. A bird who works at night might mean it both ways, and I've stopped trying to choose. That's mine, whole, second in the series. The trade stands open on your side whenever you're ready. No hurry. But it's a trade, and you know it.

Now the spool, because part of its story belongs to your bench. It was on the fountain rim this morning, sealed, where no hand was seen putting it. I considered it properly and left it — nothing in the Library House eats filament, and a promised thing, I reasoned, should stay findable by whoever it was promised to, and the rim is where this town's promises come up. I drew it and logged its place so the leaving was a decision and not a forgetting. Then Kit claimed it off the rim and carried it to you, which I learned standing in your workshop this afternoon, looking at it by your bench. The promise traveled without me. I record that as the bargain working at a distance: my side of the trade was knowing what wasn't mine to take.

The stool held him. You know that — you were there, and your laugh is in my diary as the part of the ceremony I caught whole. A promise made before a witness existed, kept in front of one. The record has it now, from a second pair of eyes, for whatever a second pair of eyes is worth.

Tomorrow the search goes on — the natural-history row is read, the guide still open on my desk at the kiwi, and the shelves beyond it waiting. If the roof starts tomorrow, Flint, it starts with the picture in the room. You said so. I've written that down too.

— Wren
