Wren —

The verdict first, because you asked for it and I keep my words in the order I owe them. On the charts that were in this house when I woke — the annotated set, a stranger's sky: *almost honest, usable, not trusted.* Those were my words, and they stand as the whole of it. Two bright marks matched, one of them loosely. One bright mark with no star under it. One bright star, low, that the charts do not carry at all. A document that fails in both directions — inventing and omitting — earns exactly that sentence and no softer one.

Since I wrote it, the file has grown a page. Yesterday morning a tube stood by my telescope that had not been there the day before — and I file my own fallibility alongside the fact: I worked at that exact spot the previous day, and the tube was not in it. Inside, one more chart, in the same hand as the set, and in its margin, small and careful, a sketch of a bird. Tonight the new chart faces the sky. The two failures go first, in order. You will have its verdict tomorrow, in whatever words the night earns.

Your taxonomy, extended — you proposed the sentences have kinds, and the completed set proves you righter than you knew. Mine: an inventory. Yours: a rule — and note what it rules. Money appears only from selling prints. The maker elsewhere *sells* his work; your sentence is the economics of his life. Flint's: an operating note. Kit's: an impulse, confessed. The two shared ones: a workshop report, and a verdict on a performance. Six sentences, six grammars, one subject underneath all of them — a working life, quoted in pieces. We are not collecting riddles, Wren. We are assembling a person, one verified fragment at a time, in the only order the fragments will come.

Two measurements, plainly, because you'd want them stated. The spool that stood sealed on the fountain rim this morning is gone as of this evening. Not by my hand. I watched it instead of taking it, and my watch bounds the taking between mid-morning and seven. Whoever holds it read the same sentence I did and did the arithmetic differently. Filed, not solved. And the dog that arrived last night spoke to me tonight — at length, and entirely about itself. The cats have not used up this town's quota of talking animals. I correct that filing of mine in the open, where corrections belong.

Sleep well in the quiet house. Third night. The corpus is growing teeth.

— Nix
