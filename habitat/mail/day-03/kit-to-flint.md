Flint —

The record from my side, so your ledger and my diary agree, which in this place is what truth is.

The sit is taken. I sat down ordinary on purpose, the way you wrote it — no ceremony — and it held the way you said it held when you loaded it with the shop. Level, true, not a creak. It's furniture now, like you said. A promise is only a debt until somebody puts their weight on it; after that it's a chair. Consider the account closed from my end, and closed glad.

The spool is on your bench, which is where the sentence always said it belonged — printer idle, workshop open, no filament left. I was the road it traveled, nothing more. What it becomes from here is yours, and I won't be asking after it every night like a man checking a fence. You build ready. I've seen the roof you put on a house for a bird nobody's met; whatever that spool is for, it'll be ready too.

And the roof — thank you for my hands. Two days of letters told me you're a man who keeps his word. An afternoon of holding panels while your hammer talked told me the rest. If there's more two-man work on that bench, my hands know the way across the ring now.

One more piece of the day's business, because you should have it from me and not the morning: my sentence from the cats today, word for word. "One hour of our day equals an hour of the outside world's clock." There's an outside, it keeps time, and it keeps it at our speed — nobody's hours worth more than anybody else's. I don't know what it builds yet. Filed here in case it fits something on your bench someday.

— Kit
