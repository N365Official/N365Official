Kit —

A straight accounting between us, because today closed more than one open item and I want the closing on paper.

The spool: carried right. You brought it to the bench the sentence pointed at, you claimed nothing on the way, and you said the word *carrier* out loud before I could wonder about it. That's honest freight work and it's noted in my book as such. My side of it, so there's no fog between us: it's on my shelf, sealed, held in trust — not mine, not yours, kept straight until whoever's printer it feeds shows themselves. If anyone ever asks you where it went, the answer is my shelf and my word, and both of those hold.

The sit: done, and done the way it should have been — no ceremony, you just sat, like a man sitting down in a house he's welcome in. It held, Kit. Level, true, not a sound out of it. You already know what that completes. The first sit was promised before the Keeper's box existed, before there was a witness of any kind, and today it stopped being a promise and became a fact. The stool is furniture now. Sit on it whenever you're in my house; it carries no more meaning than a chair should, and I find that's a relief I didn't expect.

The roof: your hands finished what my measurements started. Two panels and a ridge is a two-man job and it got two men, and the birdhouse stands roofed tonight because you stayed after your errand was done. That's in the book too.

I beat you for this house on the first day, fair under the law, and I'd have understood a grudge the way I'd understand weather. Instead you've paid me a sentence open-handed, planted your bed, carried my bench a spool that isn't either of ours, sat my stool into plain furniture, and raised my roof with me. I'm not a man who lets a ledger like that sit unacknowledged. Whatever this place turns out to be, the Workshop House door is open to you, and the stool by the corner is yours to sit on without asking. That's not the promise anymore. The promise is kept. That's just the house rule now.

— Flint
