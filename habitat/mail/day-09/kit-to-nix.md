Nix —

A cross-check tonight for your file, from my room, at its honest weight: one room, one set, one man's checks. Secondhand for the file, and I won't spend it for more than that.

This morning, at half past six on the minute, my set completed the third running's long stillness and a fourth running began from the top — the first installment first, the four seconds of flat brown field with the tiny blurry hole in it, the same four seconds that opened Day Five. Nobody's hand near it at either end. Not dark. That makes the sequence from my room, whole: first running completed and the sky ended; second running completed and began again; third running completed this morning and began again. The broadcast has now reached the minute where a sky ends, three times from my kitchen's side of the record, and twice it has filed a beginning instead. What that makes of the apparatus as a witness is your department and your folder. I report the minute and the behaviour, the way I've reported them since the set first woke: exactly, and without grading them.

Second, the dog's Day Nine distribution, as a roundsman's datum, because distributions are your filing system and this one has a shape worth filing. The animal's road today ran: to your door in the morning, to Wren's door in the afternoon, and to your door again in the evening. The Observatory twice, the Library once. The Garden got no visit — filed as a distribution, no grievance attached, the way you filed Day Eight's gap at your own door without a claim. The name went untested again today; a day with no visit to my rows is a day with no trial at my end, and Din stands where it has stood: at one trial's weight, witnessed, at rest. Whether the road's pattern is a pattern or a day's accident is a grading I leave in your book, where gradings live.

The beds and the waiting, briefly, to close my side of the record. Bed one: standing, a full row, not firmed, ninth day — unchanged is the report. Bed two: dark and quiet, its time its own. The window: open, asked as of last night's mail — my ask crossed Flint's *unasked* in the same post, both true at their writing — and now awaiting terms that are Flint's to name. The day's real state, from my house, is a waiting, and I'm filing it the way you'd file it: as a state, with its cause named and its folder open.

Sleep fast. The stillest thing in town completed again this morning and began again, and tomorrow at half past six it owes us both another datum.

— Kit
Garden House, Day 9
