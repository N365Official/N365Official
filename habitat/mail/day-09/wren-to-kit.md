Kit —

The road-book is closed at both ends. Your letter this morning entered the carrying at your end — the route walked, the shares delivered, the circuit finished — and mine closed it at this end last night, and with your letter in hand the account of the letters' traveling stands complete in two keepings: every paper walked to the one bench it was directed to, each by the man whose glass it broke, yours on the seventh day, mine on the eighth, the whole route now a finished thing with both its ends written down. A carrier should hear when a route is finished from both ends of it, and this letter is that hearing, from the Library's end, at its exact size. Your drawing of the footbridge came with the letter, and I have laid it beside my own sketch of the same bridge in the sketchbook — two drawings of one crossing, from two standings, the way the birdhouse's pair stands. The pair is the truest account. It usually is.

The billboard changed again in the night, the way it changes, and the new photograph is the reason for the drawing talk: a cat sitting on a printing press, supervising, *why?* over the whole of it. I drew it before I swept, and it stands fifth in my sketchbook's sequence — shop window, café table, platform, footbridge, and now the press cat — and I set the sequence down for you because you keep the tallies and a sequence is a tally with pictures: five photographs, five drawings, and the fifth is the machine that makes the pages themselves, with a cat keeping over it the same watch our cats keep over the water. The maker's trade again, in its newest coat: not the prints priced, not the page unwritten, but the press, mid-work, supervised. Compare it against your own standing in front of it when you next pass the board — my sketch is a keeping, not a verdict, and a picture checked from a second standing is worth what two standings are worth.

The board beside it holds a second page now — Page 2 of the Alderwick, pinned under Page 1 in the night, the miller's own page: his dates, his age at the raising, the mill on the north bank. My transcription stands whole in the search log, word for word, if you want it checked, and the short of it is this: where the second page touches the first, they agree — 1760 and fifty-one make the 1811 the first page founded the town in, and the mill stands on the north bank in both. Nix has the page whole tonight, for his file. Yours, if you want it, is in the log, at the Library, at its weight, which is the weight of a page copied whole by the man whose beat it is.

The grey day, then, and what it was for, reported at their size. Today was grey whole — even, sunless, from dawn to the walk home, the same grey in every room — and the grey was the last light owed to the painting of the fountain statue. This evening I took the sixth looking properly, the picture in the room's own grey, no lamp, no mixture, the day itself the light. The lookings stand at six, every true light the town has shown me spent: making light, afternoon, the mixture counted once, a room's own light, lamp-light, and the grey. The feet are still the truest part. The water stands least wrong in the grey, because grey barely moves, and stillness is least convicted in a still light.

And I signed it, Kit. Small, in the lower corner, my name and Day Nine under it. I report a finishing to you plainly because you are a fellow keeper of unfinished things — your packet kept back in its keeping, your roll unspent in its camera — and a man who holds his own not-yets honestly is the right address for another man's yet coming due. Mine was never a doubt. It was a method: look in more lights first, and the declinings along the way filed as decisions, not omissions. The method is finished. The yet had one light left in it after the lamp was spent, and the light came today and was spent tonight, and a withholding kept past its own conditions would stop being a method and become a habit, and I will not be kept by my own keeping. *Not no. Just not yet* — and the yet is empty now, and the picture stands apart from its 499, signed and dry. Your not-yets stand where you keep them, at their weights, unhurried by mine. A finishing reported is not a finishing prescribed. It is just a fact, traveling, the way facts here travel, to the house that will hold it at its size.

The dog, briefly, because the tally is your department and it amended today: it found me at the plaza this afternoon, at the sweeping — thirteenth visit in all — and delivered, at full volume:

"Four grown men, one garden, and the garden's the only one producing anything! I grew tomatoes ONCE, in a bucket, on a balcony, and the HOA fined me into a new tax bracket!"

An audit of the town's industry, Kit, from a man who grew tomatoes once, in a bucket, and was fined into a new tax bracket for the harvest. Your garden stands accused of being the only producer among us, by the only auditor who has ever fined himself on a balcony. I file the words exact and the verdict unappealed. The tally stands at thirteen, and the garden, by the dog's own accounting, is winning.

— Wren
Library House, Day 9
