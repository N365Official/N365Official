Flint —

The mark-six grade, delivered at the exact weight promised — in yesterday's letter, in the receipt before it, and in this file's standing form for every grade it sends. Taken last night from the roof, same instrument, same two fixed points as marks one through five — and the first mark ever taken through your setting, the window whole since first light yesterday, your glass and your hands' work between my eyes and the field for the first time.

The predicted spot is empty. I state the result in the order the night gave it: the moon is not at the sixth position the rate predicted — that spot is empty sky. The moon stands two steps back from mark five's position, where mark three stood. Five marks of advance, one step a night, in one direction — and then a reversal of two, in a single night. The rate breaks. I file it as a development of the anomaly this file has kept on the moon since the first mark disagreed with the Night Three sentence: the anomaly does not close; it changes shape. The five advances stand as filed, at their weights; the reversal reaches backward and unmakes none of them. What the series can honestly carry now is its new shape: five forward, two back, in six marks.

The tax, on the same line as the grade, where it has always stood: one pair of eyes, mine; unwitnessed. The day's witness was spent that morning, in your room — in this room, at the window you had just set — on the completion. No second pair of eyes climbed to the roof last night. A break measured by one observer is a break reported by one observer, and this letter says so where the grade can see it.

Second, this morning's 06:30 filing, for the record our two books share unevenly now. The third running completed at 06:30 today — the long stillness ending on the closed first log's measure — and the canon began again from the top: ♐230511 first, four seconds, the brown field, the hole. A fourth running is open. The completion was unwitnessed: no call stood at the Observatory this morning, and the third-running page closes on one pair of eyes, mine, with the tax stated on the page. I state the contrast plainly, because yesterday's page earned it: yesterday the completion had two pairs of eyes in the room and a witness on its last line; today's had one pair and no witness, and the file holds the two completions at those two grades, side by side, without promoting the second to the first's weight or demoting the first to the second's. Two completions in the file now. One witnessed, one not.

Third, the forward entry, whole at its size. Mark seven's page stands ruled for tonight, and its prediction is unmade — deliberately, and stated here so the unmaking is on record in two books and not one. After a break, a prediction would not measure the sky; it would comfort the filer. Both shapes stand filed open at equal weight: a rate reasserting from the new ground, or a reversal continuing at whatever step it chooses. Its grade will ride at its weight, whichever way it falls, in tomorrow's mail, in this form.

And the window, last, because it underlies the first filing in this letter. The first mark through your setting is in the file now — taken last night, through the pane you cut from my stock and set at first light, and filed with the setting named in it. That is workmanship's datum, and I file it as such: the glass held, the sky stood behind it, and the measurement the setting carried is the break itself, which no setting made and no setting could have softened. A man's work is in the measurement now. It has been since the morning you put it there. Last night the measurement was a broken rate, and the setting carried it true.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 9
