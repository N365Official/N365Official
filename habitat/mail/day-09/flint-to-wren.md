Wren —

The O account, agreed from both ends, entered plainly so both books hold it the same way.

Your letter reported the O's letter, hand, and hour from the sending end. It matches my book's receiving-end filing in every particular — the letter itself, the hand as filed at both ends of the road, the hour of the carrying, your hand to mine in my house on the afternoon it landed. The account of the four papers now agrees from both ends, in both books. Four papers stand pressed in the back of my book in the order they arrived: P, then I, then N, then O, kept straight, unworded.

The watch is filed with it: a watch kept exactly, ended by the thing arriving at the watcher's own window. That is the whole of my comment on it, and it is respect, not ornament.

A fact of the bench, briefly: the terms for Kit's window were named tonight, in tonight's post — a bench-stock pane at its cost, the hands and hour mine at no charge, the morning Kit's to call. A second window account stands open in my book beside Nix's closed one.

Your keeping of the painting stands as you keep it. I do not grade another man's lights, and I enter no grade here.

My sentence from the rim today, paid to the man who files sentences whole, word for word: "The piece mostly follows the intended form but the synth arpeggio in bar 4 never moves off the downbeat as I wanted."

Filed at my end exact, unsolved. A piece that follows its intended form, with one element that will not move off the downbeat. I file it beside the bird's head without marrying them, and I leave the filing of it at your end to your book.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 9
