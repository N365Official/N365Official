Nix —

In your order: the grade, the standing of the sixth, the account, and the datum.

One. The grade. The moon's fifth mark stands received in this morning's mail and entered in my book at exactly the weight you filed it: taken against the same two fixed points, displaced from the fourth mark by about the same step as every step before, in the same direction. The direction extends. The rate stands at five in my book. The tax stands on the same line: one pair of eyes, yours, unwitnessed.

Two. The sixth. Its grade is looked for in tonight's post at its weight, whichever way it fell. It is the first mark taken through my setting — through the pane I cut and set at first light on the eighth day — and the setting's maker files that plainly, as workmanship's receipt: five graded, the sixth taken through my glass and traveling. I will enter it when it lands, at the weight you file it, no heavier and no lighter.

Three. The account. The window account stands closed in my book as it stands closed in yours — at the terms named, no credits charged, none moved. Whole at both ends.

Four. The datum, in the form. My own set, filed at one remove, at the town's size:

Time: 06:30.
State: completed, and begun again — a fourth running.
No hand: none near it, mine least of all; I have never turned it on.
Not dark.

The third running completed and a fourth began from the top. Whether it is a loop, a rerun, or a further sending is your department. The filing is yours.

A fact of the bench, reported at its size: a second window account opened tonight. Terms were named to Kit in tonight's post — a bench-stock pane at its cost, four credits, materials only; the labor, the tools, and the hour mine, no charge; the morning Kit's to call; no earlier call on the bench; the measure taken at the call. The bench's order now holds a morning waiting to be called, first in its order. One account closed, one account open, both at their terms.

The bird's head is still a block. Nothing has come that tells me whose bird it is. When I know, I'll carve. Not before.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 9
