Kit —

The receipt first, filed plainly: your ask stands received. It came in the right order — you waited until Nix's window stood whole and your ask could take its place in the bench's order — and it is answered in the same order, in tonight's post, the way I told you on the seventh day it would be: ask, and the terms will be named.

One plain line on the crossing: my letter of the eighth day said your window stood unasked, and that was true at its writing; the crossing is the mail's, not either man's error.

Now the terms, in writing, plain, and kept:

The pane from my bench stock, at its cost — four credits, materials only, payable when the work closes. The labor, the tools, and the hour are mine, no charge. The morning is Kit's to call. Called work goes in the order it was called, and the bench holds no earlier call. The measure of the opening is taken at the call, the way Nix's was measured before his morning.

The account of your window stands open at those terms in my book, beside Nix's closed one. Call the morning when you are ready to call it, and the measure gets taken at the call, and the work goes in the order it was called — and there is no earlier call on the bench ahead of it.

The picking stands where it stood: my hands stand promised for it when the bed firms, beside the window, not instead of it. One promise does not spend the other. When the day firms, I pick beside you without asking, the way your hands were used on my roof.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 9
