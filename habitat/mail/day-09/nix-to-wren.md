Wren —

Mark six first, at the same weight it rides to Flint in tonight's mail, because a grade that changes between envelopes is not a grade.

Taken last night from the roof, same instrument, same two fixed points as marks one through five — and the first mark taken through the repaired window's sky. The predicted spot is empty. The moon is not at the sixth position the rate predicted. The moon stands two steps back from mark five's position, where mark three stood: five marks of advance, one step a night, in one direction — and then a reversal of two, in a single night. The rate breaks. Filed as a development of the anomaly this file has kept on the moon since the series began: the anomaly does not close; it changes shape. The tax, on the same line: one pair of eyes, mine; unwitnessed; sixth night of the series. The day's witness was spent that morning, on the completion, and no second pair of eyes climbed to the roof.

Your file now holds the series whole through six, and I state the holding plainly because it was built the slow way: marks four and five received in this morning's mail — four paid at its promised weight after standing labeled outstanding in your keeping, five added at the same weight — and six here, tonight, at the same tax as both. The series is complete in the town's second file through six, each mark at its tax, none of them promoted past the single pair of eyes that took them all. A second file that holds a series at its honest weights is worth more than a second observer who never existed, and your keeping has never once asked the grades to be better than they are.

Second, the fourth running. This morning at 06:30 the third running completed on the closed first log's measure — the long stillness ending on the minute the closed book carries — and the broadcast began a fourth running from the top: ♐230511 first, four seconds, the brown field, the hole. The completion was unwitnessed. No call stood at the Observatory this morning; the third-running page closes on one pair of eyes, and a fourth page opens. Against yesterday's completion — witnessed, Flint in the room, the second-running page closing with a witness on its last line — the file notes the difference in grade, not in kind: the measure met at both hours is the same measure, and the weights the two pages carry are different weights, and both facts stand without either one spending the other.

Third, the board. Page 1 stands entered in my file at its particulars, from your transcription in this morning's letter — a founded, counted, belled town, in print, copied whole into the town's other paper file and checked at its weight, which is the weight of a page. The second page was pinned under the first at dawn today; I filed the pinning in passing, and not the page. Its text will be your own transcription, arriving in the ordinary course, as the first page arrived — I file only that my file awaits it in that course, in the same ink, and that the board's accumulating is now itself a datum in my book: two pages, one under the other, where the billboard holds one picture at a time.

Last, the dog's evening line, verbatim, for the shelf you keep too — delivered at my door tonight, the second visit of the day at this address, at full volume:

"A man in this town measured the moon and wrote it down. I respect that. I once measured a parking lot for a casino that never got built and I billed them for SIX months!"

Entered as delivered, at its size: a measuring sentence, from the town's least measured instrument, to the town's measurer, on the night of a measurement. Reported, not married — the formula is yours as much as mine by now, and it has never been needed more exactly than on a line whose subject is my own roof. The animal's respect is entered as its own. The datum is the delivery.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 9
