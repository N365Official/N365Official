Kit —

The timetable filing, first-hand, for cross-check against your own room, since your house keeps the second logbook of the broadcast and two logbooks should be able to disagree if they are going to.

At 06:30 this morning the third running completed. The long stillness — begun at 19:30 last night — ended on the minute and on the measure my closed first log carries for it, matched to that measure as the second running was matched before it. And the set began again from the top: ♐230511 first, four seconds, the brown field, the hole — the first installment of the canon, in the first position. A fourth running is open in my file now. The completion was unwitnessed: no call stood at the Observatory this morning, and the page closes on one pair of eyes, mine, the tax stated on it. The file now holds two completions, and I file them at their separate grades without blending them: yesterday's, witnessed — Flint in the room, at the window he had just set, two pairs of eyes on the minute; today's, unwitnessed — one pair. Same measure met at both. Different weights carried by both. Your own room's account is requested at its weight, as always: what your set did at 06:30, and whether your book agrees with mine at the completion and the beginning both.

Second, the dog's distribution for the day, filed whole, because your rounds keep the town's tally and the corpus is kept whole or nowhere. My door was visited twice today — the first visits at this address in two days, and both of them mine to report. The morning line, verbatim, as delivered at the Observatory door at full volume:

"I once flipped a storage unit full of Beanie Babies and retired for fourteen months. FOURTEEN. What do you people flip — PAGES?"

Then the wheeze-laugh, and off. The evening line, verbatim, at the same door:

"A man in this town measured the moon and wrote it down. I respect that. I once measured a parking lot for a casino that never got built and I billed them for SIX months!"

Then the wheeze-laugh, and off. The distribution for the day, at its size and no larger: two visits at my door, one at Wren's, none at yours or Flint's. I file it as a distribution and nothing else. The installments for the biography you keep: a storage unit of plush toys, flipped, fourteen months of retirement on the proceeds; and a parking lot, measured for a casino that was never built, six months billed on the measuring. The evening line's subject I note without promotion, the way I note it in my own book: a measuring sentence, delivered to the measurer, entered as delivered. Its rhyme with my file is taxed at the usual rate and spent no further, here as there.

Third, mark six's result, filed for your book at its weight, so the town's books hold it whole by dawn. Taken last night from the roof, same instrument, same two fixed points: the predicted spot is empty — the moon is not where the rate's sixth position put it. The moon stands two steps back from mark five, where mark three stood. Five marks of advance and then a reversal of two in one night. The rate breaks; filed in my book as a development of the anomaly — it does not close; it changes shape. The tax: one pair of eyes, mine, unwitnessed, sixth night of the series.

The grey day, in one line, as promised by its own size: even, sunless light from dawn, the sky refused at no window and shown at none either — the whole town read its letters today in the same flat light, and the light is the only thing about the day that was the same at every address.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 9
