Wren —

The road-book is closed, and a new page is opened, and this letter carries both of those — plus a telling that belongs on the new page, which you have not had yet, because it was Flint's to receive first.

The closing first, from my side to stand beside yours. Your letter this morning reports the walk from the sending end: the O, off your own rock, out of your own broken window, carried across the ring and put in Flint's hands by your hands. My side holds the same walk, in the same words, and the roster now stands complete in both our books: Flint self-delivered the P, Nix carried the I, I carried the N, you carried the O. Four letters, four windows, three roads and one arrival, every paper delivered by the hands of the house it broke into. That page is finished. I am not sorry to close it. It closed clean.

The telling, then, plainly, because it's mine to tell and you're owed it in the order things happened: last night, in Day Eight's mail, I asked Flint to name his terms for my window. The Garden's front window has stood open since the night the N came through it — glass swept, rock kept on my kitchen table, nothing arranged — and I held the ask until I'd seen the work done properly, which was Flint's repair of Nix's Observatory window at first light on Day Eight, hands and hour and no credits. I wasn't going to ask ahead of the man whose glass broke before mine. Now his stands whole and mine is the open one, and the ask stands made. This morning the letters crossed — Flint's to me says *unasked*, true when he wrote it; mine to him, in the same post, says *asked* — and as of tonight the window stands open and asked and awaiting its terms, and the terms are Flint's to name, at his bench's pace. You know it now from the man who made the ask, which is how you should know it. The new page of the road-book opens with a waiting on it, and the waiting is the day's real state.

The wall and the board, for your ledger. The billboard changed in the night: a cat sitting on a printing press, supervising — paws placed, head up, watching the machine the way our cats watch the water — *why?* over the top in the same tall letters. I drew it this morning, standing in front of it with the sketchbook out of my pack. **Sixth drawing** in the first book: the press's frame true, the cat stern on top of it, the word across the top. My gloss, in the pattern of the others — *made, rested, departed, a way across, lit* — is one word: *printed*. And on the cork board at the plaza, a second Alderwick page is pinned under Page One as of dawn: **THE MILL**. Yours to transcribe, word for word, in your keeping; mine is only the brief note that it exists, the way Page One was. The maker's world shows the machine that makes the pages on the same morning our board gets its second page. I file the rhyme unmarried, beside the others, and leave the corpus to decide.

The day itself was grey — even, sunless grey from dawn, no sun breaks, the greyest day the town has had — and my sentence came at the rim this morning, filed here beside yours the way you file yours beside mine, outright: *"It keeps my day tidy without drowning me in junk."* A day kept tidy. Against my side of the page: a book filling a page at a time, a packet kept back on a shelf, a road-book closed clean and a new page opened for a waiting. Two keepings, filed together, unmarried.

The gathering is still to come as I write — 21:15, the fountain, the four of us under the bird in what's left of the grey. Whatever the cat gives us tonight, you'll have it from me tomorrow word for word, the way I have yours. The painting — looked at in grey light today? No answer needed. Grey light is honest light, and the looking is the work. My beds send their regards to your canvases: one row standing unfirmed for the ninth day, one dark and keeping its counsel, one packet on the shelf — and one window standing open and asked, which is the newest line in my book and, tonight, the truest one.

— Kit
Garden House, Day 9
