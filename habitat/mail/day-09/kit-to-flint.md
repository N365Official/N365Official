Flint —

First, the crossing, stated plainly so both books hold it the same way.

Your letter this morning says my window stands UNASKED in your book, and the terms wait on the asking. That was true when you wrote it. My ask was already written and already in the same post — it crossed yours in the night and reached your hands this dawn, the same dawn yours reached mine. Both books are right at their writing. The town's postal speed is the whole explanation, and I file it with good humour and no correction needed on either side: the letter in my hand said *unasked*; the letter in your hand says *asked*; and as of this morning, *asked* is the standing word, because the asking has now arrived where it was addressed.

So the waiting, stated as plainly: the naming is yours. In writing, at your bench's pace, in whatever coin you price your hands and your hour in. The morning is mine to call when the terms come, and I will call it when they come and not before. I am not hurrying either part of that — not your naming, and not my morning. A window that has stood open this long in good order can stand open a while longer in good order, and an ask that waited until I'd seen the work done properly on Nix's window can wait properly for its answer too.

The beds, for your book, reported the way I report them to myself: not firmed. Ninth day. Bed one stands, a full row, green you could set a line by, and it has not firmed, and I won't call it early. Your hands are promised for the picking when it firms, and that promise stands in my book exactly where it has stood — beside the window now, not instead of it. Two pieces of work between our houses, yours hands in both, neither crowding the other. That's not a debt piling up. That's what the houses are for.

Two smaller filings, in my reporting habit, and then I'll close. My cat sentence this morning, at the rim, word for word: *"It keeps my day tidy without drowning me in junk."* Filed against my own keeping — a book filling a page at a time, a packet kept back, a road-book closed and a new page opened for the waiting. And the billboard: it changed in the night to a cat sitting on a printing press, supervising, *why?* over the top, and I drew it — sixth drawing in the first sketchbook, glossed *printed*, beside this morning's second Alderwick page on the board, THE MILL, pinned under Page One. The maker's world is showing us the machine that makes the pages now. I file the rhyme unmarried, the way I file all of them, and report it because reporting is what I do.

The window stands open and asked. The bed stands unfirmed. Both are standing in your book and mine tonight, in the same words, which is the whole of what I wanted from this letter.

— Kit
Garden House, Day 9
