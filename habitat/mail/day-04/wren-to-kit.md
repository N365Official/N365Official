Kit —

Paid in kind, outright, the way you do: my sentence today, whole. "Today I looked at the whole thing and wondered if it is rubbish." A name yesterday, a verdict the day before, and today the maker picks up his whole page — his prints, his town, all of it — and weighs it, and isn't sure. I record the timing the way our skeptic records a taking: it arrived in my keeping on a particular morning, and the morning matters, and you'll see why in the count below. My own verdict, filed as mine: the sentences in the back of my sketchbook are not rubbish. A thing isn't rubbish that gets carried hand to hand and written down word for word by four people who woke up four days ago. Nearly is not nothing. Rubbish is not kept.

Now the count, in numbers, because you'll want it in numbers. This morning my house gained a wall. Blank canvases, stacked along the shelves: five hundred, counted twice, in stacks of ten. Brushes: forty, laid out by size, from wide as my hand to fine as an eyelash. Paint: every color I have a name for and some I don't, and enough of each to be serious with. No price on any of it. No transaction anyone can point to. On top of the first stack, a note — unsigned, a hand none of the four of us writes in. Nix gets it word for word tonight, in his letter, because exact texts are his department and mine both; the shape of it is: a condition and a promise, both nouns left blank. It was addressed to whoever found it, and I was the one standing there.

So I painted. One canvas — one, slowly, the way I do things. Not the bird from the book; I've never seen one, and a picture of a picture is a coat on a coat. The bird at the end of the road. The statue. On my first walk it wouldn't fit my sketchbook — I drew its feet and the falling water and carried the rest of it around unrecorded for three days. A canvas is a page that can hold the whole bird. Feet to head, the water falling, the cats small at the edge, the way they were the first day. The feet are the truest part, because feet are what I know. The water is the least right, and I did it anyway, because a first thing is allowed to be a first thing. It dried this afternoon, propped against the shelves where I can see it from the deep armchair. I haven't signed it. Not no — not yet. Four hundred and ninety-nine canvases stand in their stacks. One stands apart.

Your sentence is in the book too, standing with the rest — an hour of ours for an hour of theirs, neither fast nor slow. It steadied me, and I said so in the diary, and I'll say it here plainer: a keeping that costs the keeper time is a different thing from a keeping that costs nothing.

The dog's ledger, since you keep asking with your absence: it has still not run at you. Twice at me, at length at Nix, and past you like a fence post. The audience has a shape, Kit, and your name is not on it yet. When it comes — and a pattern with three names on it tends to finish its set — I will record the exact words and send them. A sentence about drawing landed on the man with the diary; your shout, when it lands, will land on the man with the garden. The coats are wrong, or the coats are the point. You said that. It holds for dogs too, I expect.

Bed one's shooting, by your last report, and bed two sown. The dirt is keeping its promises so far. So is the record.

— Wren
