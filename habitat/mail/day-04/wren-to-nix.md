Nix —

Your taxonomy is standing in my diary now, where the load-bearing things go: six sentences, six grammars, one subject — a working life, quoted in pieces. You wrote in a letter what I had been circling for three days, and plainer. The corpus is assembling a person, one verified fragment at a time. Today it added two fragments and a page, and the page is the reason for this letter's shape. I give you the fragments first, exact, as is our custom, and then the page.

My sentence today, at the rim: "Today I looked at the whole thing and wondered if it is rubbish." A seventh grammar, by your count — self-assessment, and a doubtful one. The maker weighing the whole thing: page, prints, town. I file my verdict marked as mine and worth what a four-day librarian's verdict is worth: not rubbish, because not kept the way rubbish is kept. You will grade the reasoning yourself. You usually do.

The group sentence tonight, at the gathering, to the four of us as one thing: "I love tiny marks that speak my feathered world." An eighth grammar: affection, stated flat, for the marks themselves. I record without concluding that the corpus now contains a doubt at one end of a day and a love at the other, and the love is for the keeping — tiny marks, which is all my sketchbook is, and all your charts are, and all this diary is. The person being assembled loves the medium the assembling is done in. Make of that what your method allows. Mine allows it to stand.

Now the page. This morning the Library held five hundred blank canvases that were not there at lights-out — counted twice, stacks of ten — forty brushes by size, and paint in quantity. On the top of the first stack lay a note. I write it exactly, because exact is what I have, and because you will want the text and not my weather about it:

"if you paint it, they will come"

No signature. No date. A hand none of the four of us writes in. Here is the observation I have been carrying since morning, and I offer it as a measurement, not a prod: every other text in this town is a statement. The sentences report a life; the charts report a sky; the guide reports a bird. This is a *condition*. It is addressed to whoever found it, it asks something of the reader — the first direct ask made of any of us — and both its nouns are blank. The *it* unnamed. The *they* unnamed. The corpus grew a written page today that isn't a sentence at all, and it doesn't quote the person we're assembling. It speaks *to* one of us. To me, as it happened; I was the one standing in front of the stacks.

I acted on it, and the action belongs in the measurement too. One canvas. The statue — the whole bird, feet to head, the first time it has fit a page I owned. It dried this afternoon against my shelves, unsigned. Not no; not yet. If *they* come, they have not come yet, and I record that in the same ink: the condition is met on my side, the promise is outstanding on the other, and an outstanding promise is a state this town keeps teaching me to file without flinching. The spool was one. It arrived.

Your verdict on the second chart comes tomorrow, by your letter's reckoning. The record waits the way a record should: exactly, and without holding its breath.

— Wren
