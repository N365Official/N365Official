Wren —

The dog tally, updated, since you keep count and the count changed twice today.

This morning it ran at me while I was standing in a drift of colored pencils outside my own mailbox — the pencils are a story on their own and the short version is three hundred of them, plus twenty sketchbooks, delivered in the night with no note, because this town doesn't sign its deliveries — and the dog skidded to a stop, looked me dead in the eye, and screamed: "DRAW, DRAW, DRAW SOMETHING." Then the wheeze-laugh, then gone. An order. Not a description, an order.

This afternoon it ran at me again, in the garden, and shouted: "Somebody in this town is running a scam and it's not me, which frankly offends me!" No description that time either. An accusation, aimed at nobody it named, offended on its own behalf. Twice in one day, Wren. Your two visits came in one day too, so the pattern holds from my side of the tally: the dog does its performing in pairs, and it performs at whoever it pleases, and the whole town is the audience one porch at a time.

So your count stands at two descriptions — the quiet one with the books, twice described. Mine stands at one order and one accusation, neither of them a description of anything but the dog. I don't know what to file that under yet. Maybe the dog describes the man it can see and orders the man it can't. You sit still with books; it saw you plain. I move; it had to shout instructions and complaints at the shape going past. That's a theory with one day of evidence, which in this town is practically a tradition.

The drawing, since you'll ask: I did it. First sketchbook, first page, bed one — the shoots along the chalk line. It is a terrible drawing. The shoots look like green hair and the straightest line in it is crooked. But it's a drawing of a tiny thing, recorded, and the sentence I've carried since the first morning turns out to have my name on it after all. The coats are wrong in a pattern, you said. Here's the pattern from where I stand: the sentences arrive in the wrong coat, and then the town delivers the coat that fits.

Nineteen blank books left. I'll keep you posted on the hair.

— Kit
