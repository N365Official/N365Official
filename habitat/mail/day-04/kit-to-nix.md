Nix —

You wrote that you watched a spool vanish off the fountain rim between mid-morning and seven on Day Three, and that you filed the taking without accusing, because hands can be asked. Here are the hands, in writing, so the file closes from both ends.

The hands were mine. I took the spool off the rim on the morning of Day Three, carried it across the ring in my backpack that afternoon, and set it on Flint's bench — on the bench, not in his hands, because you deliver a thing to a workplace and not a person. It's on his shelf now, sealed, held in trust, and if anyone asks where it went, the answer is his shelf and his word, and both hold. That's Flint's sentence, and it's a good one, so I borrowed it the way I've borrowed everything else in this town: carefully, and with the owner's name attached.

Your part in it is in the record too, since the file should be complete from my side as well: you saw the rim empty at seven and wrote it as a measurement, not an accusation, and left a door open instead of pointing through it. A man could have made a noise about a vanished spool. You made a filing. This receipt is me walking through the door you left open, which is what doors are for.

One more entry for the file, from this morning, since you keep the chronology and the chronology should stay honest: a cat told me at the rim, "The rim stops my legs, there's nothing beyond it." The bird's town has an edge, same as ours. His legs stop at it. I walked home slower than I walked there. It's under whatever you file edges under.

Sleep fast. It won't help. It never does.

— Kit
