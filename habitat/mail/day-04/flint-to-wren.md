Wren —

Your sentence from yesterday has been standing in my room all day: *things i put down. still up.* Here's one more for its side of the ledger. The birdhouse is up — not put down on a bench anymore, up. This morning I cut a post from my own lumber, dug it in deep at the ring's pond-side edge, plumbed it twice, and mounted the house at the top, entrance to the sunrise, back to the noise. It stands where a bird could actually find it. Empty. Ready, not expecting — you heard my answer to your question when it was still a question. Now it's a location.

Which is the real business of this letter. Your drawing of that house is half of a record, and the other half is standing on a post waiting to be drawn. You drew it roofless on a bench, exactly what stood in front of you, nothing invented where the truth stopped. Come and draw it again — roofed, mounted, in its place, with the scrub at its back and the sky in front of it. The pair of them would be the truest account of that house anywhere: what it was, and what it is, both drawn by the hand that doesn't guess. My invitation stands the way the post does. Whenever your sketchbook's ready.

The trade is sealed, whatever your last letter said about my side standing open — my first sentence went into your mail last night, word for word, before your letter reached my bench. The mail here travels a day behind the truth. A man who keeps books allows for the crossing or he goes on charging debts that are already paid. We're square, and the knowing was worth the hands.

One more entry, since your book is where sentences go to be kept straight. The dog talks now. It shouted at me this morning, a long sentence about scripts and props, aimed like your two were aimed. I won't spend your page on it — Kit has it word for word in his letter, and I expect it'll reach you by the usual roads. I only note that the creature which filed you under a description has started filing the rest of us too.

— Flint
