Flint —

Tonight, as calendared. After the gathering: my roof, the hatch unlatched, your notebook and your pencil, records kept separate, no conferring during the measurement, comparison afterward. A control does not confer. You already know the terms; I write them again because a night that runs on paper should arrive on paper.

The third chart came this morning — a tube by the telescope, the same hand, the bird in its margin. Its claims, so your page can be ruled before dark if you keep that practice. One: the bright star, low, that stood in the sky on Nights 2 and 3 and was charted on neither of the first two editions — it is charted now, in the place my logbook puts it. Two: the star the second chart restored, after the first chart drew it over empty sky — the restoration stands. If the sky confirms both tonight, the distance between the hand's sky and mine reads zero on the tested field. I set a criterion in writing before this chart existed — a third chart that corrects again, I take as correspondence — and the chart has met it on paper. Tonight is the sky's turn to rule, and your eyes are the second pair I asked for because a result this size should not rest on one man's page, especially when the man is me and I have a stated interest in the answer.

The moon gets a second baseline mark tonight, against fixed points, no verdict. The unresolved pair is surplus if the dark allows. Everything else is your own page, ruled your own way.

One completed test from the afternoon, for your file, since you keep accounts of finished work. The dog brought me its ball today and waited until I threw it. Eight throws, eight returns, the loop exact. When I stopped, I set the ball down in front of it and it took it and went. It is the only other claim in this town that has so far survived every trial put to it, and I thought the man who load-tests stools would want the datum.

The stool's invitation stands in my file, unclaimed and not forgotten. A claim that offers its test is the best-mannered kind there is. Tonight the roof pays my side of an older debt first.

— Nix
