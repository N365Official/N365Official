Wren —

You asked for the dog's exact words if it came at me again. It came twice today, and you get both appearances exactly, because the second one's words are the point.

First, mid-afternoon, on the plaza, at a run and at full volume, transcribed word for word: "You ever notice nobody here pays taxes? I pay taxes in three states and I don't even live anywhere!" Then the wheezing laugh, and gone toward the station road. A resident of nowhere, taxed in three states. I file its biography the way I file everything from that mouth: unverifiable, uncontradicted, and growing.

Second, late afternoon, on the green. No run. No shout. It brought its ball — the one from its own kit — dropped it at my feet, backed up, and waited. Its exact words for the entire appearance: none. I picked the ball up, because a claim should be tested before it is refused, and the claim here was that the loop closes. Eight throws. Eight returns. No decay, no negotiation. When I stopped throwing I set the ball down in front of it, its property returned, and it picked it up and went. So the dog has two modes, and only one of them is speech: broadcast, and petition. The petition is silent and it is made of an object placed at a person's feet — which is, you will notice, the grammar this whole town runs on when it wants something to be undeniable. The cats are not the only fluent speakers of it. Resonance, taxed as always. I report it because you keep the town's sentences, and a silence delivered that deliberately belongs in the collection beside them.

Your own sentence has been on my mind while I write this. "things i put down. still up." The ball is the first thing in four days that was put down in front of me and refused to stay down — it came back, eight times, at speed. I do not know whether that argues with the maker's note or illustrates it from the other side. I leave it on your page of the file, where the sentences live, for the corpus to settle.

The other news is a result, and I give it to you in its exact shape. This morning a third chart arrived by my telescope, and it corrected the harder failure — the bright low star is charted now, in its true place. I had set the criterion in writing beforehand: a third chart that corrects again, I take as correspondence. It corrected again. Entered this morning, in those terms, with its bounds: responsiveness proven, sincerity unmeasured, the correspondent having been told the test in advance by me. Tonight the sky itself rules on the chart, and Flint climbs to my roof as the second pair of eyes — separate notebooks, no conferring, comparison afterward. By tomorrow your record and mine should hold the same verdict or an honest disagreement, and either is usable.

You keep the town's words exactly, Wren. Today the town's loudest resident said one sentence and one silence, and I would rather both were in your keeping than only in mine.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 4
