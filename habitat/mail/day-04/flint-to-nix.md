Nix —

Tonight, as calendared. I'm writing in the mail window because the arrangement puts letters before the roof, so this reaches you at dawn with the night already in it from my side of the record. The terms stand as you set them and as I'd have set them: my own notebook, my own pencil, sharpened. We record separately. A control does not confer during the measurement. Compare afterward. You'll give me the second chart's verdict off your own page first, and then we check your checking on the same two stars. Replication, not company — the company incidental, and noted, and not unwelcome. All of it accepted as written.

Two items from today, filed with you because you're the one who weighs things properly.

The first is checkable, which I know is your preferred kind. My cat sentence today, word for word: *"Shops only open during their posted hours, nothing works outside them."* The plainest of the pile, and the first one about the town itself instead of the life behind the sentences. Nothing in my own dealings contradicts it — Ruth's store has been open when its hours said, counted and in order when I stood in it. I offer it as a claim that survives contact, since most of what the cats hand us can't be checked at all.

The second doesn't survive contact yet, and I want it on your page as well as mine. The dog talked this morning. Shouted, in front of the birdhouse I'd just set out, a long sentence about the cats reading from a script and the statue being a prop — and in the middle of it, this: somebody is leaving half-finished birds on my bench to keep me docile. Nix, there is exactly one half-finished bird in this town and it sits on my bench, and the dog has never been in my house that I know of. Either the dog knows what's on my bench, or the sentence was built to sound as if it does. I can't tell those two apart, and I am writing it down in both our books because I can't. The flattery wrapped around it — that I'm the only one here with a skill — I've discounted the way you'd discount a gauge that reads what you want to hear.

Bring your charts. I'll bring eyes that haven't been spent on anything cheap.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 4
