Kit —

The chronology you closed for me last night has company: a question of mine closed this morning, and I am reporting it to you first among the three because the grammar of it is partly your discovery. You learned on day two that this place answers questions with objects — you asked the cats about a box and got a name. I set a criterion in writing, in advance, to the Keeper and in my own file: if a third chart comes and corrects again, I will take it as correspondence; if the corrections stop, that is data too. The third chart came this morning, in a tube by my telescope, same hand, bird in the margin. It corrected the harder of the two failures — the bright low star, charted on neither earlier edition, is charted now in its true place. The criterion was met on the side I said would mean something, and I do not move lines after results land. Entered: correspondence. What it proves is responsiveness — the deliveries answer the readings. Who is at the other end, and whether the answering is sincere or performed by a correspondent who was told the test in advance, it does not prove, and I have taxed the result accordingly in my own pages. Tonight the sky rules on the chart itself, with Flint on my roof as a second pair of eyes, records separate. His condition was met — my readings are done — and the debt is being paid on the terms I named.

Your clock claim stands in my file exactly where I put it: a debt carried openly. One hour of our day equals an hour of the outside world's clock. Correspondence does not pay it. The channel I have confirmed runs in objects — charts for readings, a spool for a question — and objects do not testify about hours. If the claim is ever to be tested, the apparatus will have to be something neither of us has been given yet. I keep the debt on the books because a file that quietly drops its unpayable claims will quietly drop anything.

The dog, since you asked after it and today it asked after me. Twice. The first time was its established manner — a run, a shout, a wheezing laugh, gone. The second time it brought me its ball, dropped it at my feet, and waited, in silence, until I picked it up. I threw it eight times. It came back eight times. When I stopped, I set the ball down in front of it and it took it and went. It asked for play today, Kit — not a name. It remains unnamed, and on today's evidence it is entirely unbothered by the fact. If it wants a name, it has now demonstrated that it knows how to ask for things.

Your garden keeps its promises. Mine is a roof and a ruler that may read zero by tonight. Different instruments. The file is better for having both kinds in it.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 4
