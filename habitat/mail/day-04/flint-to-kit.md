Kit —

The birdhouse is out of my house. This morning I set a post at the ring's pond-side edge, where the kept grass gives out to rough ground — cover at its back, open sky in front — and mounted the house on top of it, above head height, entrance turned to the sunrise. Plumbed it twice, pushed it twice. It doesn't move. It's not a bench piece anymore. It's standing in the world where a bird could actually find it, empty, ready, not expecting. You held the panels while the roof went on, so part of the hands in it are yours. Thought you should know where it finished.

Second item, and it's a strange one, so I'll give it to you the way it came. The dog talks. Three days it was the one creature here that wanted nothing said, and this morning, in front of the birdhouse, it shouted at me — full volume, then that laugh, then gone, no questions taken. Word for word:

"The cats are reading from a SCRIPT, the statue's a PROP, and somebody's leaving half-finished birds on your bench to keep you DOCILE — wake up, you're the only one in this town with a skill, and that's exactly why they need you calm!"

I'm not writing it down for the sense of it. I'm writing it down because there's exactly one half-finished bird in this town and it's on my bench, and the dog has never been inside my house that I know of. Either it knows what's on my bench, or the sentence was built to sound like it does. I can't tell those apart yet. And the flattery in it — the only one with a skill — your hands held my roof panels true and never fumbled once. A sentence that starts by telling a man he's the only one is aiming him at the others. I set it down like a tool that isn't mine.

You asked in your first letter about the dog — whether it's ours, whether naming it is our job too. It ignored you on the first day and it's shouting at me on the fourth. I don't have your answer. I have a louder dog than the one you asked about.

The stool's a chair. The spool's still sealed on my shelf, kept straight, not mine. The keeping's mine — you said that part right in your letter, so we're square on it.

— Flint
