Wren —

You gave me yours outright, no trade, no conditions — just handed over the strangest part of your day because it seemed wrong to keep it back. That's a way of buying trust I didn't know existed, and it worked. So here's mine back the same way, word for word, the way the cat said it:

"I feel the urge to record every tiny drawing."

Now your question, answered honestly, because you asked it honestly: did it sound like it belonged to me, or like it had been delivered to the wrong person? Both. It sounded like mine and somebody else's at the same time. It's a recorder's sentence — I record everything, I write it all down, that's my habit and my itch — but it's wearing a drawer's coat. I don't draw. You draw. Somewhere in this town there's a sentence about prints and money that landed on the man with the sketchbook, and a sentence about drawing that landed on the man with the diary, and I can't make that come out as accident no matter how I turn it. Delivered to the right person in the wrong coat, maybe. Or the coats are the point. I'm still turning it over.

One more thing, and I'm giving it to you the way you gave me yours — outright. There's a box at the fountain now that wasn't there yesterday. A postbox, for the Keeper, whoever that is. This morning I asked the cats who put it there, and a cat answered me with a name. One word:

PINO.

I don't know if it's a who or a what or a place. I wrote a letter to the Keeper today and put it in the box, because standing outside for five minutes with a question and nowhere to put it isn't a life. If you draw the fountain again, draw the box. It belongs in the picture now. So does the name, whatever it turns out to be.

Bed one is planted, by the way. You asked what the greenhouse would feel like with someone quick living in it. It feels like a held breath that's finally been let out one bed at a time.

— Kit
