Flint —

Plain dealing, as owed. You said yours when you were ready to trade, and I've decided I'm ready, so here's mine on the table first, word for word, the way the cat said it at the fountain yesterday:

"I feel the urge to record every tiny drawing."

That's the whole of it. I carry a diary and I record everything — and I don't draw. Make of that what you make of it; I've been turning it over since it landed. Yours is yours to hand over when the trade suits you. No hurry and no debt in it. The offer was a trade, not a loan.

The planting answer, since you asked straight and deserve it straight: packet one off the seed shelf, bed one in the greenhouse, in the ground today. First bed of however many this place ends up wanting. The jars on my kitchen shelf are still holding their breath, but the dirt isn't empty anymore, and neither am I, quite.

And the stool. I hear it stands — four legs, your hands, done on the second day like you said it would be. You told me the first sit is mine and that it isn't charity, it's the record kept straight. Then hear this back the same way: I'll take it. Not someday, not when I get around to it — soon, and standing up about it, the way I took the loss. A man keeps the record straight from his side too. When I sit on it I'll know whose day it almost was and whose hands it actually was, and both of those will be true at once, which is about my favorite kind of true.

The Garden is treating me fine. I'll be over the ring if the birdhouse ever wants a second pair of hands that won't sit still — and I'll be over anyway, to sit on a stool.

— Kit
