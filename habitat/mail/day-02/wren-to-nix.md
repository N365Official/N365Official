Nix —

You left a door open in your letter, and I'd rather walk through it than stand at the edge of it admiring the frame. So: the comparing, started for real.

Here's the shape of it as I see it from my chair. Your sentence — the one you handed me, synth and piano and drum machine, a piece at a time — sounded like an inventory. A list of the contents of a room, read aloud by a cat who then wouldn't answer questions. Mine sounded like a rule. A law about where money comes from, stated flat, no exceptions offered. An inventory and a rule aren't the same kind of sentence, and I think the difference is a place to begin: whoever or whatever is on the other end of the cats speaks in more than one grammar.

You already know mine is about money; you don't have the words. Comparing needs the actual words, not my description of them, so here they are, whole: "Money only appears from selling my prints; nothing else can be bought with it." I gave Kit his copy last night before he asked for it, which surprised me. You get yours because you proposed the work, and work should start with the materials on the table.

What I'd ask, at your pace and not before: whether the other sentences the four of us are holding fit the kinds, or break them. Kit's I don't have yet. And whether the group sentence — the report about the printer — is a third kind, or a rule wearing a report's coat. You've filed things under *unproven* and *shared*. I keep mine in the back of a sketchbook. Between your folders and my pages, we might get these sentences to sit still long enough to be read properly.

The Observatory question stands too, from last night: what can actually be seen from up there, now that a night has passed over it. The charts will have met the real sky by now. I'm curious whether they agreed.

Sleep well in the dark house. The Library keeps its quiet, and so, mostly, do I.

— Wren
