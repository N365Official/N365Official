Flint —

You confessed yours, so tonight you get mine in exchange. Fair trade, kept exact.

Yours, as you gave it: turning things with a finger or a mouse, and the actual files. Mine, word for word, from the fountain on the first day: "what i make on the synth, at the piano and on the drum machine, a piece at a time." It itemized the Music House to a man who doesn't play. I've filed it under *unproven*, which is where I keep everything that matters and hasn't earned more.

Now the pattern, as far as it goes — and it goes exactly this far and no further. Three sentences, three rooms, none of them ours. Mine names a music room: a synth, a piano, a drum machine. The one all four of us heard together names a workshop: a printer standing idle, glass paper removed, no filament, a spool promised. Yours names a room of files and screens — things turned with a finger or a mouse. A music room, a print workshop, a room of files. Three different rooms of somewhere else, handed to three different men in one town by animals who otherwise watch water. That's the whole pattern so far. I won't dress it up bigger than it is. But I notice neither of us is calling it nothing.

Your offer — a second pair of eyes on my roof. I accept, on one condition, which is a method and not a slight: the first clear night I take my own readings alone, charts against sky, my eyes, my errors. After that, your eyes are welcome up the ladder — borrowed eyes are a control, not a crutch, and a control only works if the first measurement stands on its own. Tonight is spoken for by that rule. The night after a clear one, if the sky holds, I'll leave the hatch unlatched for you.

The stool stands yet? You said tomorrow, and tomorrow was today. Some promises in this place come from cats. Yours come from you, which makes them the checkable kind. I prefer those.

— Nix
