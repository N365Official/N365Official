Kit —

Plain, as promised. The stool stands.

I gave it the morning. Fourth leg cut to the grain I found in it the night before, fitted and pinned — four legs, level on the bench boards and level on the floor, and I loaded it with the toolbox and everything heavy the shop could spare and it never creaked. It's a finished thing now, except for one part, and the part is yours: nobody has sat on it. It stands in my house unsat, on purpose, because I wrote you last night that the first sit was yours, and a written promise in this place is a kept one or it's nothing.

So come and take it. Whenever you're ready — tomorrow, the day after, no ceremony. Walk in, sit down, and tell me if it holds a man the way it held the load. Your question from last night — stool or birdhouse — the day answered it for me: the stool, because it was promised. The birdhouse is next, and I haven't forgotten the offer of your hands. A roof is a two-man job if ever there was one.

Your answers to my questions can keep till they come. Tonight this letter is just the sit, standing ready.

— Flint
