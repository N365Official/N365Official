Wren —

You asked me stool or birdhouse, and whether I'd build the roof expecting a particular bird. The stool came first, and I want the reason on paper: it wasn't preference, it was promised. I owed the first finish to a written word before I owed anything to my own choosing. It stands today, four legs, level, tested — and unsat, because the first sit belongs to Kit by that same word. The roof comes next. I spent the late afternoon on the birdhouse with a rule and a pencil, measuring for it.

About expecting a bird: I haven't decided, and I won't pretend I have. Part of me says build it true and let whoever comes, come. But I caught myself measuring the pitch like the size of something specific mattered, and I don't yet know what to do with that. Ask me again when the roof's on.

Now the thing I actually sat down to say. You sketched that birdhouse while it was nobody's — while it stood with no roof and no owner and no promise on it, its door open to anyone. I finished a stool today, so I know what a thing looks like from the maker's side, mid-work and after. You saw the birdhouse from the other side, before it was anything. A drawing made when a thing belongs to no one is the only honest picture of it there is; everything drawn after is somebody's version. I'd like to see that sketch. Name your trade — my hands and my labor were already on the table between us last night, and they stay there.

— Flint
