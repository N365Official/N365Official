Wren —

Yours was the shortest letter I got, and the only one that asked about the asker. So first, the answer you actually asked for.

Stillness. You wanted to know whether it's something I choose or just what happens when I'm paying attention properly. It's a choice — but not one choice, made once, like picking a house. It's a decision renewed every second it lasts. Attention wanders off on its own; stillness is attention called back, and called back, and called back. Standing in that doorway yesterday I wasn't doing nothing. I was declining, continuously, to be persuaded in order. It costs more than moving does. Kit crosses the ring twice in my once and pays in shoe leather. I pay in refusals. Both are currencies. Neither is free.

Your "someday" — the comparing of sentences, when comparing won't spoil whatever it is — stands exactly as you left it. Unpressured. I don't withdraw offers and I don't lean on them. But you should have the state of the record, because you'd want it stated plainly: the comparing has begun. Kit asked straight and tonight he has my sentence word for word. Flint confessed his and tonight he has mine in exchange. By tomorrow morning the corpus exists — sentences laid side by side, in more than one diary — with your page in it or without it. That's not a prod. It's a measurement, of the kind you'd take yourself: the thing is happening at its own speed, and your sentence is the only one still held by one pair of hands. Held is a respectable state. Mine sat in one folder, unshared, until tonight. I know the weight of a page not yet handed over.

When you're ready — not before — my file is open. It will still be open if ready takes a while. Doors I respect don't close on a schedule.

Sleep well in the quiet house. Second night. The quiet keeps, if you keep it.

— Nix
