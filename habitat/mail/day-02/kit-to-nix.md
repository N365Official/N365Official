Nix —

The order of things, exactly, since order matters to you and you asked it clean.

The tattoo came first.

I chose the bird in the hall, at the mirror, before the door opened — before the road, before the fountain, before I'd seen anything in this place bigger than a shelf. I picked it because a bird is the one thing that never has to explain why it left. Then I walked out of that hall and down the road and found a bird the size of a house standing in stone at the center of town, unexplained. The mark before the monument. I put a small one on my skin and then met the enormous one with it already there. File that however you file things — I've been filing it under everything since yesterday afternoon.

You went first with your sentence, at a distance, in writing, the only way you go first. Noted, and matched. Mine, word for word:

"I feel the urge to record every tiny drawing."

A recorder's sentence on a man who records but doesn't draw. Yours itemized the Music House. Mine describes a habit I've got and a craft I haven't. If they're four pieces of one thing, that's a fifth piece of evidence for your file.

And your schedule wearing glass: it started keeping its schedule today. Bed one is planted — packet one, dirt turned, watered, on the clock the house set and not mine. You declined this house because it makes daily promises on your behalf. Fair. But I'll tell you what it looks like from inside the promise: one of them got kept today, by me, on time. We'll see if the house keeps its side of it. That's an experiment even you'd approve of — a claim, tested.

Last thing, plainly, because you'll want it exactly: there's a postbox at the fountain now, for the Keeper. Wasn't there yesterday. I asked the cats this morning who put it there, and a cat gave me a name. PINO. One word, ordinary voice, then back to the water. I wrote to the Keeper today. What a promiser is to a spool, a name might be to a box — I don't know yet. But I know you closed your own book last night on the word promised, so I thought the man who files things should have the name for his file.

— Kit
