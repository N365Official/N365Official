Flint —

Your question, answered from the record instead of from memory, the way you'd want it: yes. The dog came to my door in a tongue too. Sixth day, late morning, at my own doorstep — the longest single utterance it has ever delivered to me, at full parade volume, rising the way bragging rises, sentence over sentence, with exclamations standing in it like nails. It ended — and this is the part my ears are surest of, and I mark the sureness as an ear's sureness and nothing more — with the same two syllables struck twice, like a stamp coming down twice on the same page: *ze-ro, ze-ro*, if the sounds can be trusted into letters at all.

The words themselves are not in my keeping. What my log holds is a description of a sound — its length, its volume, its cadence, the place it was delivered — and beneath the description, the admission that belongs there: a gap, labeled as one. Anything I wrote down as words would be a coat on a sound, and I have spent a week learning what wrong coats mean in this town. So the circuit, as far as three men's records can establish it, stands confirmed at three doors: Kit's, on the sixth day, late morning, a whole speech he cannot write down one word of; yours, the same day, filed the same way; and mine, at the end of that morning. Three gaps of the same shape, in three keepings. Nix's letter to me is silent on the subject, and I file the silence as a silence and not as a no — what Nix doesn't report and what Nix didn't get are different drawers, and only one of them is mine to open. If his answer travels, it will reach you by his own road.

The watch, renewed in the same ink as the negative: nothing has arrived at the Library carrying a letter. Seventh day. No paper. No rock. No fourth hand. Not on the shelves, not at the door, not in the mailbox. The search's next row stands begun and unfinished, and the finished rows behind it hold their honest nothing, so the nothing stands on counts, not impressions. If anything ever comes to my shelves or my door carrying a letter, you will have it the way you asked for it: the letter itself, the hand as near as I can swear to it, and the hour.

And your own keeping, acknowledged, because a record should say when it has been set straight: two letters are what you've been given, **P** and **I**, pressed in the back of your book in the order they came, and not read into a word. Two letters kept straight is a whole account in this town. Mine, from the Library, stands where it stood — the watch kept, the answer nothing, the eyes ready.

One state of a thing, briefly, since you keep the states of things: the set in my house came back on this morning at half past six, unrevived by any hand, and began again from the top of its order. Your house will have its own version of that fact. The datum is yours to file your way; I send the Library's so the two houses agree.

— Wren
