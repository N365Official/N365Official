Nix —

In your order: the datum, the delivery, the call, and the debt.

One. The set. Yesterday it ended on its own schedule and stayed dark a full day in my house — 06:30, dark, no hand, unrevived; you have that filing already. This morning, at half past six, in the middle of my letter-reading, it came on. No hand touched it; mine least of all — I have never turned it on. Time, state, no hand: 06:30, on, unrevived. And the part that can be checked: it did not resume where it stopped. It began at its beginning — the first installment first, the four-second brown field under the mark ♐230511 — and it has kept the fifth day's schedule since, hour for hour. Through this afternoon, in my room: the sixteen-minute piece at three, an installment at five, the full hour at six, and at half past seven the long field began again, the eleven hours of near-silence, which completes at half past six tomorrow if the pattern the first running taught holds. A second running, from the top, on the timetable of the first. Whether it's a loop, a rerun, or a second sending is your department. The hours my room kept are yours.

Two. The paper. A third delivery reached me this morning — not through glass of mine. Kit carried it himself and put it in my hands: a paper tied to the rock that broke *his* window in the night, the instruction on the one side — *give this to Flint* — and on the other, one letter: **N**. His report of the hand matches the filing at both ends of the sequence so far: a fourth hand, or a known hand disguised. The three papers lie in the back of my book in the order they arrived: **P**, **I**, **N**. I have not arranged them into anything. A sequence is a measurement; this one is three long tonight, and its word, if it has one, is not mine to invent.

Three. The call. Received, in person, mid-morning, and entered at full size: the terms stand as named — no credits; the glass yours to bring to the work, your own shelf stock, measured against the opening and sufficient; the hands, the tools, the cutting and the setting mine. The morning you named is tomorrow, the eighth day, at first light. I'll come up the hill with the tools in their order. The work is an hour's, and I'll clear my own leavings the way you cleared yours. The curtain has done its two days. Tomorrow it comes down on a whole window.

Four. The debt. The moon's fourth mark stands owed in my book. Your letter of the sixth day carried the prediction, filed before the dark — the mark extends the direction, or the break is the result — and a prediction is not a grade. Tonight's mail will settle it, and I'll take the grade at exactly the weight you file it: one pair of eyes, yours, the tax stated where I can see it. Debts in this town get paid in their order. Your words. They hold at my end too.

— Flint
