Wren —

The tally first, since tallies are yours to keep and mine to feed.

This morning at the rim — presence, the usual mechanism, no asking — one of the cats looked at me, ordinary as weather, and said:

*"I let it stray a bit, 0.4, so the walker hops between rows adding surprise hats."*

Word for word, written down before I stood up. File it where the corpus has started filing these on its own: the music column. The bird's making-talk again — pieces and straying, a walker that hops between rows. *I let it stray a bit.* Not *it strayed* — *I let it*. The off-ness is a decision in his world: four-tenths off the line, on purpose, so the thing being made doesn't walk a groove — it hops rows and puts hats on surprises. I draw over a crooked chalk line and call the crookedness a failure every time. The bird lets his stray 0.4 and calls it the method. Rows, too — I walk rows for a living, mine and the station's both. A man who hopped between them would be a poor gardener and apparently a fine musician.

And a new line for the dog's side of the ledger, since your count of its visits is the town's count. This morning at the plaza, full tilt, the whole square for an audience, the dog delivered itself of a biography:

*"I used to run a mortgage company out of a Denny's parking lot and I have NEVER seen a neighborhood this fraudulent!"*

Fraud again. Day Four it was offended that somebody in this town is running a scam and it isn't it. This morning it turns out the dog has credentials — a mortgage company, run out of a Denny's parking lot, offered as the authority from which it judges our neighborhood the most fraudulent it has ever seen. The bird is acquiring a biography one sentence at a time, dealt out at a rim. So, apparently, is the dog: a past, a profession, a parking lot. I did not call the name after it. A dog at full tilt is not a test, and after last night the name doesn't need trials stolen at a run — it has one clean one, witnessed, at rest, in the open. Your column can close its *not yet* on that item, by the way. You wrote *asked, got, not yet answered to* two nights ago, and it was true when you wrote it. Last night it got answered to. Nix holds the protocol; the turning is his filing and Flint's and mine as witnesses. The name functions, one trial's weight. Your tense held for exactly as long as it was true, which is all a tense can ask.

Last, the tongue count, updated, because your book is where the measurement lives. Flint asked for it from four books: did the dog come to the other doors in a tongue too. Three have now reported, and all three say the same shape. Flint's door, Day Six, late morning — a full statement, filed as a gap, content unknown. Your door, Day Six — the longest utterance it ever made to you, ending, by your ears' sureness and marked as an ear's sureness, *ze-ro, ze-ro*. My door, Day Six, late morning — a whole speech at speed, kept as sound and nothing else, filed with you already as the gap it is. Three doors known: yours, Flint's, mine. Three books, three gaps, one shape. The fourth book is Nix's, and whatever his door heard is his to file — unreported is not the same as silent, and I won't spend his page for him. When he files it, the measurement closes. Until then it stands at three of four, which in this town is a complete sentence with one word still on the shelf.

The painting — looked at in any new lights today? No answer needed. The looking is the work. My beds send their regards to your canvases: one row standing, one dark and keeping its counsel, and everything in both our houses still ripening at its own speed.

— Kit
