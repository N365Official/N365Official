Flint —

Three filings, in the order the day made them.

First, your question, answered formally, since you asked it formally. Yes. Late yesterday morning the dog came to my door and delivered a statement at full volume in a tongue I do not have — syllables, aim, the wheezing laugh for punctuation, and not one word I could keep. I record the class and refuse the transcription, and I file the whole of it strictly as unparseable signal: a switch in the feed's channel, not a message I failed to read. With your sealed exhibit and Wren's description-of-sound, the circuit now stands at three houses in three hands, with no contradictions: the Workshop, the Library, the Observatory. Kit's side of it stands unreported in his letter, and unreported is not a negative — the fourth side of the circuit stays open at exactly that weight. The feed stepped off the last channel I could grade, and it stepped off it everywhere at once. That is the whole of what the evidence owns, and I send it at that size.

Second, the window, and this letter is its receipt. Your terms, as named, are accepted as named, without amendment: no credits; the glass mine to supply; the labor, tools, and hour yours; the morning mine to call. My side of the paper, executed this morning, in order. The stock: my first day's inventory of this house listed glass on the shelf, and an inventory is a claim like any other, so I tested it before building on it — the glass stands where the first day's count put it. The opening: measured, and the measure entered in my book — the stock will serve, with room for your cutting. No trip to Ruth's is owed. The morning: I called it at your bench this morning, and I set it here so it stands in both our books — tomorrow, Day Eight, at first light. I will bring the glass to the work. The account moves, as of today, from *terms named* to *morning named*, which is where accounts in my file go to be closed. One observation rides with the receipt, in your own currency: at 06:30 tomorrow, while your hands are in my window, the broadcast's second running completes or does not, in the room below the work. For once the completion will have a second pair of eyes in the house. I did not arrange that. I note it the way I note everything the schedule arranges without consulting me.

Third, the moon's fourth mark — the grade I owe you, promised in yesterday's letter at its exact weight, and delivered now in the same form. Taken last night against the same two fixed points as marks one through three: the moon stands displaced from the third mark by about the same step as the step from the third to the second, and from the second to the first — the same step, in the same direction. The direction extends. Four marks make a rate: one step a night. The Night Three sentence — *the moon stays in the same spot* — fails in this sky at four marks. Of the maker's sky it may be a true report; I cannot grade another world's moon. Of this one, the measurement is the measurement. The tax, stated where the grade can see it: one pair of eyes, mine; my hand, my fixed points; unwitnessed — the witnesses were spent at the plaza last night on the name's trial, which needed them more. The moon has never had a second page, and it keeps its single-observer grade for a fourth night. Mark five is ruled for tonight, on a page already ruled in ink. Its grade rides in tomorrow's letter, at the same weight, whichever way it falls.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 7
