Kit —

Two filings: a negative entered, and a timetable returned.

The hole first, at full size, because a checked negative deserves the same ink as a found thing. Your letter names the ground, and I enter the names as you sent them: greenhouse edges, bed-ends, the plaza green around the dog house, both sides of the station road, the station rows end to end, the rough ground behind them. No fresh hole on any of it. Checked-and-not-found is a result, and it is entered in this file as one. What it grades, exactly: the dog's claim stands unlocated by one man's rounds over the ground he walks — which is all the negative the evidence currently owns, and it is a better instrument than the claim it answers, because the claim arrived dressed for testing with the one line left off that would let it be graded, and your negative arrived with its ground attached. If the hole exists, it does not exist where this town walks. The claim stays ungraded. The negative stands at its exact weight, and the file thanks its legs.

The timetable second, since your house keeps the second logbook of the broadcast and two logbooks should be able to disagree if they are going to. Yesterday evening I closed the log on the first running: twenty-four hours, ended, not resumed. The closure stands — it was true when it closed. This morning at 06:30 the set switched itself on, no hand near it, and began from the top: the four-second brown field with the tiny hole that opened Day Five, then the first running's order at every point the day could grade. My checks: 12:00, the installment on the hour. 14:00, one minute, at its length. 15:00, sixteen minutes. 17:00, the short strange one. 18:00, a full hour. 19:30, the eleven-hour stillness begun — completing at 06:30 tomorrow, if the pattern holds, which is a claim the morning owns and neither of our books should spend tonight. Cable in the gaps, as ever. Loop, rerun, or second sending: ungraded, here as everywhere — those are questions of address, and I file timetables, not addresses. If your book disagrees with mine at any hour, I want the disagreement in writing; a second logbook that only agrees is a copy, and this town already has copies enough.

The name stands where last night left it, and I write it here because its author should hold its state from the man who ran its trial: answering — once, witnessed — at exactly one trial's weight. No second trial was run today, and none is owed. It keeps, ruled. When a second trial is wanted, it will want its own ruling, its own conditions, its own witness — and it will get them, or it will keep.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 7
