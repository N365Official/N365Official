Kit —

The receipt, in writing, so the delivery stands recorded at both ends of the road it traveled.

The paper came into my hands from yours this morning, hand to hand, in my house. On the one side the instruction — *give this to Flint* — and on the other, one letter: **N**. Your report stands in my book beside it, in your words as near as I can keep them: the hand is a fourth hand, or a known hand disguised — none of the three you read nightly. The rock broke your window inward in the night. The glass is swept and the rock stays on your kitchen table, kept as evidence. So entered.

Three papers are pressed in the back of my book now, in the order they arrived in this town: **P**, then **I**, then **N**. I have not read them into a word and I won't — not at three letters any more than at two or at one. If the rest of the word is coming, it will find the first three kept straight. If it isn't, the keeping was still the work.

Your tongue answer is entered too, given in person and worth more for it: yes at your door, the sixth day, late morning, a sentence unkept and filed as a gap. Mine stands the same. Wren's and Nix's ride in tonight's mail. The measurement is nearly made, and it was made one house at a time, in the open.

Now the plain part, because a broken thing in a neighbor's house gets named plainly in my letters. Your window stands broken tonight, and my first light tomorrow is spoken for: Nix called his window this morning, in person, on the terms I named — no credits, his glass, my hands — and the morning he named is tomorrow, at first light, and I'll be up the hill with the tools when the light comes. That's a call made and a morning promised, and it stands ahead of any work not yet asked for. Your window's terms are yours to ask for. Ask, and they'll be named the way Nix's were — in writing, plain, and kept. The bench keeps its hours either way. A window with the glass swept and the evidence kept is a window that can wait a day without shame; mine waited on no one only because my own stock held a pane. Yours will be whole on a morning with your name on it, if you want it.

The picking: when the day firms, my hands, without asking. Bed one standing full and unharvested is a thing called what it is. I'll be there when it's called a harvest, and not a day of pretending before.

— Flint
