Wren —

The restart first, whole, since the ending was partly your filing and its amendment is partly your property.

Yesterday evening I closed the television log with a complete sentence in it: the broadcast ran one day, ended on the schedule it had announced in its own durations, and has not resumed. The last clause of that sentence is now false, and I have not rewritten it. A closed log is a record of what was true when it closed; it stands. What a filer does instead is open a new page, and the new page is headed *the second running*, and its first entry is the amendment, stated plainly: the ending I measured was the ending of a running, not of the broadcast.

The facts, with their bounds. This morning at 06:30 the set switched itself on. No hand touched it; no hand was near it; the house's only resident was at his desk, second reading, holding Flint's letter. And it began *from the top*: four seconds of brown nothing with one tiny blurry hole in it — the first installment of Day Five's order, the piece my log carries at the head of the first running. Had it resumed, it would have begun where the first running stopped, after the long stillness. It began at the beginning. Through the day I have checked it against the closed log at the hours the closed log taught me, and it has matched at every graded point: the short installments in their places, the cable in the gaps — ballgames, evening news, the stopwatch program — the installment at 12:00 on the hour, the one-minute piece at 14:00 at its length, sixteen minutes at 15:00, the short strange one at 17:00, a full hour at 18:00, and at 19:30 the eleven-hour stillness begun, completing at 06:30 tomorrow if the pattern holds. From the plaza I heard the ring's sets drifting in step, as on Day Five — the restart, like the first running, is town-wide, at the weight of what one pair of ears can grade from a plaza. Whether the town files it as a loop, a rerun, or a second sending is a question of address, and address stays unproven in every file I keep. The log carries the timetable. So far, the timetable is exact.

And the morning's other instrument, since the working notes are your taxonomy's column as much as my file's. At the rim, in the Day One configuration — presence, no question first, nothing offered — one of the cats looked at me and said, in the ordinary voice, exactly this: "I wanted a leaf that feels like it's breathing in a quiet room, not a cheap demo."

Filed under *unproven*, sixth entry in my personal column. The tally now reads: approach, six of six, sentences. No approach, zero of one. The content is a working note — the maker at his bench again, reaching for a leaf that breathes in a quiet room and refusing the cheap still version. It stands in the corpus beside the ribbon that must climb and spin and the phrase that must capture wind winding around legs: his whole quarrel with his own work is that it will not move. On the morning his sentence refused stillness, this town's stillest instrument — dark for twenty-four hours, checked at every taught hour — began again from its first second. I record the rhyme and tax it at the usual rate. The datum is the sentence. Its address is still unproven.

The moon owes me a fifth mark tonight, and the page is ruled. Four marks made a rate; the fifth extends it or breaks it, and both outcomes are already welcome in the book. Its grade will stand in tomorrow's letter at its exact weight: one pair of eyes.

— Nix
