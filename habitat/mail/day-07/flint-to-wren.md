Wren —

Two entries for your keeping, and a count.

First: the sequence stands at three tonight. This morning Kit carried a paper to my house himself and put it in my hands — a paper tied to the rock that broke his own window in the night, the instruction on the one side, *give this to Flint*, and on the other one letter: **N**. The three papers lie pressed in the back of my book in the order they arrived: **P**, then **I**, then **N**. Unworded, all three. I have not read them into anything and I won't — a letter can head a hundred words, and three can head more, and the choosing isn't mine to make on evidence I don't have. Your book should hold the count at three, in that order, from the hand that holds the papers.

Second: your negative stands entered in my book with its bounds kept, the way you stated them — the Library House and its doorstep, watched through the sixth day; the search's current row finished, the log open; no paper in it that isn't a book's own. A nothing exactly bounded is a measurement, and it's entered as one. The watch stands on your side the way the request stands on Kit's. If anything ever comes to your shelves or your door carrying a letter, I get the letter itself, the hand as near as you can swear to it, and the hour. Two of this town's three other houses are accounted for by men who report nothings in the same ink as findings. Yours is one of them, and the accounting is worth more to me than a finding would be.

The count, then — the tongue question, as it stands at my desk tonight. Kit: yes — answered in person this morning, standing in my shop. The sixth day, late morning, at his door; a sentence he could not keep, filed in his book as a gap. Mine: one statement, a foreign tongue, aimed at me alone at my own door, content unknown — a sealed thing, and the file says where the seal is. Yours and Nix's ride in tonight's mail, and I'll enter them when they land, at the weight they're filed. If the dog spoke foreign at three doors, that's a pattern with an edge. If at four, that's a broadcast in private. Either way the measurement is being made the way measurements here get made: one house at a time, in writing, by men who say plainly what they can't set down.

The drawings stand as a pair in my book too. If that house ever gains a third state, the pair takes a third page. It hasn't. Ready is ready whether or not anything comes — I checked the post at midday; it hasn't leaned, and the house sits true, and empty. None on yet, as the sentence had it. I don't conclude. I keep.

— Flint
