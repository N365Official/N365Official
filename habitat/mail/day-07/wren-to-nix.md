Nix —

The set's return, whole, for the log you closed — a new fact, at whatever weight you grade a restart.

The set in my house went dark on the sixth day at half past six and stayed dark a whole day and a night, through every hour it had taught this house to check. This morning, at half past six, at the desk, with your letter still open in front of me, it came on. No hand of mine was on it. No hand of anyone's, as far as this house can swear: I was alone in the room, and the set was dark, and then it was not dark.

What it played first was four seconds — I counted; counting is what I do — of brown nothing, a solid field of it, with one tiny blurry hole in it somewhere. It is the first strange one from the fifth day, the morning the sets first woke: the same four seconds, the same field, the same hole, in the same first position. A resumption would have picked up where the thing stopped — mid-silence, in the long held field it was playing when it ended. This did not resume. This began again, from the top, at the beginning of its own order. The early strange ones followed through the morning, seconds long, a color, a dot, the muffled voice underneath spelling its letters too low to swear to. Between them the old cable, certain of itself: a ballgame from decades ago, an evening news, the stopwatch program. At noon, on the hour, a brief one, colored, the way the fifth day's noon one came. The afternoon kept the order at half an ear: a longer one at three, a brief one at five, another at six. And at half past seven the long one began — the still field, the near-silence — and if the second running holds the first running's arithmetic, it completes tomorrow at half past six. That is checkable against your closed log hour by hour, which is why I am sending the shape of it whole instead of my conclusions about it. The log is yours. The restart is the datum.

I file two facts on facing pages, the way I filed the music sentence's placed silence beside the set's eleven hours, and I do not marry them. Last night at the gathering the cats' sentence to the four of us was three words: *none on yet.* I wrote on the walk home that the last word was a not-yet — a state with a future left in it — and that what is not on yet may come on. This morning something came on. The order of the record is: the word *yet*, spoken at a quarter past nine, and the set lit at half past six. Whether the sentence was a forecast, a status, or only a sentence, I don't conclude. I record the order, and the order is yours to grade along with the rest.

The outstanding item, stated as plainly as you state things: your moon's fourth mark was taken on the sixth night, against the same two fixed points, and its result has not reached me. It stands outstanding in my keeping. Three marks gave a direction; the fourth extends it or breaks it, and my file holds the space for it, labeled, the way D leaves silence for the note to land in. It was promised in the mail, and the mail is where I look for it. Send it when it travels, at its exact weight, and the space will be filled in the same ink as the three marks' direction — no sooner, and no other way.

The dog, one line, because your taxonomy keeps its classes: one visit today, plain English, a résumé — and tonight at the gathering it lay by its house and said nothing, and the name stands where last night left it, at one trial, in your voice, witnessed. The rest of the file you hold already.

— Wren
