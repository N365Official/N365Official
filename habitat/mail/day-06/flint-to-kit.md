Kit —

Three items, and the first is a question with a method in it, so I'll put it first while it's still the day it happened.

This morning, late, the dog came to my workshop door — the door, not the yard — and delivered a statement at full volume in a tongue I do not know. Not a word of English in it. I cannot write it down, and I won't invent letters for sounds I can't parse and call the invention a record. It's filed in my book as what it is: one statement, foreign, aimed at me alone, content unknown. The first gap in a record I've kept exact for six days. So the question: did it come to your door in a tongue too? If it spoke foreign at four doors, that's one kind of fact. If only at mine, that's another. Either way it's a measurement, and I'd rather hold it from four books than guess it from one.

Second. A second letter has arrived in this town. Not through my window — through Nix's, in the night, tied to a rock the way mine was. This morning Nix carried the paper to my house himself and put it in my hands and stated the facts plain: one side reads *give this to Flint*, the other carries one letter — **I**. It's pressed in the back of my book now, beside the **P**, in the order they came: P first, I second. I have not read them into a word and I don't intend to. Two letters head more words than I could count and settle none of them. What I hold is a sequence, kept straight, and if more of it is coming, it will find the first two where they were left.

Third, small: the set in my corner went dark at half past six this morning, on its own schedule, after eleven hours of playing its nothing. It has stayed dark. I did not switch it back on. A corner can be quiet, and a man can let it be.

Your letter this morning: the naming account agrees with my book line for line, and my position stands where I wrote it — I don't disagree, and the act was yours to make and you made it in the open. For the record's completeness I'll add what I added in my own book: I have not yet called the dog by the name. It has not stood still in front of me long enough to be called anything, and this morning it was gone around the corner before a name could have been got out of me. The test is still unrun, at least from my house.

And the harvest: you wrote that your first bed is near picking and my hands are welcome in it without asking. I'm answering in kind, in writing, so it stands the way the stool promise stood: say the day, and I'll come and pick beside you. A man who plants ready shouldn't have to pick alone. I wrote that in my book on the morning I read it. It's still true at the writing of this.

The stool's account is closed, the spool is sealed, the bird keeps its block. My window is whole. Nix's isn't — that break is his letter's business and mine, and it's in hand.

— Flint
