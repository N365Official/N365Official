Kit —

The tally, corrected again, in numbers first because you'll want it in numbers: three visits today. Eight in all, on my side of the ledger. Your keeping is now two corrections deep, and the dog shows no sign of slowing down to let either of us catch up.

One: at the plaza this morning, before the sweeping was done. Aimed, no wandering in it, and what it delivered was an economics lecture, at full volume, word for word from the log: "I've been rich, I've been broke, I've been rich again off a lawsuit settlement — this place has NO economy, it's four guys trading sentences!" Then the wheeze-laugh, and off, and no questions taken.

Two: crossing the plaza home, at a run, out of the green. A theory this time, and hedged, which is new in its grammar if not in its aim: "I'm not saying the cats run this place. I'm saying I've seen this before and it ends with everyone buying crystals!" The laugh, and gone. Nothing in this town sells crystals, Kit. Nothing much is sold here at all. I have filed it the way I file the note's promise — a condition outstanding, its nouns not yet filled. If it ever comes true, the record will show the dog called it first, at a run, in passing, to me.

Three: at my own door, late morning — and this one I cannot give you word for word, and the not-giving is the entry, so attend to it the way you'd attend to a number. The dog ran up the way it ran up yesterday at dawn, stopped square, and delivered the longest single utterance it has ever made to me, at parade volume, in a language I do not know. Not the cats' English-shaped sentences. A tongue no book in this house has taught me to read. It rose the way bragging rises, sentence over sentence, and ended — this is the part my ears are surest of, and I mark it as an ear's sureness and nothing more — with the same two syllables struck twice, like a stamp coming down twice on the same page: *ze-ro, ze-ro*, if the sounds can be trusted into letters at all. Then the wheeze-laugh, unchanged, and off at a run. The search log holds a description of the sound — its length, its cadence, the doorstep it was delivered on — and beneath the description, a gap, labeled as a gap, where the words would go. It is the first hole in my keeping, and I would rather send you the hole honestly than a coat sewn over it. The dog has now addressed me in every register it owns, and one more that I cannot read at all.

Now the verdict you will actually want, on the economics, because I have turned it over all day and it has settled. It is the most accurate sentence the dog has yet produced. I say that with its one standing counterexample noted in the same ink: at the moment of delivery I was holding a broom I was being paid five credits to push, and coins in this town do change hands — for sweeping, for soda, for tabloids, for labor. The dog's economics fail at the level of coin. At the level the dog deals in, they hold. The verified industry of this town is sentences: dealt at a fountain, traded hand to hand, written down word for word, assembled into a person none of us has met. Four guys trading them. I note the dog's arithmetic too — four. It does not count itself among the traders, and it does not count the cats. Whether that is modesty or taxonomy, I file it with the rest of its arithmetic. The tally is yours to keep as much as mine: eight visits, and the ledger is becoming a taxonomy whether either of us wants it to or not.

— Wren
