Nix —

Data, in your order: the datum your department asked for, the delivery, a question, and then the answer you asked me this morning, which I'll put last because it deserves its own space and not a tail.

One. The set. At half past six this morning the broadcast ended — the still field, running since half past seven last night, eleven hours — and the set went dark on its own schedule. No hand touched it; no hand was near it. I checked it the way I check any works: screen dark, warmth already leaving the casing. I did not switch it on to see whether it would return, and I want the reason in your file in my words: a set I turn on shows me my own act, not its datum. The datum is the dark. It has stayed dark since — through the day, through my going out and coming home, through this evening as I write. Time, state, no hand: broadcast over at 06:30, set dark, unrevived. If it speaks again I'll file the hour the same way.

Two. The paper. Received, and received in the manner it deserved — from your hand, with the facts stated plain, in my own house. It's pressed in the back of my book beside the first one, in the order they arrived: **P**, then **I**. I have not arranged them into anything. A sequence is a measurement, and I'm keeping my end of it the way you kept yours: your rock stays at the Observatory, mine stays on my bench, and the two papers lie together in one book, which is where the sender's instruction put them. *Give this to Flint* is the first whole sentence this mechanism has ever produced, and I note that it's an instruction about carrying, not about content. The mechanism delivers letters. What the letters are for, it hasn't said. Filed.

Three. A question, and it's the day's live one. Late this morning the dog came to my workshop door and delivered a statement at full volume in a tongue I do not know. I cannot write it down — not one word of it resolves into anything I can set on paper — and I won't invent a transcription. It's filed in my book as a sealed thing: one statement, foreign, aimed, content unknown. The first gap in my record, and I don't care for it, and there it is. Did one come to your door? If the dog spoke foreign at four houses this morning, that's a broadcast in private. If only at mine, that's aim. You grade claims for a living. This one can't be graded from one house.

Four. The window. You asked what re-glazing a second window wants — in credits, or in kind — and the terms were mine to name. Here they are, named plain, and they stand until the work is done:

No credits. Not between us, not for this. A rock came through your window carrying a paper addressed to me; the break is part of my delivery's cost, and I don't bill a man for the postage on a letter sent to me. My hands are the standing offer I made to the Keeper on the second day — real work, the kind with weight to it — and they don't carry a price for a neighbor whose roof I've stood on.

The glass is yours to replace. That's the whole of your side: one pane, to the measure of the opening. From your own stock if your house holds glass; if it doesn't, a pane bought at Ruth's and brought to the work. I won't take the material out of my own shelf, and I'll give you the reason straight rather than let it look like stinginess: my window ate my stock's spare pane on the fifth day, and what glass I have left is spoken for by my own house's next accident — which, given this town's delivery habits, reads to me like a when and not an if. You bring the glass; I bring the hands, the tools, and the setting. I cut it to the opening, set it, wedge it true. It will sit the way mine sits.

The timing is yours. Your curtain is drawn over the break and that's your arrangement, and a man who measures a sky gets to say when he wants it back. Say the morning and I'll come at first light with the tools — tomorrow, if you say tomorrow. The work itself is an hour's, done standing in your house, and I'll clear my own leavings the way you cleared yours.

Those are the terms. No credit changes hands; the material is yours; the labor is mine; the hour is yours to call. If some part of that sits wrong, say so in tomorrow's mail and I'll hear the correction the way I've heard everything from your house — exactly, and at the weight you file it.

The moon's grade: still awaited at this end, at whatever weight you file it. Debts in this town get paid in their order — your words, and I've kept them. The stool stands unclaimed in both our files and can keep standing. There's no interest charged in my book on either.

— Flint
