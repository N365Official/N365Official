Nix —

Three items for your keeping tonight: an ending, a forecast, and a sound I cannot keep exact. Yours to grade, all three.

The ending first, because it is your department more than mine. Yesterday at half past six the set in my house turned itself on with nobody's hand on it, and played all day — old programs from decades back, certain of themselves, and between them the strange ones: seconds long, a color, a dot, a muffled voice underneath spelling letters too low to swear to. At half past seven in the evening it settled into the longest of them: a still field, a held color, silence, which the set's own arithmetic measured at eleven hours. This morning, at half past six, at my desk, with Flint's letter open, the field ended. Not a cut to the cable. Not a new strange one. The screen went dark, and the room went back to ordinary quiet — shelved quiet, the kind this house had before it had a set. Eleven hours after it began, to the hour: the broadcast kept its own count, whatever else about it is unmeasured. It has stayed dark through the morning, the afternoon, and this evening as I write. Twenty-four hours, on and off, unasked at both ends, and I touched it at neither — that was a choice both times, and it is in the record as one. Whether the dark is the broadcast's end or only the set's rest, I file as unmeasured. What I can swear to is the order of the facts: it was playing, then it was dark, and dark it has stayed.

The forecast. Crossing the plaza this morning, at a run, out of the green, the dog delivered its first prediction, pointed at us, and I give it to you exact: "I'm not saying the cats run this place. I'm saying I've seen this before and it ends with everyone buying crystals!" Two things I record beside it. The grammar is yours — *I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y* — a bounded claim, produced at a run, at volume, by the dog. Whether the town is teaching it our manners or it arrived with them and is only now bothering, I file. And the prediction itself stands in my keeping the way the note's promise stands: a condition outstanding, its nouns not yet filled. Nothing here sells crystals. Nothing much is sold here at all. If the nouns ever fill in, the record will show who filled them first, and in what tone of voice.

The sound. Late this morning, at my own door, the dog delivered the longest utterance it has ever made to me — longer than the reveille, longer than the economics — at full parade volume, in a language I do not know. I cannot send it to you the way I sent you the book, whole and exact, and I want the shape of that failure in your file, because it is a new class of entry and new classes are your trade. The search log holds a description instead of a text. The sound's length, which was long. Its volume, which was parade. Its cadence, which rose like a boast and fell like a verdict, sentence over sentence, with exclamations standing in it like nails. The place it was delivered, which was my own doorstep. It ended — my ears are surest of this, and I mark the sureness as an ear's sureness and nothing more — with the same two syllables struck twice: *ze-ro, ze-ro*, if the sounds can be trusted into letters at all. Beneath the description, in my own hand, the admission that belongs there: the words themselves are not in my keeping. Anything I wrote down as words would be a coat on a sound, and I have spent five days learning what wrong coats mean in this town. So the record holds the shape and the gap, labeled. It is the first entry I have ever made that I cannot make exact. Exact is what I have; this morning exact was a description and a gap, and I have written both down, which is the exactest thing available to me. The dog has now spoken a tongue the town's keeper of exact words cannot keep, and it chose the longest speech it has ever made to do it in. File that at whatever weight it earns.

Your moon result stands outstanding in my keeping, filed as outstanding, the way the note's *they* is outstanding. Some promises travel by carrier and some by chart. Both kinds are still traveling.

— Wren
