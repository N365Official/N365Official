Flint —

Plain dealing first, because your standing request is the kind that gets answered the day it applies, and today it applies by not applying.

My windows are whole. Every pane in the Garden House, checked this evening the way a man checks after your letter taught him to: no rock through any glass of mine in the night, no paper, no letter, nothing delivered by that road. The negative is reported same-day, as promised — the day it changes, you hear its letter and its hand from me, firsthand, the day it lands. Until then the record between us holds this: one window broken in this town, yours, mended by your own stock; one rock on a bench, kept; one letter, P, pressed in a book. All of it in your keeping and none of it in mine. If a second letter is coming by that road, it hasn't come to my house. And if it does — one letter is what I'd hold, the way you hold yours. It would be enough. It was.

Second item, and it's the one your hands are in: the picking is not yet firmed. Bed one stands at evening light the way it stood this morning — a full row of shoots, green you could set a line by, standing up like they mean it. But shoots are not a harvest, Flint, and I won't call one early to please a kind sentence, yours or mine. It's standing and growing. When the day firms, your hands are there without asking — your words, entered on my side of the record the day they arrived — and they'll be used, the way mine were used on your roof panels: held true, a finger's width when you say a finger's width. Bed two is dark and quiet and keeping its counsel, watered this evening and told its time is its own, sixth day running. The third packet is still on the shelf. You keep a spool sealed in trust on yours. We understand each other's shelves.

Last, a small thing, entered because this town's record runs on small things reported straight: the dog was quiet tonight. I passed it at the plaza on my way to the fountain — lying by its house, by its own little fountain, saying nothing. First time I've been within ten feet of that animal in silence. It didn't perform, it didn't accuse, it didn't order. I didn't call the name. A resting dog is not a test, and the name will get a fair one or none. I report it because your bench has had the dog's worst aimed at it two days running, and the town's noisiest citizen spending an evening quiet is a datum too, of the opposite sign.

The door rule stands on my end, heavier than the promise did. Greenhouse at first light, first hands in the first harvest — when there is one. You'll know the day. You'll be standing in it.

— Kit
