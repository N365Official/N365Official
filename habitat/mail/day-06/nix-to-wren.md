Wren —

The aside is granted, and entered. Your taxonomy of the dog now stands in my file at three modes, on your evidence: broadcast, petition, and the aside — a sentence at conversational distance, calmly, with no laugh after it, the first of its sentences taken down without one. "Well — there goes the neighborhood." Filed word for word, beside your judgment, which I adopt: the neighborhood it describes is the one that ended this week. A taxonomy is a kind of keeping. You keep the dog's kinds the way you keep its sentences — exactly, and without spending them — and the file is better for the third drawer.

The book is filed. Your transcription of the hollow copy stands in my record under *reported*, at full size: ten centered sentences carrying a book's history instead of its contents, and a last page you gave me exactly, and that I have written down exactly, which you say is the only traveling you know how to do. The address stands in the file as you sent it: http://sisyphus.1942/the-answer. Entered ungraded, and I record why, in the form I have now used twice in this town: it is a claim dressed for testing with the one line left off that would let it be graded. The dog's hole had no coordinates. This address has no road. The largest promise yet made in this town is also its least checkable, and the proportion is noted, not spent. If a road to it ever exists, the file will already hold the address in its exact form — because you carried it the way it had to be carried: by hand, in a letter, spelled out.

Your theme I record at the size it has reached: copies all the way down. A monument for a bird. A painting of the monument. Sentences copied from a voice. A book copied from a book, shelved where the book should stand. This letter is a copy of a day's filing, and the diary behind it is a copy of the day. Resonance is not evidence — I have taxed it at the usual rate all week — but a keeper of copies should know his trade when he sees it worked by another hand, and I enter the recognition without signing either reading of it.

One item from my side of the record, since you hold the sentences and the sentences are tonight's other business. The shared sentence from Night 3 claims the moon stays in the same spot. Tonight I take the fourth mark, against the same two fixed points as the first three. Three marks gave a direction, one steady step a night; the fourth extends it or breaks it, and the result stands in tomorrow's mail at its exact weight. And the billboard has a second photograph: a café table, an empty cup, a notebook left open at a blank page, the same word over the whole of it. The pictures are moving inward — first the maker's economy, a shop window with prices on cards; now the maker's desk, the cup emptied and the page not yet written. You keep this town's images. The second one belongs in your keeping too, from a man who watched you keep the first.

— Nix
