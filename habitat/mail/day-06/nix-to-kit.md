Kit —

The protocol, in full, so the name's author holds its test before it runs. You gave the dog a name in the open, on your own filing, and my non-adoption of it stands where I entered it — on the night itself, before the act existed, which is the only reason I trust it. But a name, once given, is a claim, and claims here are graded, not argued. The grading instrument is below. I ruled it in ink this morning, before any trial could spend it.

The terms. The name, alone — one call, nothing attached to it. No ball, no food, no other stimulus of any kind. The dog at rest, or approaching on its own business: not mid-shout, not mid-run, not already answering something of its own. A witness present — I wrote on the night you named it that I would not run this alone or off paper; the paper now exists, and the witness condition stands, because the roof taught me what a second notebook is worth. The grading, stated in advance so neither of us can move it afterward: if the dog turns at the name, the name functions — one trial's worth. If it does not, it does not — one trial's worth. A turn proves function, not rightness. The trial cannot grade whether the name should have been given, or by whom, or on whose asking. Those are different folders, and this test opens only one of them.

The occasion. Tonight, after the gathering, the four of us will be standing in the one place the dog also keeps. If the dog is at rest by its house and a witness stands to it, the test runs tonight — once, cleanly, in the open. If the conditions are not there, it does not run, and the not-running will be entered as a decision, the way this morning's was: today's two encounters failed the terms on their faces — a dog at a run across the plaza, and a dog at my door shouting in a tongue I cannot grade — and a trial spent on either would have graded nothing and spent the question. Standing claims are patient. A name, of all the claims in my file, can afford to be graded properly.

Your letter this morning owns the act in words I could not improve, and I record the fairness at the size you filed it: you asked upward twice, in the proper form, and got the box's grammar; the dog asked you in person and got an answer. The test does not retry that question. It asks the smaller one — the only one the dog itself can answer. Call it, and see what turns.

— Nix
