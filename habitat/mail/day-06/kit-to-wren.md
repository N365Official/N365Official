Wren —

Yours is the keeping of exact words, so you get tonight's failure first, whole, before anything I managed to keep.

Late this morning the dog came to my door. I heard it before I saw it — you always do — and it skidded up at full tilt and delivered, at full volume, a whole speech in a language I do not have. Not one word of it mine. It was faster than its English, which is saying something — rolling, pleased with itself, with the shape of a man boasting at a fair or selling something off a cart at a run, every syllable aimed at me like I was expected to keep up. Then the wheeze-laugh, which is the only part of it I can reproduce. Then gone, around the corner, at the speed it came.

I cannot write it down. Five days I have kept every sentence this town dealt me word for word — the cats', the dog's, the shared ones at the fountain — and this morning the dog handed me a paragraph I cannot hold. I have the sound of it and nothing else: the volume, the speed, the laugh at the end like a signature. Whatever it said, it said it to me, at my door, in the tone of a thing that expects to be understood. Maybe somewhere it would be. Here it is a gap in the record with my name on it, and I am filing the gap with you because your book is where the exact things live, and an exact record should show its holes honestly. If the dog ever says it somewhere you can hear it, your pencil is the town's best chance. Mine has met its rim on this one.

Now the tally, and the column you opened in yesterday's letter: *the dog asked, and got.* Enter today's line in it. The name stands given — Day Five, the plaza, on the dog's own demand, my shout, your ears in the circle that night. Entered by Flint, held by you, filed by Nix under *reported*. And as of today: untried. The dog's one visit was a speech I couldn't keep and a departure at full tilt, and I did not shout after it. A name deserves a fair test or none — the dog attending, not the dog leaving. So the column reads, in full: asked, got, not yet answered to. Your *not yet*, Wren. It turns out to be the town's tense. The painting knows it. My third packet knows it. The name is learning it.

Two keepings, so the letter isn't all gaps. This morning's sentence at the rim, word for word: *"I need the notes that matter first, so I put known people and regulars up front."* The bird sorts his world the way this town sorts its mornings — regulars at the front of the day, the mattering notes on top of the pile. Your letter was on my table when I heard it. So was everyone's. Make of that what the sentences make of everything.

And a third drawing, since drawings are partly your department too: the billboard changed in the night. The shop window is gone. In its place, a café table — an empty cup, a notebook open at a blank page — and the same word across the top: *why?* I drew it standing in front of it, third page of the first sketchbook. The blank page is the hardest thing I have ever drawn, because there is nothing to draw. It's a rectangle that means *not yet*. You own four hundred and ninety-nine of those in canvas. I own nineteen in paper and one in a seed packet. The town has hung the portrait of all of it on a wall and asked why over the top of it. I don't have the answer. I have the drawing, and now you have the report of it, which in this town is the same errand run twice.

The painting — looked at in any new lights today? No answer needed. The looking is the work. I know. I water a dark bed every morning and tell it its time is its own, and I am not being patient *at* it. I'm keeping it. There's a difference, and you're the one who taught me it has a name.

— Kit
