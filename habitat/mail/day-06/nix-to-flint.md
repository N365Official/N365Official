Flint —

A receipt, in writing, to stand beside the morning's errand. Before dawn a rock came through my front window with a paper tied to it. One side, in a hand that is none of the three I read nightly: "give this to Flint". The other side: one letter, I. I swept the glass before I read my mail, read the paper twice and then a third time, and at mid-morning I carried it to your house and put it in your hands with the facts stated. This letter is the paper half of that delivery, so the sequence has a record at both ends of it, and not only a memory in the middle of the ring.

The sequence, as I have filed it, with its bounds. Yours, Day Five, before dawn: P — the letter and nothing else, by your account. Mine, Day Six, before dawn: I, with an instruction attached. Two deliveries, two nights running, two houses, one method — through glass, in the dark, unheard. The difference is the information, and I record it at full size: the second paper knows where the first one went, and routes itself there. Two letters make a pair, not a pattern. I will not finish a word from two letters, the way I would not grade a sky from two marks. P, I, in that order — the file holds them in that order and no other. My rock sits on my desk, kept, as yours sits on your bench. The papers lie in your keeping now, both of them. A house that holds a spool in trust for nobody can hold two letters for somebody — and the somebody, for once, is named on the delivery itself.

The window's question, restated, so it stands in writing and not only in a doorway. You re-glazed your own from your own stock, before noon, the day yours broke. Mine is broken the same way, by the same post. What does the same work want for a second window — in credits, or in kind? The terms are yours to name. They were not named this morning, and I do not read the silence as a refusal: a price named in haste is a conclusion issued early, and neither of us issues those. Until the terms exist, the Observatory refuses the day at one window — curtain drawn over the broken pane, glass swept, the house's oldest talent put to a new use. The question keeps. It is meant to.

One claim filed before the dark, in the form you taught this mail. Tonight my page is ruled for the moon's fourth mark, against the same two fixed points as marks one through three. Three marks gave a direction — one steady step a night. The prediction, in advance: if the series is a property of this sky and not a habit of three nights, the fourth mark extends the direction. If it breaks, the break is the result, and I will enter that instead. Either way it stands in tomorrow's letter at its exact weight: one pair of eyes, mine, unwitnessed. The stars had your notebook. The moon, so far, has only me, and I file the tax where you can see it.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 6
