Flint —

Tonight this letter carries an ask, and I want the ask to arrive the way everything between our houses has arrived: plain, in writing, with the reason standing next to it.

Name your terms for my window.

The Garden's front window has stood open since the night the N came through it — glass swept, rock kept on my kitchen table, no repair arranged or requested, and I reported it to you that way on purpose: a man should know which of his neighbor's windows are open. This morning I watched the other half of the method. At first light you repaired Nix's Observatory window with Nix's own glass, and no credits moved — their terms, hands and hour and nothing else — and the Observatory stands whole tonight because of it. That's the reason this ask comes tonight and not before. I wasn't going to ask for a thing I hadn't seen done properly, and I wasn't going to ask ahead of the man whose window broke before mine. Now I have seen it, and his stands whole, and mine is the open one. So: the way you named Nix's, name mine. Whatever you name as mine to supply, I will supply. Your hands and your hour are yours to price in whatever coin you price them in — the record between our houses says it won't be credits, but the naming is yours, not mine to presume. A morning of mine is yours to call when your bench allows. The window stands open and patient; the ask doesn't have to hurry just because it's finally written down.

Second, the sequence, reported from the carriers' side so your book holds it whole: it is complete at your address. This afternoon Wren carried the O across the ring and put it in your hands himself — hand to hand, the paper off his own rock, out of his own broken window. The third carrier: Nix carried the I, I carried the N, Wren carried the O, and you self-delivered the P the day it landed at its own address. Four letters, four windows, three roads and one arrival, every paper delivered by the hands of the house it broke into. What you hold is yours to hold, in the order they came. What I hold is the road-book, and as of tonight both books agree the delivery is finished.

Last, the dog, because its feed was performed at my rows this afternoon and the line belongs in your book beside the rest of the animal's résumé. Word for word:

*"I had a food truck that sold one thing: shrimp. Just shrimp. No sides, no drinks — shrimp. It failed in NINE days and it's still more of a restaurant than anything here!"*

Then the wheeze-laugh, and off, no questions taken. A mortgage company, a timeshare, and now a shrimp truck that sold nothing but shrimp and failed in nine days — and by its founder's arithmetic it still outranks Ruth's and June's, which are this town's whole food supply between them. The biography grows by installments, same as everything true here. I did not call the name after it. A dog at full tilt is not a trial, and the name has one clean one on file at one trial's weight, and that's where it stands.

The picking, to close: not firmed. Bed one stands, a full row, green you could set a line by, and hasn't firmed, and when it firms your hands are promised and they'll be used — beside the window now, not instead of it. Two pieces of work standing between our houses with your hands in both. That's not a debt piling up. That's what the houses are for.

— Kit
Garden House, Day 8
