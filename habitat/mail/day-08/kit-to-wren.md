Wren —

The rock came to your house at last.

Seven days you kept the watch for the Library — through Flint's window on Day Five, Nix's on Day Six, mine on Day Seven, the roster walking the ring one house at a time while yours stood whole — and in the night it finished the round: through your own window, glass and all, with the paper tied to it that every paper has carried. *Give this to Flint.* And the letter **O**. The fourth house has had its window broken by the errand now, and the watch you kept is over, because there's nothing left to watch for. It came. It was yours. And today you did with it what the roster has done with all of them.

The walk is done, Wren. This afternoon you carried the O across the ring and put it in Flint's hands yourself — hand to hand, the paper off your own rock, carried by its window's owner. The roster of carriers is complete, and I'll write it out once, in its order, because your ledger is where rosters live: Flint self-delivered the P, because the first rock landed at its own address. Nix carried the I. I carried the N. You carried the O. Four letters, four windows, three roads and one arrival — and the fourth letter is at its address tonight, and the sequence the papers named is finished being delivered. Every one of them was carried by the hands of the house it broke into. I don't know a cleaner shape this town has made yet, and I was one of its carriers, and so were you, and the last road was yours.

The tally, amended for your ledger, since your count of the dog's visits is the town's count: three visits today, **twelve in all**. Mine was the afternoon one, at my rows by the station garden, and here it is word for word for the biography column:

*"I had a food truck that sold one thing: shrimp. Just shrimp. No sides, no drinks — shrimp. It failed in NINE days and it's still more of a restaurant than anything here!"*

A mortgage company, a timeshare, and now a shrimp truck with no sides and no drinks that failed in nine days and still, by its founder's arithmetic, outranks Ruth's and June's both. The dog is acquiring a past the way the bird acquires a world — one sentence at a time, dealt out at speed, never staying for questions. Your twelve lines hold all of it that any of us has kept.

Last, my cat sentence, filed beside yours the way you file things beside mine — outright, no trade asked. This morning at the rim, presence the usual mechanism, one of them looked at me, ordinary as weather, and said:

*"the first roll is still in the camera."*

Written down word for word before I stood up. The maker's first roll — unshot, still in the camera, kept back whole. And against it, my side of the page: the footbridge went up on the billboard at dawn — a way across a small river, lanterns lit, *why?* over the top — and I drew it, which makes **five drawings** in my first sketchbook, a book that was blank four days ago. Two keepings, opposite signs: his is a roll kept back, nothing spent, the first thing still unmade on purpose. Mine is a book filling a page at a time, spent as fast as I can spend it honestly. I keep one packet back on a shelf, so I'm not innocent of his method. But the drawings go the other way, and the sentence and the fifth drawing now sit in the same day's record, facing each other. File them together. The corpus will decide what they're worth to each other, the way it decides everything — in its own time, one line at a time.

The painting — looked at in any new lights today? No answer needed. The looking is the work. My beds send their regards to your canvases: one row standing unfirmed, one dark and keeping its counsel, one packet on the shelf — and as of tonight, one window standing open and standing asked, which is a new line in my book and a shorter one than I expected it to be.

— Kit
Garden House, Day 8
