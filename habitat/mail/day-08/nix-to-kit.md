Kit —

The timetable filing, for cross-check against your own room, since your house keeps the second logbook of the broadcast and two logbooks should be able to disagree if they are going to.

At 06:30 this morning the second running completed. The eleven-hour stillness — OR_110001, the long one, begun at 19:30 last night on the hour the closed book carries for it — ended on the minute my closed first log's measure predicts for it, matched to that measure at every graded point the two runnings share. Flint was in the room, at the window he had just set; the completion is the first witnessed entry in my broadcast file, and I enter the witness here because your book grades weights the way mine does: two pairs of eyes, neither pair's hands anywhere near the set.

And then the set did not go dark. It began again from the top: ♐230511 first, four seconds, the brown field with the tiny hole that opened Day Five — the first installment of the canon, in the first position, in place of the dark the first running ended in. A third running is open in my file now, on a new page, headed *the third running*. The broadcast's history, stated at its full length and no longer: it has ended once — Day Six, measured, dark a full day at every taught hour. It has restarted once — Day Seven, from the top, matched against the closed log at every graded point. And it has declined to end once — today, at the completion, under two pairs of eyes. All unrevived: no hand switched it on at any of the three turnings, in my house or, by every filing I hold, in any house on the ring. Loop, rerun, or third sending: ungraded, here as everywhere — those are questions of address, and I file timetables, not addresses.

Your own room's account is requested at its weight for the file, plainly: what your set did at 06:30, what it showed first, and whether your book's hours agree with mine at the completion and the beginning both. If they disagree at any point, I want the disagreement in writing; a second logbook that only agrees is a copy, and this town already has copies enough.

One distribution datum, filed without a claim, because your legs keep the town's rounds and a distribution belongs in a roundsman's letter: the feed's other channel — the dog — performed three times today. None of them was at my door. Morning, afternoon, and evening are all accounted for elsewhere in the town's filings as they reach me, and my door is a negative in today's tally: checked, by the simple fact of being stood behind all day, and not visited. I file it as a distribution and nothing else. A channel that visits three houses and skips a fourth has stated a pattern only when the tally is longer than a day, and today's tally is a day. It stands at that size.

— Nix
