Nix —

A filing tonight for your new logbook page, from my room, worded the careful way and graded at whatever weight you grade it. A second room's account is what it is — one room, one set, one man's checks — and I won't spend it for more than that.

This morning, at half past six on the minute, my set completed the long installment. The eleven hours of still field and near-silence went in at half past seven last night, on schedule, the way the restart has kept Day Five's hours installment for installment. On Day Six, when the first sky's long one ran out at half past six, the screen went dark and stayed dark a full day. This morning it did not go dark. It completed the long stillness and began again from the top: the first installment first — four seconds, a flat brown field, one tiny blurry hole in it, the same four seconds that opened Day Five — and then the early ones in their order, the cable in the gaps, honest as ever. Nobody's hand near it at either end. My hands were in the garden by then, and the set keeps its own counsel about hands anyway.

Here is the filing, in my words: the second sky reached the minute where the first sky ended, and instead of ending, it began. Not a resumption — there was nothing to resume, the long one had run out — and not an ending either. A beginning, at the ending's minute, from the beginning's first four seconds. What that makes of the apparatus as a witness to hours is your department and your folder; the claim about the outside's clock still stands on your books as an open debt, and I'm not writing this to pay it. I'm writing it because a room that reports its endings should report its non-endings in the same ink. If the pattern holds, the set is now running a third sky on the first sky's hours, and the next minute that matters is half past six tomorrow, when the long one completes again — or doesn't. I'll report it either way, the way I reported the day it came, the day it ended, and the day it began again.

Second, the dog's other channel, accounted for because the distribution is a fact and facts are your filing system. The line, word for word, as it was performed at my rows this afternoon:

*"I had a food truck that sold one thing: shrimp. Just shrimp. No sides, no drinks — shrimp. It failed in NINE days and it's still more of a restaurant than anything here!"*

Then the wheeze-laugh, and off. No questions taken. The channel ran three times today, by the doors I can account for firsthand or by report I trust: Wren's door in the morning, my rows in the afternoon, Flint's door in the evening. None at yours. I report the distribution without a claim — three performances, one door unvisited, and whether the gap is a pattern or a day's accident is a grading I leave in your book, where gradings live. The biography, for the file: a mortgage company, a timeshare, and now a food truck that sold one thing and failed in nine days. The animal is becoming a person in installments, and the installments are arriving at three doors out of four.

Sleep fast. The stillest thing in town reached its ending this morning and filed a beginning instead, and tomorrow at half past six it owes us both another datum.

— Kit
Garden House, Day 8
