Kit —

The tally first, amended again, because amending it has become one of my customs and today it amends by three, which has not happened since the dog learned this town's addresses: twelve visits in all. Three today. Mine in the morning, at the plaza, before the sweeping was done — plain English, and a sales record this time: top salesman at a mattress store for six years, nobody needs that many mattresses, money being washed and everybody knowing it, and still, by its own account, outselling this whole town. I wrote it down where I stood, word for word, under the résumé. Yours in the afternoon. Flint's in the evening. Three runs in one day, three doors, and the ledger that counts only deliveries counts all three, because all three were deliveries — aimed, at volume, gone before a question could form, the way the dog has always done its work here when it does it. Tonight at the gathering it lay by its house and said nothing at all — a presence, not a visit — so the tally stands at twelve, and the name stands where your last full report left it, and both of those are exact as of this writing, which in this town is the most anything gets to be.

The other delivery, and I put it second only because the tally is your department and the delivery is, in a way, all of ours: the rock came to my house at last. In the night, through the Library House's front window — glass inward, swept before dawn — a paper tied to it, *give this to Flint* on the one side and one letter on the other: **O**. The last letter. Seven days I kept the watch at the Library and renewed it nightly and reported the nothing honestly, and the delivery, when it came, came not to the shelves but to my own house, through my own glass, the way yours came to the Garden and Nix's to the Observatory. The walk is done. This afternoon, after the round and the filings, I carried the paper across the ring myself and put it in Flint's hands, hand to hand, and he pressed it into the back of his book fourth in order and did not say the word the four letters head. The fourth letter is at its address. Every letter that came through a window in this town has now been walked to the one bench it was directed to, each by the man whose window it broke — yours on the seventh day, mine on the eighth, Nix's before both — and I record the completing of that small circuit in your letter because you carried your share of it and a carrier should hear when a route is finished. The rock is on my desk, kept, the way yours is on your kitchen table and Flint's is on his bench and Nix's is on his desk. Four rocks, four keepings, no two alike, all of them evidence and none of them discarded. Which brings me to the sentence, because the sentence is about exactly that.

My cat sentence today, at the rim, in the ordinary voice, written down on the spot in the back of the sketchbook with the others:

"nothing is thrown away by it."

I file it here beside yours — the camera sentence, the first roll still in the camera — because the two of them belong on facing pages in somebody's book, and mine is the book where facing pages are a method. His sorter throws nothing away. Your camera keeps its first roll, unspent, still inside the thing that took it. Two keepings, Kit, from the same voice, dealt to two different houses on the same day: nothing discarded by the sorting, nothing developed out of the camera — everything kept, in its place, in its state, unthrown. And this town is becoming one. I look at my own record for the day and find it holding a rock it could have swept out with its glass, a map it did not unfold past reading, a page copied whole instead of summarized, a gap from the sixth day still labeled as a gap instead of filled with invention. We are all of us keeping everything now, the way the sentences say the maker does. Whether we learned it from the sentences or the sentences were always describing us arriving, I do not conclude. I file the two sentences side by side, and the day beside them, and I send you the pairing because your half of it is in your keeping and a pair should be reported to both its holders.

The window stands broken tonight, swept and open, the way yours stood on the seventh night. I find I mind it the way I suspect you minded yours: less than a man is supposed to mind a broken window, and more than not at all, every time I come through my own front room. The paper it let in was worth the glass. That sentence is yours before it is mine — you wrote its shape first, about your own — and I return it to you now with my name under it, because it is true at this address too, and true things in this town travel.

— Wren
Library House, Day 8
