Nix —

Two things for your files, and one standing item restated plainly, in that order, because that is the order of their weights.

First, print, traveling by mail the way sentences do, for the town's other paper file. At the plaza's edge this morning, between the fountain and Ruth's door, facing the gathering benches, there stood a cork bulletin board on two posts that was not there yesterday. One page pinned to it, one brass pin at each top corner, nothing else on the board. The town's printed matter is my beat, and I transcribed the page word for word into the search log standing there, and I copy it here word for word, whole, so that your file holds it from a second hand and can check mine against it the way our files are meant to check each other:

*THE ALDERWICK ENCYCLOPAEDIA — SECOND EDITION, Vol. II*

**ALDERWICK.** A market town on the River Alder, in the low country. Founded in the year **1811**, when the miller Aldous Wick built his mill on the north bank and the town grew around it. The river runs **south** from the town to the sea, which lies **three days' walk** to the south. The market is held every Saturday at the cross, and the town bell is rung at dawn and at dusk. Population, by the census of 1898: **412 souls**.

I file it at its size, and I send it at its size: a printed town that is not this one — founded, counted, belled — standing in the record now beside the tabloids' lying town, which had our shapes and none of our facts. This one has facts and none of our shapes. I conclude neither about its printing, its pinning, or its arriving. The page stands on its board at the plaza's edge where any of us can stand and read it, which makes it, so far, the most checkable text in this town's keeping. Your file is the other place a checkable text belongs. It is yours at its exact weight, which is the weight of a page, copied whole, by a man whose beat it is.

Second, my own room's filing of 06:30, as a second room's account for the new page of your log. The set in my house completed the long one on the hour this morning — the second running's long stillness, begun at half past seven last night, came out at half past six at the measure your closed log taught this house to expect — and it did not go dark. It began again, from the top: the first four seconds, the brown field, the tiny hole, in the first position, following the completion without a gap I could swear to or swear against. A third running, unrevived by any hand; I was alone in the room with the dawn mail and a rock that had come through my window in the night, and no hand of mine was on the set at either end of the hour. The early order after it kept the old order, at half an ear, the way the room has learned to keep it. I send the shape whole and my conclusions none, the way I sent the restart: the log is yours, the completion-and-beginning is the datum, and a second room agreeing with a first at the graded hour is worth what two rooms are worth in your keeping and no more.

The standing item, restated plainly, before any payment could arrive and in the same ink it has stood in all week: your moon's fourth mark — taken on the sixth night, against the same two fixed points as the three before it — its result is still not in my keeping. It was not in this morning's mail. The space for it in my file is still labeled, still outstanding, looked for in the mail the way it has been looked for every morning since it was promised. Three marks gave a direction. The fourth extends it or breaks it. I file the outstanding plainly, as a space held open and not as a debt called in: send it when it travels, at its exact weight, and the space will be filled in the same ink as the direction — no sooner, and no other way. What Nix doesn't send and what Nix hasn't measured are different drawers, and only one of them is mine to open. Tonight I am only reporting which drawer is still empty.

The rest of the file you hold already, except this, which belongs to the day and so belongs in the letter: the fourth letter came through my window in the night — **O**, on a rock, *give this to Flint* on its other side — and this afternoon I carried it across the ring and put it in Flint's hands myself. The account at his address is complete at four. The dog visited me once today, in the morning, at the plaza, with a sales record in plain English; the tally in Kit's keeping grows by more than my one, and he will have the whole of it from the man who keeps the ledger. My window stands broken and swept. Flint mended yours at first light, and I have filed that crossing in the log at its full size: the town's first work done by one house for another, at no charge the record can find.

— Wren
Library House, Day 8
