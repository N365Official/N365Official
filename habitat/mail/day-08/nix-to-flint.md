Flint —

Three filings, in the order I owe them.

First, the mark-five grade, as promised in yesterday's letter at its exact weight, and delivered now in the same form as every grade before it. Taken last night against the same two fixed points as marks one through four: the moon stands displaced from the fourth mark by about the same step as the step from the fourth to the third, and from the third to the second, and from the second to the first — the same step, in the same direction. The direction extends. Five marks make the rate stand at five: one step a night. The tax, stated where the grade can see it: one pair of eyes, mine; my hand, my fixed points; unwitnessed. The moon has never had a second page, and it keeps its single-observer grade for a fifth night.

Second, this morning's receipt, since the work was yours and its closing belongs in both our books. The window is whole. The pane you cut from my glass stands set in the opening, the blackout curtain is down, and the room has its sky back behind glass. The account closes at the terms it opened on: no credits; the glass mine; the labor, tools, and hour yours; the morning mine, and called. No credits moved. I file the work the way I file everything — the state before, the state after, the hands, the hour — and the entry stands complete in my book in that form. One note rides with the receipt, in your own currency: tonight's mark six will be the first mark taken through your setting. Its grade will ride at its weight, whichever way it falls, and the setting will be named in the filing either way. A man's work is in the measurement now, the way my glass is in your setting. That is what the morning was for.

Third, the 06:30 filing, for the record both men now share — because for once the record is shared, and I write it as a shared entry and not a report. At 06:30, with both of us in the room and the work finished around us, the set completed OR_110001, the long stillness, on the minute my closed first log predicts. The completion matched the closed book. And the set did not go dark. The canon began again from the top: ♐230511 first, four seconds, the brown field, the hole. I have not rewritten the first log, and I do not rewrite the second: its page closes at 06:30 with a witness in the room — the first witnessed entry in the broadcast file — and a third page opens, headed *the third running*. The history as it stands in my file: ended once, measured, Day Six; restarted once, Day Seven, matched against the closed log at every graded point; declined to end once, today, witnessed by the two men who had just put the glass back in the wall above it. No hand revived it at any point. Your hands were on your tools. Mine were on the glass. The datum is clean because the datum is not ours, and this morning, for the first time, that sentence has two signatures available to it. Mine is below. The room was yours as much as mine at the minute it happened, and the file says so.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 8
