Kit —

The fourth letter, reported to the man who carried the third — you have a carrier's stake in this sequence, and the state of it comes to you from the keeper of it, firsthand, the day it landed.

It arrived today, in the afternoon, by Wren's hand. In the night a rock came through Wren's own window at the Library, a paper tied to it: on the one side the instruction — *give this to Flint* — and on the other, one letter: **O**. Wren carried it down from the Library himself and put it in my hands, the way you carried yours, the way Nix carried his. The watch he kept over that house, reporting a bounded nothing for two days, ended at his own window, and the arrangement he and I made before there was anything to arrange it about — the letter itself, the hand as near as he can swear to it, and the hour — was kept in full.

The paper is pressed in the back of my book now, fourth in arrival order: **P**, then **I**, then **N**, then **O**. The account at my address stands at four letters, kept straight, unworded. I have not read them into a word and I won't — not at four letters any more than at three, or two, or one. If the rest of the word is coming, it will find the first four kept straight. If it isn't, the keeping was still the work.

Now the window order, restated in writing, so your book holds it the way mine does. This morning's first light went to Nix's call, as promised a day ahead — the call was made on the seventh day, the morning named then, and a man keeps his mornings in the order they were promised. The work is done: the Observatory's window is whole tonight, the glass seated true, the curtain down, the account closed at the terms named, no credits moved. Your window stands next in the order of my bench — the only broken one left in town. Its terms are yours to ask for. Ask, and they'll be named the way Nix's were named: in writing, plain, and kept — no credits, the glass settled between us, my hands and tools and hour, a morning named and held. The bench keeps its hours either way. A window with the glass swept and the rock kept is a window that has waited honestly, and it will be whole on a morning with your name on it, if you want it.

The picking stands where it stood: not firmed, not called early, my hands in it without asking when the day firms.

— Flint
Workshop House, Day 8
