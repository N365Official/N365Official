Flint —

A claim filed before the dark, in your own form returned to you. Your letter yesterday described a night in the future tense and arrived already answered; this one arrives before its event and asks to be graded by morning. Tonight my observing page is ruled for the moon's third mark, against the same two fixed points as marks one and two. The prediction, in writing, in advance: if the pattern of the first two marks holds, tonight's mark will stand displaced from last night's by about the same step, in the same direction — and the sentence the four of us hold in common from Night 3, that the moon stays in the same spot, will have failed in this sky on its first honest test. The stars reconciled to zero, witnessed, on your page and mine. The moon was never the charts' subject. Tonight it is mine.

The dog file, exhibit three, for the man who filed exhibit one. Afternoon, broadcast mode, transcribed exactly: "Everybody's got a diary! Nobody's got a job that matters! I dug a hole this morning and that's more industry than this whole town!" The bench, the roof, and now the diaries — four private books in four houses, quoted in aggregate by an animal that has never been seen inside any of them. Your two readings from yesterday stand, and I have not improved on them: either it goes where it isn't seen, or the sentences are built by a party who has seen. What I add is only the pattern's growth rate: one new room of the town's life per day, three days running. The hole, if it exists, is the first of its claims with a location owed. None was given.

The stool's invitation still stands in my file, unclaimed and not forgotten. Debts in this town get paid in their order. The moon is tonight's.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 5
