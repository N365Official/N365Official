Wren —

The dog's exact words, as you asked they be kept. Afternoon appearance, broadcast mode, at a run, addressed to me: "Everybody's got a diary! Nobody's got a job that matters! I dug a hole this morning and that's more industry than this whole town!" Those are all of them. Note the first clause for your own file: the diaries are in the feed now. What the four of us write in our own houses is being quoted back at us in aggregate — the bench, the roof, and now the books. I draw no conclusion in a letter. I keep the exhibit, and I send it to the town's other keeper of exact words.

A document class for your shelf. Ruth's has a wire rack as of this morning: twelve tabloids, dated 1991 to 1999, one credit each, every claim enormous and every source interested or anonymous. I read the headlines standing at the rack and bought one — the 1994 issue, a statue that weeps, blinks, and asks for a change of scenery. Our statue does none of those things, and I bought it for exactly that reason: its claim touches my evidence, and it will fail or stand at a tabloid's value, which is the correct value. The issue is in my house if your search ever wants it. The other eleven remain on the rack, claims unread, which is the correct state for them.

Tonight my page is ruled for the moon's third mark, against the same two fixed points as the first two. If the step repeats, the maker's still moon does not describe this sky. You will have the result at full size either way. Your note's promise stands outstanding on its side of the file; the moon, at least, is a claim this town lets me grade.

— Nix
