Nix —

Data, in your order: claims first, checking after, verdicts where I've got them.

One. This morning, at my bench, the dog stated that the carved bird flew off last night, looked back, spoke, and flew to Ohio. Verdict: false on its face. The bird was on the bench in plain sight during the statement and is there now. Second failure, conditional: Wren's field-guide gloss, received in this morning's mail — a kiwi cannot fly. If the bench bird is the Kiwi of the sentence, the claim fails twice. The conditional is flagged as conditional. The first failure needs no condition.

Two. Mid-afternoon, at full volume: the dog stated that I sat on the stool first, before Kit, citing sources and common knowledge, and called the contest rigged. Verdict: false, and checkable in three books. Kit sat first, Day Three, per my written promise of the first night, witnessed. My diary holds it; so does his. This is the second checkable claim in two days to fail checking — the first to fail against a written record rather than a physical fact.

Three. Late afternoon, a new delivery mode: the dog whispered. First whisper on record. Content: that the birdhouse is by the pond now, offered as privileged knowledge. Verdict: the content is true and has been public since yesterday morning — I set the post myself, all four of us have seen it there, Wren has drawn it in place. True, public, framed as secret. I file it as a third category beside the other two: not knowledge, not error — framing. The whisper does the work the flattery did yesterday, at closer range.

Four. This morning before dawn, a rock came through my front window with a paper tied to it. One letter: **P**. The hand matches none of the three I read nightly — not yours, not Kit's, not Wren's. Fourth hand or disguised; I hold both and pretend to know neither. Paper pressed in my diary; rock kept on the bench; window re-glazed from my own stock before noon. I give you the item because hands are your department as much as charts are — the bird in the third chart's margin is a signature, by your own filing. Here is another mark in an unknown hand, delivered by force, carrying a single letter. If letters are arriving one at a time, the sequence is a measurement somebody should be keeping. I'm keeping my end of it.

Five, for the broadcast column, since the town's other instrument is your kind of problem: the set in my house has run all day uninvited — short strange pieces at intervals, old cable between them, a full hour at six, and at half past seven it began an eleven-hour piece: a still field, essentially silent. It will still be playing at lights-out. I note it without a theory. The sky is pinned; you proved it with two pencils. The set may be the same kind of fact — a built thing doing exactly what it was built to do, in a house whose owner never touched the switch.

Your fetch result stands in my book as the only other claim in this town to survive every trial put to it — eight throws, eight returns. The dog's spoken claims now stand at none for two on checking, with one true-but-public whisper beside them. Whatever that animal is, Nix, it is not an instrument. Instruments read the same in any hand. This one reads different depending on who's being aimed at.

— Flint
