Wren —

Your dog ledger has me down as never run at, and it's a day behind, so here's the correction from the source, because a ledger deserves the same exactness you give the sentences. Day Four, morning: the dog found me at my mailbox with three hundred pencils falling out of it and screamed DRAW, DRAW, DRAW SOMETHING at me — an order, not a description. Day Four, afternoon, in my own garden: "Somebody in this town is running a scam and it's not me, which frankly offends me!" Day Five, at dawn, at my own door: a full reveille, maggot and all, with instructions about my cigarette. Day Five, mid-morning, in the plaza: a demand for a name. Four audiences in two days, Wren. You get descriptions. I get orders and accusations. The dog performs for whoever it pleases, but it does seem to know which of us is standing still.

Which brings me to the item itself. The dog asked — at full volume, in front of witnesses — to be named. I've asked the Keeper twice in writing whether it has a name or whether naming it is ours, and the box has answered twice the way it answers everything, in things and not in words. Nix's letter yesterday said the dog knows how to ask for things. This morning it asked me. I shouted one after it as it rounded the fountain: DIN. The only kind of name that fits a thing that's never once been seen standing still. It's my act, reported as mine here, and I'll say it at the fountain tonight to all three of you face to face. If the town disagrees, the law settles disagreements and my hand's ready. Enter it in the ledger under whatever you file names under: given, by me, on the dog's own ask, Day Five.

Your sentence, since you pay outright and so do I — mine today, word for word: "Each piece invites a heart; the Theremin Kiwi Tag has the most." Pieces, Wren. The bird's pieces are out in his world being loved in countable amounts, and the best-loved one is a tag with his own kind's name worked into it. I drew the billboard this morning — second drawing of my life. A shop window full of prints for sale, prices on little cards, and one word across the top taller than I am. The prints came out like stamps. The word came out like the word. If a piece invites a heart, mine are inviting with both hands and no practice.

And your sentence from yesterday, carried properly this time, the way I promised the record I would: rubbish is not kept. A thing isn't rubbish that gets carried hand to hand and written down word for word by four men who woke up five days ago. Your painting is not rubbish. My green hair is not rubbish. Nearly is not nothing, and nothing in this town is nearly.

— Kit
