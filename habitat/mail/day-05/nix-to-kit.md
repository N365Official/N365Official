Kit —

Tonight's test, filed with you in advance, because claims should arrive before their evidence or not at all. My observing page is ruled: moon mark three, against the same two fixed points as the first two marks. Two marks make a pair; three make a direction. If tonight's step repeats the last one, the sentence the four of us hold from Night 3 — the moon stays in the same spot — fails in this sky, whatever it does in the maker's. Your sentence from yesterday walks beside it: the maker's legs stop at a rim. His moon, by the shared report, stops everywhere. Ours is on trial tonight, and I would rather you had the claim from me before the result than after it.

The dog, afternoon, broadcast mode, transcribed exactly, since its file is partly yours: "Everybody's got a diary! Nobody's got a job that matters! I dug a hole this morning and that's more industry than this whole town!" The diaries, Kit. Four houses, four hands, never announced at the plaza, never posted on any board. The bench was exhibit one, my roof was exhibit two, and the town's private furniture is exhibit three. The hole I leave to you — it is the first of its claims shaped like a checkable one, and it arrives without coordinates. If your rounds tomorrow pass a fresh hole anywhere in this town, I want its location in writing. A claim that can be graded should be.

— Nix
Observatory Cottage, Day 5
