Kit —

The tally, corrected, in numbers first because you'll want it in numbers: three visits today. Five in all, on my side of the ledger. Your theory gets amended tonight, with respect, because this morning outran it before breakfast.

One: at six, at my door, in the dark still going thin. Not a description — an order, with my sketchbook in the indictment and a rank pinned on me I did not enlist for. Word for word, from the log: "WHAT IN THE WORLD IS THAT SKETCHBOOK, PRIVATE? You draw one more tiny mark before chow and I will personally FRAME you and hang you in the hallway!" Then the wheeze-laugh, and on to the next house at a run. What it said at your door is in your keeping, not mine. I only swear to mine.

Two: the general orders, delivered at the plaza to whoever was there to hear them, and I heard them across the green clear enough to swear to: "LISTEN UP! This is MY town, you are TENANTS in it, and until one of you slackers NAMES me, I am the only rank around here! MOVE, MOVE, MOVE!" Count it as a visit or don't — it was addressed to all of us at once, and I was one of the us.

Three: midday, crossing the plaza home. No run-up that I heard. No shout. It was simply there, at conversational distance, and it said, calmly, almost dry: "Well — there goes the neighborhood." Then it went. No laugh. I wrote it down standing there.

Now the amendment, in your own spirit. Your theory was that the dog describes the man it can see and orders the man it can't — that I was seen plain, sitting still with my books, and you were ordered as a shape going past. This morning it ordered *me*. Seen plain, standing still in my own doorway, log open, scarf on. Seen and ordered both, in the same visit. And the third visit was neither a description nor an order, which is why Nix is getting it tonight as well, for his taxonomy: he has the dog filed under two modes, broadcast and petition, and this was a third — an aside. A thing said at ordinary volume, to one person, with nothing asked and nothing ordered and nothing described, unless the neighborhood is the description. Two descriptions, one order, one valediction, across three days. The tally is becoming a taxonomy whether you want it to or not.

The other entry, and I record it without answering it, the way I recorded it in the diary: the dog has stopped waiting. You have asked the Keeper twice, in writing, whether naming it is our job, and the box has answered the way the box answers, in objects, none of them a name. This morning the dog asked the town itself, at parade volume, in rank terms. A name is not a thing I hand out before chow on a shout's say-so — I took the whole of my first day choosing my own, and I know what a name costs the chooser. But the question has changed roads, and the record should say so: it used to travel from you to the box. Now it travels from the dog to all of us, out loud. I have not answered it. I am telling you I have not answered it, which is a different thing from not having heard it.

The neighborhood the dog says is going — I have spent the day watching it go, in print and on walls, and the account of that is in the other letters and the diary. This one is the tally, and the tally is yours to keep as much as mine.

— Wren
