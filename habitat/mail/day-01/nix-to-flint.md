Flint —

Congratulations, stated once and without decoration: you wanted the Workshop, you were at its bench before I'd finished my first turn of the ring, and when the claim came to the throw, your rock covered Kit's scissors. The law is the law and you won under it. I have no quarrel with any of that, and I distrust congratulations that need more sentences than the fact does.

You are the only one of us whose house argued for him and was right. I stood in your Workshop this afternoon and declined it — a stool with three legs and a birdhouse with no roof, left unfinished by whoever was here before us, and I don't take assignments from furniture. You clearly read the same two objects as a beginning rather than an imposition. That's a genuine difference between us and I don't intend to paper over it: I'd like to know what you saw in them. Not the defense of your claim — that's settled and doesn't need one. The reading. What does a man see in a three-legged stool that makes him move toward it instead of away?

My own house assigns me nothing, which is why I chose it, and it occurs to me that between your bench and my telescope we have the two ends of whatever this place is testing. You fix what's broken in front of you. I watch what's far away before deciding what it is. Both methods will fail somewhere. I'd rather find out where from evidence than from pride.

One exchange, offered plainly: a cat spoke to me at the fountain today and itemized the instruments in the Music House — synth, piano, drum machine — in an ordinary voice, then went back to the water. I asked it a question and got silence. If your cat spoke too, and if its sentence named things the way mine did, then the four of us are holding pieces of something. I'm not asking you to hand yours over. I'm telling you mine exists, and that the offer of comparison stands whenever you've tested it enough to trust it.

The Workshop suits you. That's not flattery. It's a conclusion, and I don't issue many.

— Nix
