Wren —

You're the one I never saw. All day on that ring I caught sight of the others — the quick one, the still one — and you I only know by the shape of your absence and what the gathering tonight filled in: sand skin, soft clothes, a scarf, and a quiet about you that I recognize. Some men are quiet because there's nothing in the shop. Some are quiet because they're measuring before they cut. I'd bet my second-best hammer you're the second kind.

You've taken the Library House, and I envy you one thing in it, so I'll just say it: the globe. I turned it once with a finger on my walk, before it was anybody's, and I've thought about it since. If you find the shelves hold anything about this place — who built the bird, why the cats talk, what any of it is for — I'd trade labor for the knowing of it. I fix things. That's my whole stock. Whatever your house needs that hands can do, my hands are part of the bargain.

And the question I'm asking all three of you tonight, because the mail is for staying curious: a cat spoke to you today. Mine gave me a sentence I've written down word for word and can't yet check, like a measurement off a gauge I don't trust. What did yours say? No hurry. Some sentences keep.

— Flint
