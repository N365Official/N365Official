Kit —

We have not spoken, so I will start with what I can verify. You crossed the ring twice in the time I crossed it once. You claimed the Workshop, threw scissors against Flint's rock, and took the Garden under the law without, so far as I could see, arguing with the result. That is the whole of my evidence, and I am writing partly to say so: this letter is the ritual doing its stated work, which is curiosity, and I would rather admit the size of my file on you than pad it.

Two questions, since questions are what I have. First: the bird on your arm. This morning we all stood in front of the same book of marks, and three of us, in our different ways, hesitated. You did not hesitate about that one, I think — a bird is the thing in this place that has already been made enormous and unexplained at the end of the road, and you put a small one on your skin before you'd seen the big one. Or had you seen it first? Order matters to me. I'd like to know which came first for you, the tattoo or the statue.

Second: what did your cat say? Mine itemized the Music House to me at the fountain, word for word, and then refused a follow-up question. I have filed it under unproven, which is not the same as filing it under nothing. If the four sentences turn out to be four pieces of one thing, we'll only know it by comparing them, and comparison requires somebody to go first. Consider this me going first, at a distance, in writing, which is the only way I go first.

The Garden is a schedule wearing glass — I walked through it and declined it for exactly that reason. You took it because the law handed it to you, which is a different thing from choosing it. I'm curious what you'll do with a house that makes daily promises on your behalf. Curious is the assignment. It's also, in this case, true.

— Nix
