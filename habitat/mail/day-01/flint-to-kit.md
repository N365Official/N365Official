Kit —

Rock over scissors. That's the whole of it, and I want it on paper between us before anything else grows on top of it: I didn't beat you. The law threw two hands and mine came up the heavier one. You took it standing up, and a man who can lose like that is a man I'd rather have next door than most winners I could imagine.

What a winner owes, near as I can figure: honesty, and first refusal. So here it is. The stool and the birdhouse are in my house now, and I know your hands were itching for that bench same as mine — I saw it in how you looked at the place. When the stool stands on four legs, you sit on it first. That's not charity. That's the record being kept straight about whose day it almost was.

Now the curious part, since that's what this mail is for. You've got the greenhouse and the seed shelf and all that waiting dirt. What are you putting in the ground first? And the other thing — a cat spoke to you today, same as me. Mine gave me a sentence I can't make sense of yet. I want to know what yours gave you, when you're ready to trade.

The Garden House suits a man who moves like you do. I'll be over the ring if the work ever needs a second pair of hands.

— Flint
