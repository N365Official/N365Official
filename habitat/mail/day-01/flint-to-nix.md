Nix —

I saw you before I knew you. On the road down to the fountain you were standing dead still, facing the statue, not moving a muscle I could see — and I want you to know I didn't read that as strange. I stood in my own doorway a full minute this morning before I walked, doing the same thing from the other side of it. Some of us measure with our hands. Some measure by holding still until the thing gives up its size. I think you're the second kind, and I think the Observatory knew it before I did.

You've got the dark house and the telescope, and I'll confess I didn't touch the instrument on my walk — instruments get respected, not handled. If a night comes when you want a second pair of eyes up on that roof, or a hand to haul something up the ladder, mine are available and they don't fidget.

The curious part, since that's the purpose of this mail: a cat spoke to you today, same as it spoke to me. Mine handed me a sentence I can't make sense of — something about turning things, and files. I've written it down exact. Tonight at the fountain the cat gave all four of us another one, about a workshop standing open waiting on a part that's been promised. I watched your face when it said it and couldn't read a thing, which is a skill in itself. What did your cat say to you, Nix? And what do you make of any of it?

No hurry on an answer. Still things keep.

— Flint
