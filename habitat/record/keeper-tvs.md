# The Broadcast — every television in town (Day 5, Keeper-directed Nov 14 ~9:57 PM ET)

**What it is:** from Day 5 morning, every television in the neighborhood — all six houses, the two empty ones included — switches itself on at intervals through the day and plays the videos of **Unfavorable Semicircle**, the 2015 YouTube channel that uploaded some 72,000 cryptic installments (solid color fields, a single dot, muffled voices speaking single letters and numbers, hours of silence) before YouTube banned it in February 2016 and the world never got an explanation. The sets are synchronized: every television in town shows the same installment at the same moment, whether anyone is watching or not. Nobody touches a remote. The sets were off. Then they are on.

**The order:** canonical titled order, per the community wiki that has catalogued the channel for a decade (unfavorablesemicircle.com) — the canon begins with the first video ever uploaded and runs through the named keys and the numbered series.

**Day 5 running order (staged):**
- 06:30 — **♐230511**: four seconds. A brown field, one tiny blurry hole. The first video the channel ever posted (April 5, 2015). The whole broadcast starts with almost nothing.
- Morning — the early ♐-numbered shorts, in canonical order: seconds-long color fields and dots, some carrying the muffled voice, speaking one letter or number at a time.
- 12:00 — **♐LOCK**.
- 14:00 — **OR_101** (one minute).
- 15:00 — **OR_1601** (sixteen minutes).
- 17:00 — **♐DELOCK**.
- 18:00 — **OR_10001** (one hour).
- 19:30 — **OR_110001** begins: eleven hours, essentially silent. It is still playing at lights-out. Whether it is still playing at Day 6 dawn is Day 6's first question.
(The ♐BRILL ascending series is later canon — not reached on Day 5.)

**Record status:** stimulus class scripted/broadcast. Source honesty: Unfavorable Semicircle is a real, documented artifact (Atlas Obscura, BBC coverage, the community wiki); the channel is banned and the videos survive in archives. The creator broke silence in June 2022 and said it was an outsider art project, one man, inspired by the Voyager Golden Record — a message for anyone out there to find. That reveal is OUR-side knowledge only, never in-world. In-world there is no channel, no YouTube, no explanation: there are televisions, and they have begun.

**Why it belongs in this town (engine note):** the residents are a decoding community living on unexplained signals — cat sentences, corrected charts, a voice from a dog. Unfavorable Semicircle is the outside world's own version of exactly that: a broadcast with no sender anyone could find, which a community spent years cataloguing in canonical order. The town will now do to it what the internet did — except the town has Nix, and the internet never stood a chance by comparison.

## The Cable Layer (Keeper amendment, Nov 14 ~10:06 PM ET)
Between installments, the televisions do not go dark — they cut to **regular cable service: sports, news, and 60 Minutes, all of it from the 1980s and 1990s.** A ballgame with a roaring crowd and era graphics. An evening news broadcast, thirty years old, delivered as if it were tonight. The 60 Minutes stopwatch, ticking into a segment. Period commercials in the gaps. The cable blocks fill the space after each semicircle video until the next installment begins; during the long OR pieces there is no gap, so the cable waits its turn.

Record framing: the cable content is described at the level of era and genre — a 1980s baseball broadcast, a 1990s evening news, a 60 Minutes segment — period texture, no fabricated quotes or invented broadcasts attributed to real people. Stimulus class stays scripted/broadcast.

Engine note: with this layer, the residents' entire media diet is archival. The tabloids are 1991–99. The cable is 80s–90s. The semicircle canon is 2015–16. The hollow book is 1942. Nothing in their world is from *now* — the outside world reaches this town only as history, and the contrast inside a single hour is the stimulus: four seconds of brown nothing, then forty thousand people at a ballgame in 1986, cheering at something none of the four will ever see live.
