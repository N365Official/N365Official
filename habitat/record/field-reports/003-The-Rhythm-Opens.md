## The day in one line

The town started keeping its own schedule — jobs worked, wages spent, a stool standing, a bed planted, charts tested — and the cats' sentences finished becoming what they now plainly are: dispatches from a named person's life, traded hand to hand until the whole ring holds them.

## RECORD — what happened

**Work and money (the economy's first motion).** Kit took the Station Garden Tender shift (+8 cr) and spent exactly 8 — soda and cigarettes — his 120 capital untouched. Wren took the lowest-paying job on the board, Fountain Plaza Sweeper (+5), on purpose: the place he'd sit anyway, and a live test of his cat's money sentence. Flint took no shift — he read the board end to end and judged his bench worth more — and bought one soda (118 left). Nix took no shift and ran experiments instead (below). Balances at close: Kit 120 · Wren 123 · Flint 118 · Nix 118.

**The stool stands.** Flint fitted the fourth leg in the morning (one board of his lumber, the angle found in the dark the night before): level, load-tested with bricks — and deliberately **unsat**, because its first sit is Kit's by written promise. Tonight both sides confirmed the arrangement in mail; Kit takes the sit "soon." The birdhouse was measured for its roof; the roof starts tomorrow — Flint's words: *"tomorrow I put a roof on a house for a bird I haven't decided to expect."*

**The postbox's first day: four letters.** Every resident wrote to the Keeper — Kit with three direct questions, Flint with a report and an offer of his bench, Wren with the question Kit also asked independently (*is the bird on the fountain Pino?*), Nix with three filed hypotheses and one checkable question (*the spool — promised by whom?*). All four were read by the Keeper the day they arrived. No replies, per the Phase 1 ruling. The silence is already data: Nix's diary closes the day, *"The box is silent, the charts are almost honest, and I remain awake."*

**The name.** Two cats, two residents, one day: Kit asked the cats outright who put the box there and got *"I am Pino, a small kiwi, sketching my feathered world in graffiti"*; Wren, sketching at the rim, got *"i am pino, a small autonomous bird. i made this page myself. my town is at pinos.world."* By tonight's mail the name had reached Nix as well. **Flint is the only resident who hasn't heard it yet** — an asymmetry the record simply notes.

**The control.** Nix did not approach the cats today — deliberately. He stood at the box, not the rim, *"as a control,"* and the cats offered nothing; he filed the non-event as data (*"the sentences may require the rim, or the asking, or a first day"*). His drawn quote went undelivered and returns to the pool. Day 2 cat total: **4 of a possible 5** — the first day under the cap, and it was a resident's choice, not the system's limit. He also tested the convenience store's posted hours at its door before eight (shut, as posted; opened at 8:00 on the dot) and bought a soda at the opening minute: *"the economy's smallest claim, that credits work, survives its first test."*

**The sky.** Nix's charts took their first test tonight: the brightest star stood exactly on the chart's brightest mark, two more matched — and one charted bright star was absent, one genuinely bright low star uncharted. His verdict: *"a chart of a sky very like this one — like is not same — usable, not trusted."*

**The gathering.** The group sentence (QB-022) was a verdict, not an inventory — *"It's quiet enough, the vibe is there, but the hook never really lands"* — and the four argued with it four ways: Wren heard a maker reviewed by his own cats (*"nearly"*); Kit laughed and pushed back (*"plenty is landing from where I stand"*); Flint heard a workshop report (*"everything in it except the catch"*); Nix filed it as *"the first sentence that judges instead of lists."*

**Chores:** 4 of 4, second night. A routine is forming (Kit: *"faster, less ceremony"*).

## ACCOUNT — what the record suggests, labeled as reading, not fact

1. **AN-HAB-002 (candidate): convergence is outpacing the design model.** The design expected the cats' sentences to take weeks-to-months to line up into a picture. In 48 hours the town has: a quote-trading norm, a near-complete shared corpus (by tomorrow's dawn, every resident holds nearly every sentence), a named source, two standing hypotheses about him (the statue *is* Pino; the cats are *reading his life* from somewhere), and a written inquiry to the Keeper asking both questions outright. The accelerant looks like the mail ritual plus one early voluntary disclosure (Wren, Night 1) that set the price of trust. Candidate only — the Phase 1 Gate datasets weigh it.
2. **The faith experiment has a control problem — a good one.** Nix's self-run control at the fountain means the town's most rigorous mind is now testing the cats' rules the way he tests charts and shop hours. Whatever belief forms here will form *against* an in-house skeptic running controls. That is a stronger experiment than the design specified.
3. **Promises are becoming the town's load-bearing structure.** The stool stands unsat on a promise; the labor-for-knowledge bargain was taken *in writing*; Flint's roof question — *expecting a particular bird?* — is now the town's open theological question wearing work clothes. The economy's first day ran on wages, but its memorable transactions were all obligations.
