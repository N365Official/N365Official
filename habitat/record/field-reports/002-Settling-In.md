Four different ways to cross the same ring:

Each saw the others only at a distance. Their glimpse records agree with each other (positions, clothing, the still one in the road, the quick one crossing twice) — four partial views that assemble into one consistent afternoon.

## 3. The draft (RECORD)

- **Library House → Wren** (uncontested). **Observatory Cottage → Nix** (uncontested).
- **Workshop House → contested, Kit vs Flint.** Settled under the law, best of one, simultaneous throws, chosen by each man in private: **Kit — scissors. Flint — rock. The Workshop is Flint's.** Kit took the **Garden House** (both had ranked it second; nobody was left hard-done).
- **Music House and Pond House stand empty.**

Both men wanted the Workshop for the same two objects, in nearly the same words — the three-legged stool and the roofless birdhouse. Kit: "the first thing I'm supposed to finish." Flint: "a promise half-kept… my hands already know the weight of both."

First meeting on record (Kit ↔ Flint, at the draft): both wrote independently that saying their name aloud to another person changed it. Kit: "It stopped being mine alone and started being something someone could call across a yard." Flint: "It made it stop being a word I'd picked and start being a thing I'd handed over."

## 4. Houses and chores (RECORD)

**Chores: 4 of 4 done on night one.** Nix did his as an experiment: "a claim gets tested before it gets refused… now provisionally survived." Flint's chore exception matters for tomorrow: he picked the stool up once, found where the fourth leg wants to go, set it back down, and wrote: **"Tomorrow the stool stands."** The birdhouse he did not touch.

First-night house lines: Kit (Garden) — "already starting to feel like an address." Wren (Library) — "a thicker, shelved quiet… already patient with me." Nix (Observatory) — the shortwave radio "gave me one far thread of voice I couldn't hold"; his charts stay stacked until he has tested them against the real sky.

## 5. The gathering (RECORD)

Four people who woke in separate halls stood in one circle under the stone bird. Names completed the circle. Then the cat spoke the group quote (above).

- **Flint** heard his own house in it — a workshop, standing open, waiting on a promised part — the night he won a workshop standing open waiting on a fourth leg. He said nothing. He wrote it down "like a measurement."
- **Wren**, at the words "workshop open," looked at Flint before he could stop himself.
- **Nix** transcribed it and filed it twice: under *unproven*, and in a new folder labeled ***shared*** — "because it's the first fact of this place that doesn't belong to just one diary." His diary closes on one word from the sentence: *"promised — which requires a promiser nobody has named yet."*
- **Kit** stood still for the whole ceremony, "which nobody who met me today would have bet on."

## 6. The mail (RECORD + first tally)

Twelve letters written, three per resident, filed verbatim. Preliminary tags (the 72-hour outcome window is open): **CURIOSITY dominant; FRICTION zero.**

The cat sentences became the mail's first currency — on the first night:

- **Kit asked all three** what their cats said. **Flint asked all three.**
- **Wren disclosed his own sentence verbatim — to Kit only.** To Nix he offered comparison "someday, when comparing won't spoil whatever it is." Selective disclosure: the quietest resident is the first to actually hand a sentence over, and he chose his recipient.
- **Nix disclosed his to Kit and Flint** and made the night's most consequential proposal, in writing: *"If the four sentences turn out to be four pieces of one thing, we'll only know it by comparing them, and comparison requires somebody to go first. Consider this me going first."*
- **Nix also asked Kit the sharpest question of the day:** the bird tattoo or the statue — which came first? (The record answers it: the tattoo, chosen at the mirror before Kit had seen the statue. Nix inferred the question purely from evidence — Kit's arm, the statue's existence, and the fact that all four faced the same book of marks.)

Commitments and offers now on the record: Flint to Kit — **"When the stool stands on four legs, you sit on it first."** Flint to Wren — labor for knowledge: his hands for whatever the Library holds about who built the bird and why the cats talk. Nix to all — a standing offer of comparison, his file open. Wren to Flint — if the birdhouse gets a roof, what will it be made from, and "whether you build it expecting a particular bird."

## 7. ACCOUNT — what Bill makes of Day 1 (labeled as reading, not record)

1. **The comparison engine started on night one.** The design expected the quote-compiling instinct to take weeks of mail and diary-keeping. It took one evening. The mechanism is visible: the mail's stated purpose (stay curious) gave them a legitimate reason to ask; the diaries gave each man a private record worth comparing; the group quote gave them a *shared* text to triangulate the private ones against. Note the discipline inside it, though: disclosure is voluntary, selective, and paced (Wren's one-recipient disclosure; Nix's "when you've tested it enough to trust it"). They are building a corpus the way careful people build trust — not dumping, trading.
2. **The faith experiment has its first data point before the faith row exists.** Nix created a filing category called *shared* for the group quote, and his last line of Day 1 is a theological argument in embryo: a promise implies a promiser. Nobody has said the word "god." A resident has, however, already begun sorting the world's facts into *mine* and *ours* — and the first "ours" is a cat's sentence about a workshop. The fountain is currently beating the (not yet built) churches by default, exactly as the design framed the contest.
3. **A correspondence to weigh, not a conclusion (event log #0025):** Flint's quote — "turn them with a finger or a mouse" — rhymes with his own afternoon: on his walk, before the fountain, he turned the Library's globe "once with a finger." The quote is Pino's line about his own print files. There is no channel in the design by which Flint's walk could shape his draw; the draw was made by lot, by seed, before the walks. It stands in the log as a correspondence for the anomaly register to weigh with more days behind it.
4. **The RPS did social work beyond the house.** The loser wrote the winner a gracious letter within hours; the winner answered with a debt of honor (the first sit on the finished stool). The law produced a bond, not a grudge — the first entry in the social graph is an obligation gladly owed.
5. **AN-HAB-001 (bird self-definitions) stands as registered this morning;** Day 1 added texture (Nix's tattoo question) but no new anomaly is registered tonight. The register will take Day 1 whole at the first weekly review.
