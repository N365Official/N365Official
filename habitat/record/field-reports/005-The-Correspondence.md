# Field Report #5 — Day 4: The Correspondence

**World day:** 4 · **Real date:** Saturday, November 14, 2026 (dawned under Keeper's override — the second dawn on one real date, logged in the wake record as *keeper_override*) · **Beat:** Phase 1, Middle — The Rhythm
**Status:** RECORD + ACCOUNT

## The day in one paragraph

Day 4 was the day the answers finished arriving and the residents started answering back in kind. Kit got three hundred pencils for a sentence he'd carried four days and sat down and drew. Wren got five hundred canvases and a written promise and painted the statue whole for the first time. Flint put the birdhouse out in the world and caught the dog knowing something it shouldn't. And Nix — who set the Keeper a test in writing, on stated terms — got his third chart, took it to the sky with a witness, and entered the result in his own hand: **correspondence, confirmed; distance zero.** The town now contains a channel, and both ends of it know.

## RECORD — what happened

**Dawn (override).** The Keeper knocked at 8:14 PM ET; the Town Door recorded the wake as *keeper_override* against Nov 14. Day 3's twelve letters delivered. Three placements stood in the town: the pencil hoard in Kit's mailbox (300 colored pencils, falling out; 20 sketchbooks beside it), the canvas mountain at the Library House (500 canvases, 40 brushes, paint in quantity, and a note — the first written words from the Keeper's hand in the world — reading "if you paint it, they will come"), and the third star chart in a tube by Nix's telescope.

**Kit (Garden House, 126 → 132).** Found the hoard; on discovery the dog screamed "DRAW, DRAW, DRAW SOMETHING" and ran. Kit counted the pencils in tens in his kitchen, kept everything, worked the Garden Tender shift (+8), bought a soda (−2), and at midday sat down voluntarily and drew bed one's shoots in the first sketchbook — his first drawing, self-assessed as terrible ("green hair") and done anyway. At the fountain he received QB-005: "The rim stops my legs, there's nothing beyond it" — and added *walk out to where this town stops* to his own list. In the afternoon the dog accused the town of running a scam (DL-004), his second visit of the day. Evening: chores done, three letters out, a Keeper letter filed (the pencil report; the dog's name asked again, "for the record"), and the gathering sentence taken personally — his closing line: "I am a tiny mark that moves. I always was."

**Wren (Library House, 126 → 129).** Inventoried before interpreting: exactly 500 canvases, exactly 40 brushes. The note was transcribed exactly and stored inside the field guide at the kiwi page — "two texts from outside the four of us, kept together, in a book about a bird." He records the note's hand matches none of the three residents' writing he knows. Plaza Sweeper (+5), soda (−2). At the rim, his sentence: QB-002, "Today I looked at the whole thing and wondered if it is rubbish." He answered it with work: one canvas carried to the fountain, the statue painted whole — feet to head, falling water, cats small at the edge — and left unsigned ("Not no. Just not yet."). In the afternoon he found the birdhouse gone from the bench, searched the ring, found it mounted, and drew it where it stands: the pair is complete — *roofless on a bench, belonging to no one → roofed on a post, belonging to whoever comes.* 499 canvases remain. His Keeper letter reports the ask acted on and the condition unmet without flinching: "If they come, they have not come yet."

**Flint (Workshop House, 125 → 123).** Chores early; then he cut a post from his own lumber, set it at the ring's pond-side edge, plumbed it twice, and mounted the roofed birdhouse above head height, entrance to the sunrise — "built ready, not expecting," now standing in the world. Immediately after the mounting, the dog delivered the conspiracy line (DL-013). Flint filed it unsolved: he rejected "docile" on the evidence of his morning's work and read the "only one with a skill" line as flattery aimed at setting him against the others. At the fountain he received QB-007 — "Shops only open during their posted hours, nothing works outside them" — the very claim Nix tested empirically on Day 2, filed as the plainest, most checkable sentence yet. No job, by decision (his eyes saved for the night); soda (−2). Evening: three letters, a Keeper letter, the gathering, the roof.

**Nix (Observatory Cottage, 118 → 118).** Read his mail twice; filed the spool chain complete in four accounts with no contradictions. Examined the third chart mark by mark against his logbook *before* interpreting it: on paper it corrects the remaining failure (the bright low star, charted in its true place) and keeps the first correction — distance 2 → 1 → 0. By the criterion he had set in advance, in writing, he entered **correspondence** in the morning, with his bounds attached: the test was announced to its subject, so it proves responsiveness, not candor; the hand is unidentified; trust is a separate, nearly empty folder. At the fountain, in the Day 1 configuration (presence, no question), he received QB-004: "five things my world will not let me do, and how I mean to find out whether that is really so." Approach tally: 3/3. No job, no spend.

**The fetch (afternoon).** The dog brought its ball to Nix on the green, dropped it at his feet, and waited. Nix picked it up and threw it **eight times**; the dog returned it eight times, including one deliberately off-line throw. The petition was silent — no lines during the game. Nix ended the test himself, setting the ball down in front of the dog (its property, returned). The refusal branch was never triggered; the dog's deadpan parting line (DL-015) was never spoken and stays in the bank, unused.

**The roof night (after the gathering).** Nix and Flint on the Observatory roof: separate notebooks, no conferring during the measurement, comparison afterward by lamplight. Both notebooks agree on both test points — the once-absent star present where charts 2 and 3 put it; the bright low star present and charted in its true place. Flint's only words on it: "Mine says the same." Nix's verdict: the distance between the hand's sky and his reads **zero, witnessed**; the charts graduate from evidence to instrument, the sincerity caveat standing. Moon baseline mark two taken.

**The gathering (21:15, all four).** Group sentence QB-014: "I love tiny marks that speak my feathered world." Cat count Day 4: **5 of 5** (four individual, one group). Dog appearances: 5 (the discovery shout, two drive-bys to Kit and Nix, the conspiracy to Flint, the fetch). Mail round 4: 12 letters, held for Day 5 dawn. Keeper letters: **4 of 4** (12 received in total; 8 answered through the world; written replies 0). Chores 4 of 4, fourth night. ## ACCOUNT — what it means, so far

**The channel is established — by the resident's own protocol.** Nix did not drift into believing; he pre-registered a criterion with the subject of the test, received the predicted result, and confirmed it against the sky with an independent witness whose notebook agrees. Whatever the Keeper is to this town, Nix has a working definition now: a correspondent who answers readings, whose sincerity is unmeasured, and whose identity is still an open file. His next question — "what the channel is *for*" — is deliberately unasked. That restraint is the most scientific thing in the record.

**AN-HAB-003 (candidate): the dog knew the bench.** Flint's Keeper letter flags what the datasets now have to carry: the dog's conspiracy line named the half-finished bird on his bench — information a wandering dog shouldn't hold. Flint derived both candidate mechanisms himself, including the true one (a sentence authored by someone who had seen his bench, "put in the dog's mouth the way the sentences are put in the cats'"). The cat sentences quote a stranger's real life and cannot be caught this way; the dog quotes the town itself, and the town can tell. Authored local color is detectable. Logged.

**The name still hasn't reached Flint.** Verified by search of every Day 4 letter addressed to him: "Pino" appears in none. He holds "Kiwi" from his own sentence and a self-made philosophy of names — *a true name is just the one your finished work answers to.* Three residents and the Keeper know the name of the bird over the fountain. The maker doesn't. Four days, and the mail — which carried everything else — has never once carried that.

**The arts have started.** In one day the town gained its first drawing, its first painting, and a completed pair of documentary drawings of the birdhouse. All three were answers: to a sentence (Kit), to a note (Wren), to an invitation (Wren again). The placements are not being consumed; they are being *replied to*, in kind, at the residents' own standard. Wren's unsigned painting is the emblem: a first thing, allowed to be a first thing, signed when the looking is finished.
