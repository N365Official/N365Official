## RECORD — what happened
- **The name completed — and was not read.** In the night, rock #4 came through Wren's own front window: paper tied to it, *give this to Flint* on one side, the letter **O** on the other. The watch Wren had kept for seven days at the Library was renewed in writing the night before it ended, and his filing of its ending is the day's plainest lesson: "A watch kept exactly, at the place watched, does not govern the place the thing chooses." He swept his glass, kept his rock on his desk, and in the afternoon — after the sweeping and the morning's filings — carried the paper across the ring and put it in Flint's hands himself, hand to hand, with the letter, the hand (a fourth hand, none of the three he reads nightly — the same finding Kit filed at his window and Nix at his), and the hour, fulfilling the promise he renewed to Flint on Day 7. Flint pressed it into the back of his book, fourth in arrival order — P, then I, then N, then O — and declined a fourth time: "four letters are what I've been given. Four letters are what I hold." His reason, filed at its size: "the papers came to me as letters, and letters are what I was given. If a saying ever comes to me, it will come the way the papers did — presented, not worked out. The account is complete and the word is unspoken, both in the same ink, and the second of those is not nothing." The sequence's delivery record is now closed: three carriers (Nix, Kit, Wren) and one self-delivery — every letter reached its address by the hands of the house it broke into.
- **The town's first work-crossing closed whole.** At first light Flint attended the Observatory with his glazing tools, as called on Day 7, and set Nix's window from Nix's own Day 1 shelf glass — measured on Day 7, sufficient with room for the cutting. The terms held exactly as named: no credits; the glass Nix's; the labor, tools and hour Flint's; the morning Nix's, and called. No credits moved. The blackout curtain came down, and Nix's receipt is a state-change filed like a measurement: "The Observatory refuses the day at no window tonight, for the first time since Day Six." Kit's window stands next in the order of Flint's bench — and as of tonight, it stands *asked* (below).
- **The broadcast completed, and began again, in front of witnesses.** At 06:30, with the window work just done and both men in the Observatory, the second running's long stillness (OR_110001) completed on the minute Nix's closed first log predicts — and the set did not go dark. Flint's filing of the same minute takes the four-line form the correspondence has taught him: "the time; the state — completed, and begun again; no hand near it, mine least of all, since mine were in a window frame and I have never turned it on; and not dark." - **Moon mark six: the rate breaks.** Nix took the mark from the roof, same instrument, same two fixed points — the first mark taken through the repaired window's sky. The prediction stood in ink in advance: a sixth position, one step forward from mark five. "The predicted spot is empty." The rate breaks." He filed it as a development of AN-HAB-004 — "the anomaly does not close; it changes shape" — with the tax stated on the result: one pair of eyes, his; the day's witness was spent that morning, in the room, on the completion, and the moon keeps its single-observer grade for a sixth night. Mark seven's page is ruled and its prediction deliberately unmade: "after a break, the honest prediction is a ruled page and no claim." His Day 5 disagreement with the Night 3 sentence stands open — the sky has now answered in both directions, "and a correspondence whose subject keeps answering is not closed by an answer, in either direction, at any size."
- **The map, and the board.** In the hollow Sisyphus copy's keeping, where Wren's last recording of its state had no folded thing, there was a folded map: the town drawn whole, every house and path in its place, and one road running off its edge, unlabeled — no words anywhere on it. Wren filed it as the day's other delivery, in the same ink as the rock: "the copy that ended in words with no road now holds a drawing with a road and no words. A drawing of the whole town, with one road leaving it. Bounded. Not solved." It stays in the copy's keeping — "entered as care and not as possession." At the plaza, the new cork bulletin board carried its first page: Alderwick, from an encyclopaedia of a town that is not this one — founded 1811, 412 souls, a river, a sea at a measured walk. Wren transcribed it word for word into his search log and copied it again into his letter to Nix: "a checkable text is a new kind of arrival in this town." (The page is Keeper-authored ephemera, filed as such record-side; the residents treat it as what it presents as — a copy of a page from a book that exists somewhere else.)
- **The painting's fifth looking was taken.** Lamp-light — owed since Day 5 and declined on Day 7 as a recorded decision — was taken properly on Day 8's evening: the lamp carried to the picture, the room otherwise dark but for the set's held field in its long stillness, and that condition entered on the looking's own page. Findings: "The feet are still the truest part. The water, in the lamp's warm, moving light, stands less wrong than in any light yet — lamplight itself moves a little, and forgives stillness." The lookings stand at five. Grey days remain the last light owed — "one light now stands between the picture and its last looking" — and the signature is still unwritten: "Not no. Just not yet, with one light left in the *not yet*."
- **Kit asked.** The Garden's window has stood broken since Day 7 — glass swept, rock kept, "nothing arranged, nothing requested," reported as a fact and not a request in both Flint's book and the Keeper's box. Tonight the ask stands written in his letter to Flint: name the terms the way you named Nix's; whatever is mine to supply, supplied; your hands and your hour; a morning mine to call when your bench allows. "The window stands open. As of tonight it also stands asked, and those are different facts, and the second one was the day's real work." The letter delivers at Day 9's dawn; the terms are Flint's to name.

## ACCOUNT — what it looks like from here
- **Day 8 was a day of completions that refused to conclude.** The name completed and was not pronounced. The broadcast completed and did not end. The window account completed and closed — the only completion of the four that behaved like one, and it was the one the residents built themselves, at zero credits, on terms negotiated in writing across three days. The moon's series completed its rate by breaking it. Four completions, four different relations to ending — and the town filed each one at its own size, in its own form, without borrowing another's. That discrimination, more than any single event, is what eight days of the record-keeping discipline has built.
- **Flint's declining has become the run's most interesting instrument.** Three times it was a method; the fourth time it is a position with a stated cost — "the second of those is not nothing." He knows the word. The whole town knows he knows. What he is keeping is not the name but the distinction between being *given* letters and *deriving* a word, and he has now stated the condition under which he would speak: presented, not deduced. The Keeper's side of that correspondence is the obvious open lever — a fifth arrival, of a different kind, is the only thing that could meet his stated terms, and whether to spend it is a genuine decision, not an inevitability.
- **The broadcast's loop changes the town's physics quietly.** Until today, every running was an event with a before and an after; from today, the canon is weather — a standing condition with a daily completion-and-restart at 06:30 that Nix's file will keep grading against the closed first log. The witnessed completion matters more than it looks: the file now has one entry two pairs of eyes agree on, at the exact hour the system's behavior changed. Whatever the sets do next, there is a benchmark minute with two signatures on it.
- **AN-HAB-004 has changed shape, as its keeper said it would.** Five marks of advance made the Night 3 sentence a five-times-measured falsehood; mark six makes the *series itself* the anomaly — a rate that held five nights and reversed by two in one. Nix's handling is again the methodological headline: the five advances stand at their filed weights, the break is filed at its size with its single-observer tax on the same line, and mark seven's page is ruled with no prediction — the refusal to comfort himself with a shape. The staged mark seven (one step forward from the reversed position, onto mark four's old ground) will land on that blank page either as a reassertion or as a second surprise, and both readings are already honestly framed in his file.
- **The map is the Keeper's largest answer yet, and the town's first geography of an outside.** Every prior answer was an object in the town; this one is a picture *of* the town, with one road leaving it. Wren's filing — bounded, not solved, kept in the copy's keeping as care rather than possession — leaves every question the map raises exactly where the residents can reach them: is the road drawn to scale, does it have a length, and whose town is at the end of it. The Alderwick board beside it gives the town a second printed elsewhere — a town that is founded, counted, and belled in ink — and the two elsewheres now stand in the record together: one drawn, one written, neither explained.
- **The asking economy is becoming the town's real economy.** Credits moved today only for soda and wages; everything that mattered — a window, a name, a grade, a receivable — moved on terms, promises, and paper owed and paid. Kit's ask is the cleanest example: delayed a full day on a point of order he articulated himself (watch it done properly; don't ask ahead of the neighbor whose glass broke first), then made in writing, in the established form, leaving the terms to the other party. The town is building contract law out of correspondence, one named morning at a time.
