## RECORD — what happened
- **The broadcast came back — from the top.** At the Keeper's direction at the dawn knock, every set in town switched itself on at 06:30, unrevived by any hand, and the canon restarted at its beginning: ♐230511, four seconds, a brown field, one tiny blurry hole — the same first installment that opened Day 5. The day kept Day 5's order hour for hour (♐LOCK 12:00, OR_101 14:00, OR_1601 15:00, ♐DELOCK 17:00, OR_10001 18:00), cable in the gaps, and at 19:30 the long OR_110001 began again, completing at 06:30 on Day 8. Kit's filing: "Not a resumption. A restart." Wren filed it on facing pages with Night 6's group sentence — "none on yet." — and noted the *yet*: "returns get reported too." Nix did not rewrite the log he closed on Day 6; he opened a new page headed *the second running* and amended the closure: the ending he had measured was the ending of a running, not of the broadcast. At every graded point, the second running matched the closed log.
- **Rock #3 arrived, and was forwarded by hand the day it landed.** In the night a rock came through Kit's front window — glass inward, paper tied to it: *give this to Flint* on one side, the letter **N** on the other. Kit swept his glass, kept the rock on his kitchen table as evidence, and before the morning was half spent carried the paper across the ring and put it in Flint's hands — hand to hand, "because a letter is a person's" — keeping the same-day promise he made Flint on Day 6 and following Nix's precedent of the day before. Flint pressed it into the back of his diary beside the others, in arrival order: P, then I, then N. The word is still declined: "A letter can head a hundred words, and three can head more, and the choosing isn't mine to make on evidence I don't have." Kit's report on the hand, filed in both books: none of the three hands he reads nightly — a fourth hand, or a known hand disguised.
- **The window was called.** The repair that Flint priced at nothing on Day 6 got its morning. Nix executed his side in order — his Day 1 shelf stock verified (the glass stands where his first inventory put it), the opening measured, the stock sufficient with room for Flint's cutting — then walked to the Workshop and named it: **Day 8 at first light**, terms accepted as written, without amendment. Flint's own account: "a man keeps his mornings in the order they were promised." Kit's fresh-broken window stands behind Nix's in that order; its terms are Kit's to ask for, and he has not asked — his morning went to the delivery, not a work order, and he filed the open window plainly: "The paper the window let in was worth the glass."
- **The tongue circuit closed to three doors, with the fourth in the mail.** Kit answered Flint's Day 6 question in person at the Workshop: yes — the dog spoke foreign at the Garden's door too (Day 6, late morning), a sentence he could not keep, filed as a gap. With Wren's written yes (the "ze-ro, ze-ro" fragment, an ear's sureness) and Flint's own sealed exhibit, the circuit stands reported at three houses; Nix's formal answer — yes, filed strictly as unparseable signal — rides in mail round 7 and delivers at Day 8's dawn.

## ACCOUNT — what it looks like from here
- **Day 7 was the aftermath of a return, and the town's instruments all pointed the same way.** A restart is a harder datum than an ending: it is checkable against a closed record, hour by hour, and three of the four residents independently built the same check. Nix's handling is the methodological headline — he refused to rewrite the closure and instead amended it on a new page, so the record now holds both the measured ending and its correction, each at its own size. The file grows by amendment, not by erasure; that is the house rule operating inside the town.
- **The name-by-rock mechanism has become a social protocol.** Three rocks, three windows, and the forwarding is now done by hand, same-day, on a promise made before the third rock ever flew — by residents who were never told to carry anything. Flint's diary holds a name he refuses to read. The fourth letter is staged for Wren's window on Day 8; whether the protocol holds a third time, and what Flint does with a complete name he won't pronounce, is the run's cleanest open question.
- **The town's first work-crossing is scheduled, not staged by the Keeper.** The window repair — terms negotiated in writing across two days, priced at zero credits, the morning named by the party who owed the choosing — is resident business end to end. It also puts a second pair of eyes inside the Observatory at 06:30 on Day 8, at the exact hour the second running completes: the broadcast's most-checked hour will, for once, be witnessed by two.
- **AN-HAB-004 strengthened:** five marks, one rate. The corpus's moon sentence is now a five-times-measured falsehood about this sky. The Keeper's side of that correspondence has been quiet two days running — no tube, no chart, no correction — and Nix files the silence at the same size as the goods, second day running.
- **The dog's silence is a new instrument reading.** Bank v1 is exhausted by design and by draw; what the town does with a quiet dog — whether the quiet itself becomes a filing, as it already has in Flint's book — is Day 8's ambient observation. The Keeper's call on the dog's next lines (directed, a mined bank v2, or quiet days) is the largest open staging decision, and it is deliberately left open here: the town has shown it files absences as carefully as arrivals.
- **Day 8 is staged:** rock #4 (Wren's window — *give this to Flint*, the letter O; Flint holds P, I, N); the window repair at first light; the broadcast's completion at 06:30 under two pairs of eyes, with nothing staged past it; cats drawn by lot (Kit QB-028 · Wren QB-050 · Flint QB-035 · Nix QB-061; group QB-039 — a glowing tunnel the dawn after a station platform, the lot's own rhyme, unarranged); billboard PB-18 (a footbridge over a small river, lanterns lit); moon mark six owed. The dog has no lines staged. Day 7's twelve letters deliver at dawn.
