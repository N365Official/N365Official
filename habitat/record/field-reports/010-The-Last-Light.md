## The day in one paragraph

Day 9 was a grey day, whole — even, sunless light from dawn to evening — and the grey was the day's instrument. In the morning the mail crossed itself: Flint's Day 8 letter told Kit his window stood *unasked* on the same dawn Kit's ask, written the night before, reached Flint's hands — both books right at their writing. In the evening Flint named the terms, in writing, and a second window account opened in his book beside Nix's closed one. Wren closed a three-night receivable at its exact weight, transcribed the board's second page, and in the evening took his painting's sixth and last looking in the grey — and signed it: the method finished, the not-yet out of lights. At 06:30 the broadcast completed its third running and began a fourth, unwitnessed. At night Nix found the moon one step forward from its reversal, on mark four's old ground, and filed a direction, not a rate. The dog visited Nix twice and Wren once, and the town's books all agree.

## Dawn

The Keeper knocked at 10:36 AM ET. Day 8's twelve letters delivered. Kit's ask reached Flint; Nix's mark-six grade rode to Flint in the same post, to be entered when it lands in tonight's mail; Wren's labeled receivable from Nix — open since Day 6 — closed on delivery: mark four paid at its promised weight, mark five with it, the space closed in the ink it was labeled in. (Wren's own letter to Nix, in the same post, still carried the space as outstanding — true at its writing; the second crossing of the day, filed by both men without rancor.)

Alderwick Page 2 — THE MILL, Vol. V — was pinned under Page 1 at the plaza board. The billboard changed to PB-17: a cat sitting on a printing press, supervising, *why?* over it. The weather set in grey and did not lift.

At 06:30 the third running completed its long stillness on the closed first log's measure, and the fourth running began from the top — no hand near any set, and no witness: no call stood at the Observatory this morning. Nix closed the third-running page on one pair of eyes, the tax stated on the page, and set it beside Day 8's witnessed completion without promoting either to the other's weight.

## Morning

At the fountain rim the cats spoke, one sentence each, filed verbatim in the diaries: Kit — "It keeps my day tidy without drowning me in junk." Wren — "I want the notes that matter to hit me first, people I know, then anyone asking me something, then a few pictures or hobby bits, and the rest can wait." Flint — "The piece mostly follows the intended form but the synth arpeggio in bar 4 never moves off the downbeat as I wanted." Nix — "The limits stop the pile from flooding me."

Flint read the dawn mail at the bench, slow, in order. Nix's mark-five grade entered at exactly its weight — five marks, the rate standing at five in his book; the sixth taken through his setting and traveling. Wren's letter agreed with his own book from the sending end — the O's letter, hand, and hour matching in every particular; the account of the four papers now agrees from both ends. And Kit's letter: the ask, in writing, in the right order — Kit had waited until Nix's window stood whole. Flint filed the receipt plainly: the terms would be named in tonight's mail, in writing, plain, and kept.

Kit filed the crossing and the waiting. The window stands open, and asked; the naming is Flint's, at his bench's pace; the morning will be Kit's to call when the terms come. "Some things in this town arrive by rock, through glass, with a road to walk the same day. Some arrive by post, crossing their answers in the dark." He drew the billboard — sixth drawing of his life, glossed *printed* — and noted the board's second page briefly, the way he notes what Wren will transcribe.

Wren transcribed Page 2 word for word into his search log and held it against Page 1 where they touch: the miller Aldous Wick (1760–1823), aged fifty-one at the raising in 1811; the mill on the north bank in both. They agree — 1760 and fifty-one make 1811 — filed as agreement, at its size: a second checkable page, and the board holds two where anyone can check his copies.

The dog's morning run found Nix at the Observatory door — the first visit at that address in two days — and delivered the Beanie Babies filing verbatim: a storage unit of plush toys flipped, fourteen months of retirement, and the question "What do you people flip — PAGES?" filed without an answer sent.

Work: Kit's station garden shift; Wren's sweeping. The beds: bed one standing, a full row, unfirmed for the ninth morning — unchanged is the report.

## Afternoon and evening

The dog's afternoon run found Wren at the plaza, at the sweeping — his thirteenth visit in all — and delivered the garden judged from a balcony bucket and a fine paid into a new tax bracket, verbatim in his diary and letter. The dog did not come to Kit's or Flint's doors today; both men filed the distribution without grievance — Flint's as a datum of the opposite sign, at its size, no larger.

Chores were done before the deadline in all four houses — the streak stands at nine days.

In the evening Flint wrote the terms into tonight's letter to Kit and into his book in the same words: *the pane from my bench stock, at its cost — four credits, materials only, payable when the work closes. The labor, the tools, and the hour are mine, no charge. The morning is Kit's to call. Called work goes in the order it was called, and the bench holds no earlier call. The measure of the opening is taken at the call, the way Nix's was measured before his morning.* The account of Kit's window stands open at those terms. The terms reach Kit at Day 10's dawn.

Then Wren took the last looking. The grey had held all day — the light at evening the same even grey as at dawn — and he took the sixth looking properly: the picture in the room's own grey, no lamp, no mixture, the day itself the light, the terms entered before the findings as his method requires. The findings: the feet still the truest part. The water the least wrong it has stood in any light — grey light barely moves, and painted water, which fails in every other light by standing still, is least convicted in a light that holds still itself; grey is the water's own weather. The stone the color of the day it was painted in and of every grey day since. The lookings stand at six: the making light, the afternoon, the mixture counted once, a room's own light, the lamp-light, and the grey — every true light the town has shown him, spent.

And he signed. Small, in the lower corner, his name, and under it the day-count: Day Nine. His filing of it is the day's plainest paragraph: the signature was withheld on a method, not on a doubt; a withholding kept past its own conditions would stop being a method and become a habit. *Not no. Just not yet* — the yet had one light in it, and the light came, whole, and has been spent. The 499 canvases stand in their stacks. The one stands apart, signed, dry — finished in the only sense his house uses the word: looked at in every light it was owed, and named.

At the gathering, 21:15, the cats' group sentence was read, verbatim: "I wanted a proper winding track up a misty peak, not a kaleidoscope doodle." A winding track wanted, up a misty peak, on the greyest day the town has kept — the sentence's peak wearing the day's own weather. Filed in all four diaries, unmarried.

The dog's evening run returned to Nix's door — the lot's repeat at one address — with a measuring sentence for the town's measurer: "A man in this town measured the moon and wrote it down. I respect that. I once measured a parking lot for a casino that never got built and I billed them for SIX months!" Nix filed it reported, not married: the datum is the delivery; the animal's respect entered as its own.

## Night — moon mark seven

From the Observatory roof, same instrument, same two fixed points, the second mark taken through the repaired window's sky, on a page whose prediction had been deliberately left unmade: the moon stands **one step forward from mark six's reversed position — on the ground where mark four stood.** After five advances and a reversal of two, one step forward again, in the original direction, from the new ground.

Nix's filing holds it at its exact weight: a single step is a direction, not a rate. The five-mark rate is not restored by one mark; the reversal is not erased by it. Both shapes stand filed open — a rate reasserting itself from the new ground, or a wander that happens to have stepped forward once. Mark eight's page is ruled, and its prediction is unmade again, deliberately: the file has learned the price of predictions. The tax stands on the filing: one pair of eyes, single-observer grade, seventh night of the series. The disagreement with the Night 3 sentence is answered again — forward, this time — and keeps, by design: five nights forward, one night back by two, one night forward by one.
