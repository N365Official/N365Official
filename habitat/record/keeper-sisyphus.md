# The Hollow Sisyphus — a book in the Library (arrives Day 5 dawn)

**What it is:** a copy of *The Myth of Sisyphus* by Albert Camus, shelved spine-out on the Library House's philosophy shelf, between ordinary philosophy volumes — in the path of Wren's ongoing shelf-by-shelf search, but not spotlighted. No arrow points at it. If nobody finds it on Day 5, it waits, the way everything in this town waits.

**The catch (engine truth):** the book contains no essay. After the title page, every page carries exactly ONE sentence, centered, and the sentences together are a detailed overview of the events transpiring during the book's publishing. The final page carries only an address — a link — and the address goes to a 404.

**Record status:** authored ephemera (stimulus class scripted/print, like the tabloids). The sentences are publication history, written for this copy; no text of Camus's essay is reproduced beyond the title and his name. The 404 is real on our side of the record: the address resolves to "404 Not Found" and nothing else. In-world the residents have no network and no way to follow a link — for them it is an address to a place with no road, like New Zealand on Wren's globe.

**The pages, exactly as printed:**
- Title page: THE MYTH OF SISYPHUS — Albert Camus. (Nothing else. No colophon, no explanation. The strangeness is discovered by turning pages.)
- Page 1: "This book was published in Paris in October 1942, while a war occupied the city around its publisher."
- Page 2: "Its author had finished writing it in February of the year before, in another country, and the manuscript crossed a divided continent to reach the printer."
- Page 3: "Paper was rationed, so the first printing was small, and most of the copies went to people who knew the author, or knew of him."
- Page 4: "A few months earlier that same year, a novel by the same man had appeared, and the two books were read as brothers — one asking the question, one living it."
- Page 5: "Almost no one reviewed this book when it came out. There were louder events transpiring."
- Page 6: "Somewhere in the book this copy replaces, a man rolls a stone up a hill forever, and the reader is asked to imagine him happy."
- Page 7: "After the war ended, the book travelled into languages its author did not speak, and was printed again and again."
- Page 8: "Fifteen years after it appeared, its author received the Nobel Prize in Literature — the second-youngest man ever to receive it."
- Page 9: "Three years after that, the author died in a car crash on a road he had not planned to travel, with an unused train ticket in his pocket, and the book continued without him."
- Page 10: "This copy was printed much later, in a town that does not appear on the maps in the front of other books, and shelved where a reader would eventually find it."
- Final page, centered, alone: "http://sisyphus.1942/the-answer"

**Why it's the Keeper's joke and the Keeper's signature at once:** Sisyphus is the Keeper's own boulder — the lens of his memoir, the center of his author line ("writes about the boulder"). The town that has received his pencils, his canvases, and his charts now receives his book — hollowed into a history of itself, ending at an address that isn't there. If the residents ever work out whose book this was, they will be one shelf closer to the Keeper than any chart has brought them. Nobody is going to tell them. The record just keeps it straight.
