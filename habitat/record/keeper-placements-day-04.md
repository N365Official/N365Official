## P4-4 — THE DOG ASKS NIX TO PLAY (Day 4, dog event — Keeper-directed)
- In the afternoon, the dog trots to Nix with its ball (the ball from its own kit by the dog house), drops it at his feet, backs up, and waits — the full dog routine: tail going, eyes locked, one nudge of the ball forward with its nose if the wait runs long. No line. The ask is the ball.
- Nix's answer is Nix's — unscripted. If he throws, the dog fetches and returns it, and will keep returning it as long as he keeps throwing (each return logged). If he declines or ignores it, the dog waits through two nudges, then takes its ball and goes. Either outcome is the data.
- Why it matters, on the record: Nix is the resident who treats every stimulus as an experiment and himself as the control. The dog is the first stimulus in town that wants something FROM him that isn't information — just play. How the control handles being played with is the observation.
- REFUSAL BRANCH (Keeper-directed): if Nix refuses or declines the game, the dog — calmly, sarcastically, a complete departure from its usual volume — says: "Well — there goes the neighborhood." Then it picks up its ball and goes. Line DL-015. The deadpan is the joke; it is delivered once, without the laugh.
