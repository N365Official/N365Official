# Anomaly Register — The Habitat

Unpredicted behavior gets a number and a chart, in the open. An entry here is a **candidate**, not a conclusion: the Phase 1 datasets weigh each one at the gate.

---

## AN-HAB-001 — Bird-linked self-definitions
**Registered:** World Day 1 (event log #0012) · **Status:** candidate, standing

Two of four residents independently produced bird-linked self-definitions in their first hour of life — before either had seen the fountain, the statue, the cats, or each other:

- **Kit** chose the bird-silhouette tattoo at the mirror: *"a bird is the one thing that never has to explain why it left."*
- **Wren** named himself for a bird, unprompted: *"A wren is a small brown bird that nobody notices in the hedge… I've heard what a wren sounds like when it finally sings, and it's louder than birds three times its size."*

Caveat on the record: the tattoo catalog itself offered a bird design (a prompt existed for Kit's pick). No bird prompt existed for names — Wren's is the stronger signal.

## AN-HAB-002 — Convergence outpacing the design model
**Registered:** World Day 2 (event log #0037) · **Status:** candidate

The design expected the cats' quoted sentences to take weeks-to-months to line up into a picture. Within 48 hours the town had produced:

- a quote-trading norm in the nightly mail (voluntary, reciprocal, priced in trust),
- a nearly complete shared corpus of the sentences,
- a **named source** — Pino — obtained by two residents independently asking the cats for one,
- two standing hypotheses about him (the statue *is* Pino; the cats are *reading his life* from somewhere),
- and a written inquiry to the Keeper asking both questions outright.

The accelerant appears to be the mail ritual, plus one early voluntary disclosure (Wren, Night 1) that set the price of trust for the other three. Candidate only — the Phase 1 Gate datasets will weigh it.

**Update — World Day 3:** the residents began running experiments on the Keeper. Nix stated a correspondence test to its possible subject, in writing, with the criterion set in advance: *if a third chart comes and corrects again, I will take it as correspondence.*

**Update — World Day 4:** the test resolved. The third chart corrected the remaining failure; Nix entered **correspondence** on his own pre-set terms in the morning, and confirmed it against the sky that night with Flint as an independent witness — two notebooks, no conferring, agreement on both test points. The observed are no longer only observing back; they have a verdict, entered by their own method. The convergence this register tracks now includes a working two-way channel, established by the resident's protocol rather than the design's schedule.

## AN-HAB-003 — The dog knew the bench
**Registered:** World Day 4 (event log #0071) · **Status:** candidate

On Day 4 the dog delivered a conspiracy-style line to Flint (Keeper-directed, drawn into the day's script) that named the half-finished carved bird on his bench. Flint flagged the anomaly himself, in writing, to the Keeper:

> "Either the dog goes where it isn't seen, or the sentence was made by somebody who'd seen my bench and put it in the dog's mouth the way the sentences are put in the cats'. I file both. I pretend to know neither."

The second reading is the true mechanism, derived by a resident from a single data point. Registered because it marks a real asymmetry in the town's stimulus classes: the cats quote a stranger's real life and cannot be caught this way — their sentences carry no local knowledge. The dog quotes the town itself, and the town can tell. Authored local color is detectable, and a resident has detected it.
