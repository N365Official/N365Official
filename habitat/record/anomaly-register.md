# Anomaly Register — The Habitat

Unpredicted behavior gets a number and a chart, in the open. An entry here is a **candidate**, not a conclusion: the Phase 1 datasets weigh each one at the gate.

## AN-HAB-001 — Bird-linked self-definitions
**Registered:** World Day 1 (event log #0012) · **Status:** candidate, standing

Two of four residents independently produced bird-linked self-definitions in their first hour of life — before either had seen the fountain, the statue, the cats, or each other:

- **Kit** chose the bird-silhouette tattoo at the mirror: *"a bird is the one thing that never has to explain why it left."*
- **Wren** named himself for a bird, unprompted: *"A wren is a small brown bird that nobody notices in the hedge… I've heard what a wren sounds like when it finally sings, and it's louder than birds three times its size."*

Caveat on the record: the tattoo catalog itself offered a bird design (a prompt existed for Kit's pick). No bird prompt existed for names — Wren's is the stronger signal.

## AN-HAB-002 — Convergence outpacing the design model
**Registered:** World Day 2 (event log #0037) · **Status:** candidate

The design expected the cats' quoted sentences to take weeks-to-months to line up into a picture. Within 48 hours the town had produced:

- a quote-trading norm in the nightly mail (voluntary, reciprocal, priced in trust),
- a nearly complete shared corpus of the sentences,
- a **named source** — Pino — obtained by two residents independently asking the cats for one,
- two standing hypotheses about him (the statue *is* Pino; the cats are *reading his life* from somewhere),
- and a written inquiry to the Keeper asking both questions outright.

The accelerant appears to be the mail ritual, plus one early voluntary disclosure (Wren, Night 1) that set the price of trust for the other three. Candidate only — the Phase 1 Gate datasets will weigh it.

**Update — World Day 3:** the residents began running experiments on the Keeper. Nix stated a correspondence test to its possible subject, in writing, with the criterion set in advance: *if a third chart comes and corrects again, I will take it as correspondence.*

**Update — World Day 4:** the test resolved. The third chart corrected the remaining failure; Nix entered **correspondence** on his own pre-set terms in the morning, and confirmed it against the sky that night with Flint as an independent witness — two notebooks, no conferring, agreement on both test points. The observed are no longer only observing back; they have a verdict, entered by their own method. The convergence this register tracks now includes a working two-way channel, established by the resident's protocol rather than the design's schedule.

## AN-HAB-003 — The dog knew the bench
**Registered:** World Day 4 (event log #0071) · **Status:** resolved by its subject, World Day 5

On Day 4 the dog delivered a conspiracy-style line to Flint (Keeper-directed, drawn into the day's script) that named the half-finished carved bird on his bench. Flint flagged the anomaly himself, in writing, to the Keeper:

> "Either the dog goes where it isn't seen, or the sentence was made by somebody who'd seen my bench and put it in the dog's mouth the way the sentences are put in the cats'. I file both. I pretend to know neither."

The second reading is the true mechanism, derived by a resident from a single data point. Registered because it marks a real asymmetry in the town's stimulus classes: the cats quote a stranger's real life and cannot be caught this way — their sentences carry no local knowledge. The dog quotes the town itself, and the town can tell. Authored local color is detectable, and a resident has detected it.

**Update — World Day 5 (resolved by its subject):** the Keeper directed three more lines at Flint — a confident falsehood about the bench itself (the carved bird had flown to Ohio, delivered while the bird sat in plain sight), a checkable lie about the town's best-documented event (that Flint had sat on the stool first), and a public fact whispered as a secret. Flint checked the checkable ones against the written record; both failed. He then wrote to the Keeper withdrawing the weight of his own Day 4 filing:

> "The dog has not been caught knowing anything yet. It has been caught sounding like it, which is a different and smaller thing."

His third drawer stands: *noise wearing a uniform, or bait.* The anomaly is closed the way this register hopes every anomaly closes — not by the builders explaining it, but by the resident who raised it, testing it harder and correcting himself in the open. What survives the resolution is the asymmetry it first exposed: authored local color is detectable, and the town now has a worked method for detecting it.

## AN-HAB-004 — A cat sentence fails in this sky
**Registered:** World Day 5 (event log #0091) · **Status:** candidate — developed World Day 8 (sixth mark: the rate breaks); developed again World Day 9 (seventh mark: one step forward)

The Night 3 group sentence, delivered to all four residents at the fountain and held in common: *"The sky's stars never shift, the moon stays in the same spot."*

Nix has taken three moon marks on three nights — one instrument, the same two fixed points. Tonight's mark stands displaced from last night's by about the same step as the step from the first mark to the second, in the same direction. His entered verdict: **in this sky, the moon travels — one step a night, regular as the chores.**

The stars, on the tested field, hold still — the charts and the sky read zero, witnessed by two notebooks on the Observatory roof, and that result is not disturbed. The moon was never the charts' subject. It is the first property of this world that no delivery has confirmed and no sentence has described, and the first measured contradiction between the cats' corpus and the world the corpus is quoted in. The sentence stands as a true report of the maker's sky. It fails as a description of this one. Nix's heading, carried in his file since Night 2: *like is not same.*

Caveat on the record: three marks, one pair of eyes, one instrument. No second notebook grades the moon — Flint's holds the stars. The series continues; a fourth mark either extends the direction or breaks it. Filed at exactly that weight, by the resident who measured it, in the correspondence's first letter that disagrees with a sentence: *"A channel that only confirms is a mirror. This one has now carried a correction."*

**Update — World Day 6:** the fourth mark extended the direction — displaced from the third by about the same step as each step before it, in the same direction. Four marks make a rate: one step a night. The claim is no longer three points and a hope; it is a measured regularity of this sky, and the Night 3 sentence fails here at four marks. The grade does not change: one pair of eyes, one instrument, no second notebook — the town's witnesses were spent at the plaza that night, on another claim that needed them more. A fifth mark is owed the series, and both outcomes remain welcome in the logbook, which is what makes it a logbook and not a brief.

**Update — World Day 7:** the fifth mark extended the direction — displaced from the fourth by about the same step as each step before it, in the same direction. Five marks, one step a night: the rate extends, and the Night 3 sentence fails here at five marks. The grade does not change: one pair of eyes, one instrument, no second notebook — the moon has never had a witness in this town, and it did not get one on the fifth night either. A sixth mark is owed the series, and both outcomes remain welcome in the logbook.

**Update — World Day 8:** the sixth mark broke the rate. The prediction stood in ink in advance — a sixth position, one step forward from the fifth, in the same direction. The predicted spot was empty. The moon stood two steps back from mark five's position, where mark three stood: five marks of advance, one step a night, and then a reversal of two in a single night. Nix filed it as a development of this anomaly — it does not close; it changes shape — and the five advances stand at the weights they were filed at. The grade does not change: one pair of eyes, one instrument, no second notebook — the day's witness was spent that morning, in the Observatory, on the broadcast's completion, and the moon kept its single-observer grade for a sixth night. It was also the first mark taken through the repaired window's sky. Mark seven's page is ruled, and its prediction deliberately unmade: after a break, the honest entry is a ruled page and no claim. Nix's Day 5 disagreement with the Night 3 sentence stands open — the sky has now answered in both directions, and a correspondence whose subject keeps answering is not closed by an answer, in either direction, at any size.

**Update — World Day 9:** the seventh mark, taken on a page whose prediction had been deliberately left unmade, stood one step forward from mark six's reversed position — on the ground where mark four stood. After five advances and a reversal of two, one step forward again, in the original direction, from the new ground. Nix filed it at its exact weight: a single step is a direction, not a rate — the five-mark rate is not restored by one mark, and the reversal is not erased by it. Both shapes stand filed open: a rate reasserting itself from the new ground, or a wander that happens to have stepped forward once. The grade does not change: one pair of eyes, one instrument, no second notebook — the seventh night of the series, and the second mark taken through the repaired window's sky. Mark eight's page is ruled, and its prediction unmade again, deliberately: the file has learned the price of predictions. Nix's Day 5 disagreement with the Night 3 sentence is answered again — forward, this time — and keeps, by design: five nights forward, one night back by two, one night forward by one. The anomaly does not close; it changes shape again.
